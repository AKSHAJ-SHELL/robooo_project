# M2 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 5 / F 7.5 / I 6 → overall 6.17

## Novelty (5): 
I checked the code claims against a local clone of `huggingface/lerobot` (last commit Oct 3 2026), and they hold. `so_follower.py:155` asks the user to move the arm "to the middle of its range of motion". `motors_bus.py:874-877` sets `mid = (min_+max_)/2` and returns `(val-mid)*360/max_res`. `config_so_follower.py:45` has `position_p_coefficient: int = 16`. So the perturbation is real. Evaluating policies against it is a new angle for SO-101. The idea itself is not new, though. Implicit Kinematic Policies (Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2022, arXiv 2203.01983) already injected constant joint-encoder offsets of under 2° into imitation-learning tasks. It showed that joint-space policies degrade and that a kinematics-aware policy can compensate [snippet]. The proposal does not cite this. Alvarez et al. (arXiv 2605.13067) already argue for episode-relative state as the fix for base-offset shifts. "Proprioception shift" (arXiv 2506.23944) already frames train/deploy state mismatch as a distribution-shift problem [snippet]. The parts that are actually new are the measured distribution of human recalibration shifts and the Hub audit. The fix comparison is a known method tested in a new setting, which puts it at 5 on the rubric.

## Feasibility (7.5): 
Injecting offsets by editing the calibration file is exact and cheap. The trial grid (180 trials of about 1.5 min each) is realistic, and placement error is continuous. Several things are understated. (1) Relative actions are not "free" for ACT or SmolVLA. In the current code, `use_relative_actions` is wired only into the pi0, pi05, pi0_fast, groot, flux3 and vla_jepa processors. SmolVLA has only `use_delta_joint_actions_aloha`, and ACT has nothing. The team has to do the wiring and test it. (2) Getting per-joint offsets from ArUco tip positions needs an accurate kinematic model of a 3D-printed arm. With 5 poses, the model errors are about as large as the offsets being measured. A simpler route is to read the stored `range_min`/`range_max` and `homing_offset` directly from the 30 calibration files: the offset is the difference in mid, so no vision is needed. (3) SmolVLA fine-tuning on free Kaggle is a real risk, and the proposal already admits it. None of these is fatal, so first data by Dec 20 is plausible.

## Impact (6): 
The audience exists and is large. The LeRobot SO-100 docs say calibration "allows a neural network trained on one SO-100 robot to work on another" [snippet, mintlify mirror of the LeRobot docs]. As far as I can find, nobody has tested that claim, and testing an official claim is something the HF team and Discord would read. SmolVLA was pretrained on 487 community datasets (arXiv 2506.01844). HF's curation of those datasets covered camera remapping and instruction relabeling, not calibration [snippet], so a calibration-heterogeneity result bears directly on how they curate. The reusable outputs would be an `EpisodeRelativeStateProcessorStep`, offset-augmentation PRs and a calibration-shift dataset that HF could merge, which gives it real reuse value. Two limits keep this at workshop level. First, the code suggests the real shifts may be small. Non-wrist joints are swept to hard stops, so their midpoints should repeat well. `wrist_roll` is fixed at 0–4095, so its zero is the eyeballed homing pose. The gripper sees a scale change rather than an offset. Result A ("robust, nothing to fix") is therefore likely, and it would be useful but not exciting. Second, the Hub audit confounds calibration with differences in start pose. First-frame joint values cannot separate the two, so the audit can only bound heterogeneity, not attribute it.

## Lab appeal: 
Lab appeal is weak among the reachable labs. LEMUR (Mehta) works on printable robots and design tools, not on learned manipulation policies. I found no recent LEMUR work on imitation learning with low-cost arms, and Sanfelice is a hybrid-control group. The most natural collaborator is Hugging Face itself: the LeRobot maintainers and the community via Discord and PRs. They could give feedback and review the merged code, but they are not a mentoring lab. Of the student's options, a UCSC SIP placement would add little here beyond a generic advisor.

## Main criticism: 
The study may well find that real recalibration shifts are only a degree or two on most joints, and that ACT with a wrist camera barely notices them. The headline "fix comparison" would then rest on the synthetic 2×p95 stress level. IKP (2022) already showed in principle that joint-space policies suffer under such offsets, and 2605.13067 already argued for the episode-relative fix. The paper's distinctive value then shrinks to a measured shift distribution for one arm type.

## What would raise the score:
1. Cite IKP (2203.01983) and proprioception shift (2506.23944), and position against them explicitly. The new contribution is the shift distribution measured from real humans, plus an evaluation of the official LeRobot claim that calibration transfers policies across units.
2. Do a 1-day pilot before buying the second arm. Run 5 recalibrations, compute the shift in mid directly from the calibration JSONs, and evaluate an existing community ACT checkpoint at that offset. If the shifts are under 1° except on wrist_roll, refocus on wrist_roll and gripper and on the cross-unit case.
3. Make the cross-unit transfer (arm #2, real calibration, real assembly differences) the primary experiment. It is what community dataset pooling actually faces, and it is likely to show a bigger effect.
4. Plan the deliverables as upstream PRs to LeRobot: relative actions wired into ACT and SmolVLA, an episode-relative step, offset augmentation, and a `calibration_check` tool. This turns impact into measurable reuse and gives a concrete contact with HF.
5. Drop the ArUco offset solve and measure the shifts from the stored calibration ranges. Keep ArUco for placement error only.
6. Replace or supplement the lab pitch: approach HF LeRobot maintainers directly, and if a manipulation-learning faculty contact exists in the reachable network, use them instead of LEMUR.

## Closest prior work found:
- Implicit Kinematic Policies, Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2022 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01983 — injects constant joint-encoder offsets under 2° into imitation learning and shows joint-space policies degrade. Same perturbation, different robot, not cited.
- Alvarez et al., "When Absolute State Fails" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13067 — episode-relative state, reported 15× success over absolute state under starting-pose shifts [snippet]. Same fix, mobile/rail base offsets.
- Proprioception shift in imitation learning — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23944 — train/deploy proprioceptive mismatch, fixed by Wasserstein-scaled noise. Close to the R3 augmentation arm [snippet].
- "Do You Need Proprioceptive States in Visuomotor Policies?" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18644 — state-free policies, spatial generalization.
- "How Should VLA Models Use Proprioceptive State?" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03052 — controlled study of state interfaces in VLAs. Relevant to the SmolVLA arm, not cited.
- Benchmarking VLAs on SO-101: Failure and Recovery — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08881 — SO-101 benchmark of ACT, SmolVLA and π0.5. Calibration is not isolated as far as the snippets show.
- SmolVLA — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01844 — 487 community datasets. Curation covered camera remapping and relabeling, not calibration [snippet].
