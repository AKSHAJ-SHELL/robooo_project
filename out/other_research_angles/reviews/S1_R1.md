# S1 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 6.5 / I 6 → overall 6.00

## Novelty (5.5): 
I cloned slam_toolbox `lyrical` and the code claims check out. `publishLocalizedScan(..., range_scan->GetOdometricPose(), ...)` is at L85-90 with an identity covariance. `addExternalScan` calls `Mapper::Process` at L144-156. No optimized poses or sequence numbers are exchanged. The yaml defaults (3.0 m, 0.349 rad, 0.5/0.5 travel thresholds) are as stated. So "N replicas, each optimized on its own" is real. Nobody has published replica-disagreement numbers for this node (I found none in ~15 searches).

But the research question is old in a new setting. Getting replicated or distributed map estimates to agree under limited communication is the core topic of consensus-based map merging (Aragues, Cortes, Sagues, T-RO 2011, which proves convergence to a common global map). The same goes for DDF-SAM, Kimera-Multi (Tian et al. 2023 "lessons learned" explicitly tests delays and unreliable packet delivery), D²SLAM (robust to transient comm loss and delay) and Swarm-SLAM. There is also a 2026 ROS 2 2D map-fusion paper ("Enhancing multi-robot map fusion in ROS 2 via dynamic trust factor weighting", Springer; snippet only). Its snippet reports pose RMSE under 0.2 s delay and 30% packet loss in Gazebo, and the proposal misses it. The "GT-free" metric is just the pairwise disagreement used throughout the consensus literature, and "consistency" is overloaded: in Chang/Chen/Mehta and DDF-SAM it means *statistical estimator consistency* (no overconfidence), not agreement between replicas. The digest-prior fix amounts to "share optimized poses", which is what factor-sharing systems already do. So what is new is the specific measurement on the newly merged default ROS 2 node. That is useful but incremental.

## Feasibility (6.5): 
The core measurement needs no slam_toolbox changes. The replay plan is sound: Stata (2.3 TB, ~2 cm floor-plan GT, ROS 1 bags, confirmed) plus `rosbags` conversion, and N containers on a laptop. 1,440 replays at ~120 laptop-hours is plausible. The power analysis is honest about the missing pilot.

Risks:
- Lyrical is a very new distro running on a Pi 5.
- The Stata topic extraction.
- V2 (soft priors inside the Karto/Ceres graph) is real C++ graph surgery, beyond a HS team without a mentor.
- The live 30-run campaign plus building two robots by late November is tight.

The bigger risk is technical validity. The node uses `create_publisher(topic, 10)`, which is the default RELIABLE, KEEP_LAST(10), VOLATILE QoS. On real DDS, netem "loss" is mostly retransmitted. It turns into latency and bursty late delivery, not loss. True loss only happens when an outage overflows the 10-deep history (~5 s at ~2 scans/s) or a peer drops out of discovery. An app-level i.i.d. or Gilbert–Elliott drop relay therefore models a regime the stock system does not run in. Unless the relay is reframed as outage modelling, the headline dose-response curves could be called artifacts.

## Impact (6): 
If replicas really diverge, a measured envelope for the default ROS 2 multi-robot mapper would be cited and used by Nav2 practitioners. Upstreaming a logger and a fix to slam_toolbox gives real reuse value. It also directly answers the PR's stated future work ("Experiment on lossy WiFi networks", confirmed on the PR page). Most likely it becomes a solid workshop paper or a borderline CASE paper. The main-track story weakens if the fix turns out to be "set TRANSIENT_LOCAL and a deeper history", which is a QoS one-liner. Result A ("they agree") is publishable mainly as a ROSCon-style validation.

## Main criticism: 
The loss model and the fix ignore the DDS transport. The stock node already runs over RELIABLE QoS, which has built-in retransmission, so the obvious baseline for "V1 gap-triggered retransmission" is a QoS change (TRANSIENT_LOCAL durability plus deeper KEEP_LAST or KEEP_ALL), not "no retransmission". Without that baseline and a transport-faithful outage model, a reviewer will see the stock-vs-V1 contrast as a straw man. The novelty then shrinks to a benchmark of one package.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Model loss at the transport level: netem on real DDS/Zenoh traffic, plus outage and roaming episodes (e.g., replayed from the Lajoie/Beltrame IEEE DataPort traces, DOI 10.21227/z1zp-e621). Report how much "loss" actually reaches the application under RELIABLE QoS.
2. Add a QoS-only baseline (TRANSIENT_LOCAL, depth sweep) between stock and V1. Keep V1 only if it beats that baseline (e.g., on bandwidth after long outages).
3. Address double counting in V2. Owner-optimized poses already contain the receiver's own scans via inter-robot loops, so feeding them back as priors is the rumor-propagation problem DDF-SAM addresses. Use CI-style weighting or state why it is safe.
4. Separate the sources of disagreement at 0% loss (processing order, asynchronous loop closure, matcher basins) before adding loss. Then the loss effect is a clean increment.
5. Cite and position against Aragues et al. 2011, Tian et al. 2023 (Kimera-Multi lessons), D²SLAM, and the 2026 ROS 2 trust-weighted map-fusion paper. Rename the metric "inter-replica disagreement" so it is not confused with estimator consistency.
6. Report the PR's own claimed 2/4/8-robot real tests, and contact the PR author for their configuration, so the baseline is truly "the original team's setup".

## Closest prior work found: 
- Aragues, Cortes, Sagues, "Distributed consensus on robot networks for dynamically merging feature-based maps", T-RO 2011 (https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/56830) [snippet]. Proves that replicas of a global map converge under limited connectivity. Same question, done theoretically for feature maps.
- Tian et al., "Resilient and Distributed Multi-Robot Visual SLAM: Datasets, Experiments, and Lessons Learned" (Kimera-Multi), arXiv 2304.04362 [snippet]. Empirical study of decentralized SLAM under delays and unreliable packet delivery.
- Xu et al., D²SLAM, arXiv 2211.01538 [snippet]. Decentralized system with globally consistent trajectories, robust to transient comm loss and delay.
- Lajoie & Beltrame, Swarm-SLAM, arXiv 2301.06230 [snippet]. Sparse decentralized C-SLAM, neighborhood-based eventual consistency.
- Lajoie et al., planetary analogue C-SLAM, arXiv 2601.21063 [snippet]. Intermittent comms with 3 robots. Throughput and latency traces are on IEEE DataPort (DOI 10.21227/z1zp-e621), so the proposal's "check that they are released" is answered: yes.
- "Enhancing multi-robot map fusion in ROS 2 via dynamic trust factor weighting", Springer 2026 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44443-026-01079-6) [snippet only; page blocked]. ROS 2 2D multi-robot mapping under 0.2 s delay and 30% packet loss. Missed by the proposal.
- Chang, Chen, Mehta, T-RO 2022, arXiv 2108.08789 [snippet]. Covariance intersection for cooperative localization under blocked comms. "Consistency" here means estimator consistency.
- Paolo Vanella, Politecnico di Torino MSc thesis 2023, "Implementation of ROS-based Multi-Agent SLAM Centralized and Decentralized Approaches" (https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/29366/) [snippet]. ROS 2 decentralized 2D SLAM on 2 TB3s. A minor precursor.
- "Optimizing ROS 2 Communication for Wireless Robotic Systems", arXiv 2508.11366 [snippet]. DDS retransmission and fragmentation behaviour over Wi-Fi. Directly relevant to the loss model.
- Zhivkov, Schneider, Sklar, TAROS 2017 [snippet]. Nonlinear team-performance drop over 0-75% packet loss.
