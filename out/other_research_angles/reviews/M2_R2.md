# M2 — Reviewer 2 (Methodology rigor and feasibility for this team)
Scores: N 5 / F 7 / I 5.5 → overall 5.83

## Novelty (5): 
The specific perturbation (a routine SO-101 recalibration) has not, as far as I found, been isolated and measured, and an exact software injection is a clean probe. But the scientific question "how sensitive are joint-space visuomotor policies to constant joint-frame offsets, and does a different representation fix it?" has canonical prior work the proposal misses. Implicit Kinematic Policies (Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2022, arXiv 2203.01983) simulated constant joint-encoder offsets under 2° on all six UR5e joints and compared joint and Cartesian action spaces for learning to compensate (search snippet). The episode-relative fix and its invariance argument are already the thesis of 2605.13067 (snippet: "a simple episode-wise relative frame provides the best trade-off", 15x gain under start-pose shifts). The state-free option is 2509.18644. Every fix is prior work and the theory says which one should be invariant, so the open part is narrower than the proposal claims. It comes down to (a) the real shift distribution and (b) whether servo realities break the theory. I would rate this "known method, new setting" in the 5-6 band, at the low end because of the missed IKP paper.

## Feasibility (7): 
I cloned `huggingface/lerobot` (HEAD of 2026-10-03) and checked the mechanism. In DEGREES mode (`use_degrees=True` is the default in both `config_so_follower.py` and `config_so_leader.py`), `_normalize` computes `(val - mid)*360/max_res` with `mid=(range_min+range_max)/2`, so the core claim holds for joints 1-5. Three details the proposal misses: (i) `wrist_roll` is excluded from the range sweep and hard-set to 0..4095, so its zero is wherever the person held it at the "middle" step (`set_half_turn_homings`), not a sweep midpoint. That makes it the most person-dependent joint, and the injector needs a separate path (homing_offset) for it. (ii) Most SO-101 joints sweep into mechanical hard stops, so sweep midpoints may repeat to within about a degree. That points toward Result A, with the real p95 possibly smaller than servo backlash and sag at P=16. (iii) `write_calibration` writes the shifted range into the servo `Min/Max_Position_Limit` registers, so injecting δ by shifting ranges also clips δ of travel at one end, which could hit joint limits during eval. The software is cheap, the robot is cheap and 180 trials (about 5 h plus resets) is realistic, so the build is clearly finishable by Feb. My concerns are about the methods. (a) `RelativeActionsProcessorStep` is wired only into pi0/pi05/pi0_fast/groot/flux3/vla_jepa processors, not ACT or SmolVLA, so R1 is not "free". It needs processor wiring plus `compute_relative_action_stats` (about a day, but say so). (b) Each variant is trained once, so differences between variants are confounded with training-seed variance, which is large for ACT with 60 demos. (c) Using one random direction vector per offset level confounds magnitude with which joint moved: 3° on shoulder_lift at about 30 cm reach is roughly 15 mm, while 3° on wrist_roll is nearly nothing. (d) The SD ≈ 12 mm figure is assumed, and placement error is undefined on failed grasps (bimodal or censored data), so the d≈1.1 power calculation is not credible as written. (e) SmolVLA: the official estimate is about 4 h for 20k steps on one A100 (snippet). A T4 has no bf16 and is several times slower, so 6-10 h on Kaggle is likely an underestimate and needs checkpoint-resume across 12 h sessions. Real-time inference on a laptop without a GPU is also unaddressed. ACT itself is fine on a Kaggle T4 at a reduced batch size.

## Impact (5.5): 
A measured answer to "do I have to retrain after recalibrating / can I pool community data?" has real practical value for the very large SO-100/101 user base, and the measured shift distribution would get cited by LeRobot practitioners. The most likely outcome, though, is small real shifts plus a known invariance result. That is a useful workshop paper or technical note, not a CASE main-track result. The Hub audit is weak as designed. First-frame joint distributions are confounded by task start poses, and older SO-100 datasets were recorded under the pre-refactor RANGE_M100_100 convention (the config comment says `use_degrees` exists "for backward compatibility with previous policies/dataset"). Convention mismatch will swamp calibration offsets in that audit.

## Lab appeal: 
Weak. As the proposal admits, none of the reachable labs does manipulation learning. LEMUR's cheap printable robots connect only thematically, and the Sanfelice link is a stretch. A team member's SIP placement might give some mentoring, but expect no technical co-ownership.

## Main criticism: 
The study's interesting branch (Result B) depends on real recalibration shifts being large. The code suggests they are small for hard-stopped joints and possibly large only for wrist_roll, and the dose-response is then built on one direction per level, one training seed per variant and an assumed SD. The proposal could easily end up measuring noise from backlash and seeds and then reading it as "fix X wins".

## What would raise the score:
1. Measure same-arm shifts directly from the calibration JSONs before building anything. With a single-turn absolute encoder, the absolute midpoint is about `(range_min+range_max)/2 + homing_offset` in raw ticks, so the 5 people × 3 repeats study needs no vision solve. Keep the ArUco method only for cross-arm differences, where horn mounting differs. Do this in week 1 as a go/no-go: if p95 is under about 1° on joints 1-5, refocus on wrist_roll and cross-unit transfer.
2. Analyze per joint: inject single-joint offsets (shoulder_pan, shoulder_lift, elbow, wrist_flex, wrist_roll) at 2-3 magnitudes rather than one random vector per level, or use at least 4 direction vectors per level as a random effect.
3. Train at least 3 seeds for R0 and the leading fix (2 for the others) and treat seed as a random effect in the mixed model.
4. Run a 20-trial pilot at δ=0 to estimate the real placement-error SD and failure rate, then redo the power analysis. Pre-specify how failed grasps are handled (a hurdle model: success logistic + error-given-success).
5. Fix the injector to shift homing_offset (frame) rather than ranges (which also moves the servo position limits), or explicitly log limit hits.
6. Cite and position against Implicit Kinematic Policies (2203.01983) and 2605.13067. Frame the contribution as "real-world servo arm check of the invariance claim plus measured shift distribution".
7. Drop SmolVLA from the core plan, or budget it at about 25-40 T4-hours with resume and confirm it runs on the eval laptop. Replace the Hub audit with a convention-aware audit, or cut it.

## Closest prior work found:
- Implicit Kinematic Policies, Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2022 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01983 — simulated constant joint-encoder offsets (<2°) on UR5e and studied which action space compensates. Directly overlaps the dose-response idea and is missing from the proposal.
- Alvarez et al., "When Absolute State Fails" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13067 — episode-relative frame as the robust proprioceptive encoding (verified to exist via snippet). This is the proposed fix R2 and its rationale.
- Zhao et al., "Do You Need Proprioceptive States in Visuomotor Policies?" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18644 — state-free policies, the R4 fix. Its perturbation is spatial generalization, not frame shift.
- SO-101 VLA benchmark — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08881 (via emergentmind snippet) — evaluates ACT, SmolVLA, π0.5 and Wall-X on SO-101 under "embodiment uncertainty". It does not isolate calibration.
- "When would Vision-Proprioception Policies Fail in Robotic Manipulation?" ICLR 2026 — https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011742 — related analysis of over-reliance on proprioception (snippet only, not read).
- LeRobot `relative_action_processor.py` + `compute_relative_action_stats` — existing code for R1, but wired only into pi0-family/groot/flux3/vla_jepa processors, not ACT/SmolVLA.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- "`RelativeActionsProcessorStep` (already in LeRobot) — Fix R1 for free": the step exists, but ACT and SmolVLA processors do not use it (grep of `src/lerobot/policies/act` and `smolvla` finds no reference). The team must wire it in and compute relative-action stats.
- "The joint's zero angle is therefore the midpoint of however far that person swept the joint": true for joints 1-5, but false for `wrist_roll`, whose range is hard-coded to 0..4095 and whose zero is set by the pose held at the homing step.
- Issue #1360 is cited as evidence that "users must recalibrate after each power disconnect". The issue (closed not planned, June 2025, no maintainer reply visible) is one user's report. In current code, `calibrate()` offers to reuse the saved file and `write_calibration` rewrites homing offset and limits on every connect, so routine recalibration after a power cycle is not required by design. The frequency claim is not established.
- Injector "edits MotorCalibration ranges by ±δ": this also shifts the servo hardware position limits (`Min/Max_Position_Limit` written in `feetech.py write_calibration`), which is a confound the proposal does not mention.
- SmolVLA "6-10 h on Kaggle": unsupported. The only number found is about 4 h for 20k steps on an A100, and a T4 is much slower. ACT "2-4 h per variant" on a T4 is plausible only with reduced batch or steps (snippet: T4 fits about batch 15 vs the recommended 16+).
