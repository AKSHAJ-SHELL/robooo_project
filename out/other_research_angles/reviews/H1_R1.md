## Novelty (5.5):
The problem framing is real and not solved in the *open* skin literature. AnySkin and eFlesh both name magnetic/ferromagnetic interference as a limitation, and the eFlesh repo has no compensation path. I cloned it: there is no mention of shielding, ferrous objects or compensation in the code or READMEs. The Dec 2025 actuator paper (arXiv 2512.00907) does recommend ≥10 mm clearance from high-permeability objects [snippet]. But the proposed *tools* are not new:
- Halbach/sinusoidal magnetization is already the core of the Yan et al. soft magnetic skins (Sci. Robotics 2021; NMI 2024). Their one-sided field is used with the strong side toward the Hall sensor [snippets].
- Soft-magnetic shielding of magnetic taxels is already published: OSMO uses 0.15 mm MuMetal plus dual-magnetometer differencing (arXiv 2512.08920) [snippet], and MagTac-class fingertips use a "soft magnetic shielding film" [snippet].
- The method of images for a high-permeability plate is textbook.
- XELA, a commercial magnetic-skin maker, announced in Apr–Jun 2026 a uSkin compensation feature that "removes complex interference from nearby magnets and ferromagnetic materials" so the skins can handle iron [press snippets]. The proposal does not cite it, and it weakens the claim that the problem is "open". The method is proprietary, so an open fix still has value.

More seriously, the "non-obvious insight" does not hold up when checked. The proposal claims eFlesh's alternating poles "reduce the wrong quantity". Yet eFlesh's own 100× stray-field reduction was measured on a plane 15 mm *above* the array [snippet], which is the object side. I ran a quick magpylib check (my own toy model: ideal μ→∞ steel plate as a mirror image, and an 8 mm steel ball as an induced dipole; 3 mm N52 cubes 3 mm below the top surface; 5 sensor points). The steel-induced offset as a fraction of a 1 mm press signal came out as follows:
- **Plate:** alternating 0.010–0.048, 1-D Halbach (strong side down) 0.051–0.149, aligned 0.35–3.2.
- **Ball:** alternating ≈0.000–0.012, Halbach ≈0.000–0.016, aligned 0.03–0.29.

So in idealised physics, eFlesh's alternating layout already suppresses the object-side near field as well as or better than a discrete Halbach row. Halbach mainly buys a larger absolute signal. This is a toy and ignores saturation, real lattice deformation and eFlesh's actual disc layout. Still, it means the headline hypothesis (Halbach beats alternating on steel) is more likely to be refuted by the team's own week-1 simulation than confirmed. The ferrite pre-mirror is the more original element, but a 0.1–0.2 mm NFC ferrite sheet (low μr, low B_sat) next to N52 magnets will likely saturate and be a weak mirror. Steel behind it would still add a much stronger image. A competent reviewer would read this as "known magnet tricks applied to a known sensor".

## Feasibility (6.5):
The bench study is genuinely cheap and well specified: a printer-stage indenter, PLA-trained/steel-tested regressor, a paired design and a sensible power calculation. eFlesh's MLP characterization code (`characterization/model.py`), slip data (`slip_detection/data/seq_*.pt`, 138 files) and LSTM code exist as claimed. The risks come from scope:
- **KiCad re-draw and two PCB spins:** not needed for the research question. The released ReSkin Gerbers work, and the "differential" board B is a separate project.
- **SO-101 build and LeRobot integration:** these sit on top of four skin variants.
- **Magnet change:** moving from eFlesh's 3/16"×1/16" or 3/8"×1/8" discs (per its README) to 3 mm cubes changes the magnet moment and spacing. That confounds layout with magnet size unless the aligned and alternating controls are rebuilt with the same cubes.
- **Hand-placing magnet orientations:** placing 4 orientations of 3 mm cubes into printed pouches during a print pause is fiddly, and N52 cubes in a Halbach pattern actively flip and jump.

The bench work alone is feasible by Dec 20. The full plan is not, unless the PCB and robot arms are cut.

## Impact (5.5):
If steel causes large errors for the open-skin community, a validated design rule plus files would be cited by ReSkin, AnySkin, eFlesh and OSMO users. But the likely result, going by the physics above, is that "eFlesh's best (alternating) layout already makes steel contact a few-percent effect; aligned layouts are bad". That is useful, but it is a narrow workshop or short-paper result. The downstream grasp and slip tasks are reasonable, but they will only show a gain if the pilot finds a large steel error on the *alternating* skin. The plan pilots on the aligned "original", which is not eFlesh's recommended configuration and would overstate the problem (brief lesson 4). The fallback ("magnetic skin as ferrous pre-contact or material sensor") overlaps existing magnetic pretouch work (e.g. GMR pretouch graspers, TU Graz) and is not novel on its own.

## Main criticism:
The central mechanism claim is unsupported and probably wrong. Alternating polarity does reduce the field the skin projects toward the object (eFlesh measured it on that side). In an idealised image or induced-dipole model, it already suppresses steel-contact perturbation about as well as a Halbach row. The project is therefore built around a fix whose advantage over the proper baseline is likely near zero, and around a pilot run on a straw-man (aligned) baseline.

## What would raise the score:
1. Do the magpylib study first, and make the *alternating* eFlesh skin the baseline in the pilot. Only then decide whether the steel problem exists at a magnitude worth fixing: report steel offset ÷ press signal, and steel-induced ΔRMSE on alternating.
2. Reframe it as a characterisation paper: which object geometries (thin sheet, plate, small screw or ball, steel can) and which layouts produce the errors, validated against the image/induced-dipole model, with saturation of thin sheet steel measured. That characterisation is the novel contribution, and it can go either way.
3. Cite and position against XELA's ferromagnetic compensation, OSMO's MuMetal and differencing, Yan et al.'s Halbach films and MagTac shielding. If keeping a fix, compare against a cheap software baseline as well: a back-side reference magnetometer, or learned compensation from the pre-contact approach signal.
4. Drop the KiCad re-draw and the second PCB spin; order the released Gerbers. Keep the SO-101 task to one task (force-limited grasp on a steel can vs. a PLA can).
5. Hold magnet size constant across all layouts (rebuild aligned and alternating with the same cubes), or use eFlesh discs in alternating vs. tilted-disc pseudo-Halbach.
6. For the ferrite arm, use a thicker or higher-B_sat soft layer (e.g. 0.1 mm MuMetal or iron-powder TPU) and measure the stiffness change.

## Closest prior work found:
- eFlesh, arXiv 2506.09994 / https://github.com/notvenky/eFlesh: alternating polarity cuts stray field 100×, measured 15 mm above the array, i.e. the object side. This directly overlaps the proposed fix.
- AnySkin, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08276: names interference from (ferro)magnetic objects as the limitation; no fix.
- ReSkin, https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00071: the original sensor and board; the same susceptibility.
- OSMO glove, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08920: MuMetal shielding plus dual-magnetometer differencing (57% RMS noise reduction). This is prior use of soft-magnetic shielding on magnetic taxels.
- Yan et al., Sci. Robotics 2021, "Soft magnetic skin for super-resolution tactile sensing with force self-decoupling" (https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/soft-magnetic-skin-for-super-resolution-tactile-sensing-with-force-self-decoupling): a Halbach/sinusoidal film with the strong side toward the Hall sensor, so a one-sided skin already exists.
- Yan et al., Nat. Mach. Intell. 2024 (https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/lirmm-04825067): orthogonal Halbach films for 3-axis self-decoupling.
- Magnetic tactile-driven soft actuator, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00907: ferrite gives up to 0.4 G at contact (<0.1 G at 3.5 mm); recommends ≥10 mm clearance from steel or iron. Evidence the issue is acknowledged.
- XELA uSkin magnetic interference compensation (2026 press, e.g. https://www.therobotreport.com/xela-robotics-adds-2-enhancements-uskin-sensor-family-ahead-robotics-summit/): a commercial fix for ferromagnetic materials and nearby magnets; method proprietary. Missing from the proposal.
- Numerical Halbach flexible magnetic sensor, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12693847/: simulation; mentions reduced stray interference on the weak side.
- Magnetic and capacitive pretouch for grasping, TU Graz (https://graz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-pretouch-sensing-system-for-a-robot-grasper-using-magnetic-and-): ferrous-object pre-contact detection with a magnet and GMR sensors. Overlaps the fallback framing.
