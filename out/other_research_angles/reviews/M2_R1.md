# M2 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 4.5 / F 7.5 / I 5.0 → overall 5.67

## Novelty (4.5):
The proposal misses the closest prior work. **VLA-REPLICA** (arXiv 2605.20774, UT Dallas IRVL) already names the exact problem on the same arm. From search snippets: "non-standardized manufacturing introduces the problem of differing initial offsets of servo motors across differently constructed SO-101 arms, which constrains each arm to its own specific action space." It proposes a fix (a universal "L-shape" zero pose plus a diagonal calibration matrix built from offset/min/max). It then tests transfer of ACT, SmolVLA and π0.5 policies to an independently built and calibrated second SO-101, and reports success rates "comparable" to the original (ACT 0.28 vs 0.32 on in-distribution tasks; snippet). That is the proposal's Result A and its cross-unit check, already published. Constant joint-encoder offsets as a perturbation on imitation policies also predate this: **Implicit Kinematic Policies** (Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2022, arXiv 2203.01983) injected constant offsets under 2° on every UR5e joint and compared joint-space with Cartesian-space policies. The episode-relative fix comes from 2605.13067. I confirmed it is ACT-based on a dual-rail system (snippets; I could not confirm the "ICRA 2026 WS" venue). Proprioception over-reliance and its fixes are also crowded: 2509.18644 (state-free), "When would Vision-Proprioception Policies Fail" (ICLR 2026, GAP gradient damping), "Adapt Your Body" (2506.23944, state-noise matching; withdrawn), GeoProp, and AxisGuide (2606.06761). Two things remain new: (a) a measured human-to-human distribution of LeRobot recalibration shifts plus a Hub audit, and (b) a head-to-head of existing fixes under exact offset injection. That is a careful benchmark of known methods in a new setting. It is not a new insight.

## Feasibility (7.5):
The plan is cheap and the software injection is exact. I checked the code claims against current lerobot `main` (Oct 3, 2026). They hold: the "middle of its range of motion" prompt in `so_follower.py`, `mid = (min_+max_)/2` in `motors_bus.py` DEGREES mode, `use_degrees=True` by default, `position_p_coefficient = 16`, and `processor/relative_action_processor.py` exists. ACT on Kaggle T4s and 180 trials of about 1.5 min each are realistic, and the continuous mm metric with ArUco is sensible. The risks: SmolVLA fine-tuning on free compute; Step 1 needing pose-solving from tip positions, which is fiddly to do accurately with a 5-DOF arm and an overhead camera; and a likely null result (see Main criticism). One protocol gap: episode-relative encoding is only invariant if each episode starts from the same *physical* pose. If the eval reset pose is commanded in the shifted (reported) frame, the start is physically displaced by δ, so the "exact invariance" is not exact. The reset protocol has to be specified.

## Impact (5.0):
The result is useful to LeRobot practitioners, especially as a "can I recalibrate or pool datasets?" answer. Because VLA-REPLICA already reports that calibrated cross-unit transfer works, the headline is weakened. The most useful new output would be the Hub-wide audit of first-frame joint distributions, which is a data-quality finding for SmolVLA-style pretraining. Without a new method, the realistic ceiling is an ICRA workshop paper. CASE main track is unlikely.

## Lab appeal:
None of the listed labs does manipulation learning. LEMUR (Mehta) values cheap printable hardware, but this project is a policy-robustness benchmark, so their gain is indirect. Expect light mentorship at most. The proposal says so honestly.

## Main criticism:
The core claim to novelty (that calibration offsets on SO-101 have not been studied and cross-unit transfer is folk knowledge) is false. VLA-REPLICA already identified per-arm servo offsets as the problem, proposed a calibration standardization, and measured cross-unit transfer of ACT and SmolVLA as comparable. The code also suggests real shifts may be tiny. Users sweep to hard stops, so the DEGREES-mode midpoint is roughly physically repeatable and moves only by half of any sweep shortfall. The exception is `wrist_roll`, whose range is fixed at 0–4095, so its zero is wherever the user held it during homing. The proposal does not notice that asymmetry. A small p95 makes Result A (robust, no effect) the likely outcome.

## What would raise the score:
1. Cite VLA-REPLICA and IKP, and position the work against them. For example, VLA-REPLICA tested only *standardized* calibration; this study would test the *default* LeRobot procedure, plus repeated recalibration by different people on the same arm.
2. Run a one-day pilot first. Five calibrations by two people, then compare the JSON `range_min`/`range_max` and the implied midpoints. If p95 is under 1° on body joints, pivot the study to `wrist_roll` and the gripper (RANGE_0_100 scaling), where the shifts are plausibly large.
3. Add the cheapest existing baseline: state-noise augmentation in the style of 2506.23944, alongside R3. Also add a VLA-REPLICA-style L-pose re-zeroing as a "fix at calibration time" arm. That arm is probably the practical winner and needs to be beaten or confirmed.
4. Specify the eval reset protocol (physical fixture-defined start pose vs commanded pose) so the invariance claim is actually tested.
5. Make the Hub audit a first-class contribution: quantify the spread of rest-pose and first-frame states across SO-100/101 datasets, and test whether filtering or re-zeroing changes a small SmolVLA fine-tune.

## Closest prior work found:
- VLA-REPLICA, arXiv 2605.20774 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20774, https://irvlutd.github.io/VLAReplica/): identifies differing SO-101 servo offsets across arms, proposes a universal L-pose zero plus a calibration matrix, and shows ACT/SmolVLA/π0.5 transfer to a second arm at comparable success. Direct overlap.
- Implicit Kinematic Policies, Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2022, arXiv 2203.01983 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01983): constant joint-encoder offsets under 2° on a UR5e as a perturbation to imitation policies; compares joint and Cartesian action spaces.
- When Absolute State Fails, arXiv 2605.13067 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13067): episode-wise relative state/actions with ACT on a dual-rail system; this is the R2 fix.
- Do You Need Proprioceptive States in Visuomotor Policies?, arXiv 2509.18644 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18644): state-free policies generalize better (fix R4).
- When would Vision-Proprioception Policies Fail in Robotic Manipulation?, ICLR 2026, arXiv 2602.12032 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12032): proprioception dominates optimization; GAP gradient fix. Missing from the proposal.
- Adapt Your Body: Mitigating Proprioception Shifts in IL, arXiv 2506.23944 (withdrawn) (https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.23944): state-noise augmentation for train/deploy proprioception shift; closest to R3.
- GeoProp, arXiv 2607.07101 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07101): grounds state in vision; reports robustness to camera-robot calibration drift (a different calibration).
- AxisGuide, arXiv 2606.06761 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06761): renders action-frame axes into RGB for robustness to distribution shift. Missing from the proposal.
- Benchmarking VLAs on SO-101: Failure and Recovery, arXiv 2606.08881 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08881): SO-101 VLA benchmark with failure taxonomy. I could not confirm the "imperfect calibration" wording from snippets.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- Implied novelty that cross-unit and calibration transfer on SO-101 is unmeasured, and the "first measured" framing in (a): VLA-REPLICA already measured cross-unit transfer and addressed per-arm offsets. The human-repeat shift distribution may still be new.
- Issue #1360 used as evidence that "recalibration happens often": the issue (title and "closed as not planned" verified) is about the calibration-*check* comparing homing offsets after a power cycle, which forced recalibration through a verification bug. It is not evidence that the physical frames drift. The current code also offers to reuse the saved calibration file.
- "The joint's zero angle is the midpoint of however far that person swept the joint": correct for body joints, but with sweeps to the hard stops this is close to repeatable. For `wrist_roll` the zero is instead the homing pose (range fixed at 0–4095), which the proposal omits.
- "Episode-relative encoding is mathematically invariant": true only if episodes start from the same physical pose. This is not stated.
- 2605.13067 described as for "mobile carriages and rails": consistent with snippets (dual-rail). The "Alvarez et al., ICRA 2026 WS" attribution is unverified; I could not confirm the venue.
- The SmolVLA heterogeneity claim is supported in general terms (noisy demos, inconsistent camera naming), but I found no SmolVLA statement about calibration specifically.
- Code claims (prompt text, `mid` formula, P=16, relative-action processor): verified, none wrong.
