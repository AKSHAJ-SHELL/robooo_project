# M3 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 6 / I 5.5 → overall 5.67

## Novelty (5.5): 
I could not find a controlled study that varies leader-arm friction or quantization while keeping the kinematics the same and then measures ACT precision downstream. The papers I found compare across modalities. "How to Train Your Robots?" (2503.07017) compares kinesthetic, VR and SpaceMouse demonstrations. A newer paper, "How Should We Teach Robots?" (2605.28033), compares kinesthetic, joystick and gesture teaching. U-Arm (2509.02437) compares leader devices only on collection efficiency against a JoyCon. So the 2×2 cell design is new. The closest conceptual neighbour is "Mind the Gap" (2602.08776). It argues that on low-cost leader-follower hardware the operator acts as a closed-loop compensator, and that the leader command, not the follower's execution, is the right label. That is the same "the leader is part of the data-generating process" insight, from a different angle. The proposal does not cite it. The "friction as damping or a virtual fixture" twist is the only result a reader could not guess beforehand, and it is a nice one. Still, "better teleop hardware gives better data" is the default expectation, so this sits at "new result in a narrow setting", not "clearly new".

## Feasibility (6): 
The hardware is within the mechanical student's skills. Six AS5600 sensors plus an RP2040 is a well-trodden build, and a LeRobot `Teleoperator` subclass is small. Three issues lower the score:
- **The low-friction cell may be unusable.** A detent-free, low-friction leader with SO-101 kinematics will sag under gravity the moment the operator lets go. GELLO-style leaders add springs or counterweights for this reason. The study needs gravity compensation, or that cell measures operator fatigue rather than label fidelity.
- **The SO-101 follower may hide the effect.** The follower has STS3215 gear backlash (vendor spec ≤0.5°) and its own tracking error. ACT's chunking and temporal ensembling smooth labels. Leader-side differences could disappear before they reach placement error. With 25 trials per policy and one policy per condition (2 seeds only for the baseline), the policy-level comparison is underpowered, and training noise between seeds is confounded with condition.
- **The core evidence is thin.** The headline rests on one closed issue with no comments. On the timeline, 240 counterbalanced demos plus 100 evaluation trials between Nov 30 and Jan 15 is tight but possible.

## Impact (5.5): 
**Lab appeal.** For the LeRobot community the reusable artifact is real. Six AS5600 sensors (~$25) plus a carrier board is cheaper and lighter than six geared STS3215 servos, and "plug-in replacement leader that emits the same joint dict as `so_leader`" is something hobbyists do build. That said, the community has already shown it will adopt alternative leaders when they are good enough (U-Arm, Hugging Face's own homunculus exoskeleton with Hall-effect sensing). So the PCB alone is not a contribution. It is the measured answer that matters.
**Result A versus Result B.**
- If Result A holds ("detents hurt precision"), it is useful but expected. It reads as a hardware tip. Hugging Face closed the issue as not planned, which suggests maintainers do not see it as a priority.
- If Result B holds ("friction helps"), it is the more citable outcome. But it is hard to show convincingly with 2 operators who are also the authors and not blinded to condition.

Imitation-learning researchers would cite this as a data-quality footnote. A full ACT result on n = 2 operators is workshop-level: ICRA 2027 workshop fits, and CASE or IROS main track does not. Expected readership is narrow but real (SO-101 owners).

## Lab appeal: 
SJSU Winncy Du is a plausible fit for the sensor and board side. She wrote a CRC book on magnetic sensor technologies and has GMR sensing work, so a magnetic-encoder carrier with a careful characterization (resolution, noise, detent histogram, breakaway torque) speaks her language. The imitation-learning half will not interest her much. UCLA LEMUR is a weak fit, since a printable leader is only loosely "cheap printable electromechanical design". Neither lab would bring IL expertise, and the proposal admits this. So expect at most a mentoring conversation, not co-authorship.

## Main criticism: 
The whole contribution depends on a mechanism, 29 detents per revolution in the leader, that rests on one uncommented issue report. The proposal also misdescribes the stock sensor. The STS3215's 12-bit magnetic encoder reads the output shaft, so stock leader readings are not "through gears with backlash", and the fallback "friction × resolution" framing loses most of its contrast. Add a follower that may mask leader-side differences, and the most likely outcome is a null result that is hard to interpret.

## What would raise the score:
1. Run the detent histogram on 2–3 SO-101 leaders, including a borrowed or bought one, before committing. Report how prevalent the detents are, which joints they affect and whether gear-ratio variant matters. If the detents are real and common, the motivation is strong. If not, pivot early.
2. Cite and position against "Mind the Gap" (2602.08776) and U-Arm (2509.02437). Consider making the outcome "how leader mechanics shape the intent–execution mismatch", which ties directly into that active thread.
3. Add gravity compensation (springs or counterweights, as in GELLO) to the encoder leader. Make friction the only manipulated variable via the collar, and keep the software-quantization cell. That gives a clean, cheap quantization contrast without new hardware.
4. Measure the effect at the demonstration level with more operators: recruit 4–6 classmates for a short tracing task, blinded to condition, with continuous metrics such as tremor-band power, jerk and target error. Make that the primary result, with ACT as secondary. Train ≥2 seeds per condition.
5. Fix the follower confound. Report the follower's repeatability floor first, so a null policy result is interpretable.
6. Release the board as a documented drop-in (BOM under $40, a LeRobot PR for the teleoperator) to maximize community reuse.

## Closest prior work found:
- LeRobot issue #2074, "SO-101 leader precision": https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2074. Confirmed via fetch: closed as not planned, quote matches, no visible comments. This is the only evidence for the detents.
- "Mind the Gap: Learning Implicit Impedance in Visuomotor Policies via Intent-Execution Mismatch" (2602.08776): https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08776 (snippet only). Covers how leader–follower discrepancy and operator compensation on low-cost hardware shape BC labels, which is the closest conceptual overlap.
- U-Arm (2509.02437): https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02437 and https://github.com/MINT-SJTU/LeRobot-Anything-U-Arm. A $50 3D-printed leader compatible with LeRobot, with a newer STS3215 version. It compares devices on efficiency, not fidelity.
- "How to Train Your Robots?" (2503.07017): https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07017 (snippet only). Finds kinesthetic teaching gives the cleanest data. Cross-modality.
- "How Should We Teach Robots? A Comparison of Kinesthetic, Joystick, and Gesture-Based Teaching" (2605.28033): https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28033 (snippet only). Cross-modality. Missing from the proposal.
- GELLO (https://github.com/wuphilipp/gello_software): a passive servo-based leader. Its gravity-compensation design is relevant to the low-friction cell.
- LeRobot HopeJR homunculus arm/glove (Hugging Face): a non-servo teleoperator with Hall-effect sensing already exists in LeRobot (snippet from docs mirror). Shows the `Teleoperator` reuse path is real.

- "the stock STS3215 leader reads 4096 counts per revolution through gears with backlash": vendor specs describe the 12-bit magnetic encoder as absolute output-shaft position sensing. Backlash affects how the leader feels, not what it reads. The fallback framing overstates the resolution contrast.
- "LeRobot issue #2074 ... closed as not planned": confirmed, and the quote is accurate. Not wrong, but it is a single report with no comments or maintainer confirmation.
