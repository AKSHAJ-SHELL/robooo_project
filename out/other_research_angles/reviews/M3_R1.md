# M3 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 6.5 / I 5 → overall 5.67

## Novelty (5.5): 
I found no paper that holds leader kinematics fixed and varies only the leader's mechanical fidelity (detents, friction, resolution), then measures the effect on an ACT policy. Within-modality ablations of this kind look unstudied. The papers closest to it compare *across* modalities: "How to Train Your Robots?" (2503.07017) compares kinesthetic, VR and SpaceMouse teaching, and "Static Is Not Enough" (2601.13042, an HRI'26 late-breaking report) compares VR with SpaceMouse. Both are summarized correctly in the proposal (checked via search snippets). Each part of the hypothesis has precedent, though. ALOHA 2 (2405.02292) deliberately cut leader backdrive friction: it switched to XC430 motors with metal, lower-ratio gears and a rail gripper needing ~10x less force, though it justified this by ergonomics and ran no policy ablation. The SO-101 leader itself already lowered gear ratios (1/147, 1/191) to make the arm easier to backdrive. FACTR (2502.17432) adds active friction and gravity compensation to a low-cost leader and reports better teleop completion and better policies (bundled with force feedback). The "damping suppresses tremor" twist is old in teleoperation and haptics: passive viscous damping in master devices for tremor, tremor filtering in surgical and welding master–slave systems, and CMU's tremor suppression during dextrous teleop. It is not a new insight, only new to imitation learning. The paper would sit at "known factor, first controlled IL measurement, low-cost setting": 5-6.

## Feasibility (6.5): 
The hardware is simple (AS5600 + RP2040 + mux, with a perfboard fallback), and the LeRobot `Teleoperator` subclass is genuinely small. But the plan builds two leaders, a friction collar, a PCB, 240 demos and 3-4 ACT trainings, and then runs ~100 policy trials, all while the conditions depend on matching breakaway torque with a luggage scale. That is a lot for part-time students, and the Dec 20 first-data date leaves only ~3 weeks after encoder-leader assembly. The statistics are weaker than they look. The "operators" are the two authors, who are not blind to the hypothesis. The 80 demos per condition come from 2 people, so the d≈0.45 power claim treats correlated demos as independent (pseudoreplication). The policy-level comparison (25 trials per arm, detects only d≈0.8) is where ACT's chunking and temporal ensembling are most likely to wash out any label effect. Expect a null at the policy level that is hard to interpret. The premise is also shaky. Issue #2074 does exist, says "roughly 29 distinct positions per revolution", and was closed as not planned (verified via github.com fetch). But it is a single report, and 29 detents per *output* revolution is physically odd for a 1:147-1:345 gear train, where motor cogging would show up as hundreds of detents. The Week-1 histogram test is essential and may well fail.

## Impact (5): 
If detents are real and matter, SO-101 users get a practical fix and a released board. That is useful but narrow, and the community can already pick cheaper leaders such as U-ARM ($50 BOM, LeRobot-compatible) or GELLO-style builds. A "fidelity doesn't matter, ACT smooths it out" result would be modestly useful guidance. The 2x2 friction-versus-quantization separation is the intellectually interesting part, but with n=2 operators and one task it will not generalize enough to be cited much. This is workshop-level work (ICRA 2027 low-cost-learning workshop), as the proposal itself says.

## Main criticism: 
The study rests on an unconfirmed, physically odd single issue report. It manipulates a variable that ALOHA 2, SO-101 and FACTR have already "fixed" by design without measuring, so the result will be judged mainly on the policy-level effect. With 2 self-operators, 25 trials per arm and ACT's built-in smoothing, the study is underpowered to detect that effect.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Run the detent histogram on a real SO-101 leader *before* committing, and report its mechanism: detent spacing in degrees, whether it varies with gear ratio, powered versus unpowered. If there are no detents, reframe around friction alone.
2. Add a nearly free, PCB-less fidelity manipulation, which strengthens the science and cuts risk. Options: (a) SO-100-style 1/345 leader motors versus SO-101 1/147 motors (same kinematics, different friction); (b) leader servos with the motor/pinion removed, if the STS3215 encoder magnet sits on the output gear as I believe, giving a detent-free leader with stock electronics. Then the PCB becomes an optional artifact rather than the critical path.
3. Run the quantization arm entirely in software. Re-quantize or low-pass the *same* recorded demos (29 levels, varying amounts of added tremor-band noise) and retrain ACT. This gives a dose-response curve with zero extra demos and makes the policy-sensitivity result far stronger than three hardware cells.
4. Recruit 4-6 naive operators, even just for the demo-quality metrics, and analyze with mixed-effects models (operator as a random effect).
5. Use a task where label resolution plausibly matters (sub-5 mm insertion) and report placement error as a continuous metric with more trials (≥40 per arm). Also test Diffusion Policy or ACT without temporal ensembling, to check whether chunking hides the effect.
6. Cite and position against ALOHA 2's friction reduction, FACTR's friction compensation, U-ARM, and the master-device tremor/damping literature.

## Closest prior work found:
- ALOHA 2 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02292): cut leader gripper backdrive friction (XC430, metal gears, rail gripper, ~10x less force) for ergonomics; no policy ablation. The closest "fidelity" intervention.
- FACTR (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17432): low-cost leader with friction/gravity compensation and force feedback; better teleop completion and policies, but the factors are confounded.
- How to Train Your Robots? (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07017): demo modality (kinesthetic/VR/SpaceMouse) versus downstream learning; across modalities, not leader mechanics.
- Static Is Not Enough (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13042): VR versus SpaceMouse user study (HRI'26 LBR); interface comparison only, no policy training per snippet.
- U-ARM (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02437, github.com/MINT-SJTU/LeRobot-Anything-U-Arm): $50 3D-printed leader arms; compares data-collection efficiency/success with Joycon. An alternative cheap leader the community already has.
- GELLO (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13037): passive kinematic-replica leaders using servos as encoders; no fidelity study.
- Data Quality in Imitation Learning, Belkhale et al. NeurIPS 2023 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02437): action divergence / transition diversity framing. Note: it proposes *transition diversity*, not "state diversity" as a quality metric; it argues against blindly maximizing state diversity.
- Comparison of IL algorithms for bimanual manipulation (https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06536): finds ACT/Diffusion (chunking) more robust to noise perturbations, which predicts Result B.
- Tremor suppression in teleoperation (e.g. CMU "Suppressing pathological tremor during dextrous teleoperation"; tremor filtering in master–slave welding; US patent 8649905 on mechanical damping in input devices): the damping-versus-tremor idea is established outside IL.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- "the stock STS3215 leader reads 4096 counts per revolution through gears with backlash": likely wrong. The STS3215's 12-bit magnetic encoder reports output-shaft position (0-4095 per output revolution despite 1:147-1:345 reduction), and the handle is fixed to the output horn. Gear backlash therefore sits between motor and output, not between handle and encoder. The fallback "resolution/backlash" contrast is much weaker than claimed.
- Belkhale et al. described as defining "action divergence and state diversity metrics, which we reuse": the paper's proposed properties are action divergence and *transition diversity*. State diversity is the conventional notion it critiques.
- "29 distinct positions per revolution" is quoted correctly from #2074 (closed as not planned, verified). But the proposal treats it as a quantization of ~12.4 degrees without noting that this is implausible for the gear ratios involved. Issue #1333 not verified (GitHub API blocked in sandbox).
- ALOHA 2 "improved leader ergonomics (my recollection)": correct, but understated. ALOHA 2 explicitly reduced leader backdrive friction, which makes it closer prior work than the proposal implies.
