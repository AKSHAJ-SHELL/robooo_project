# H1 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 6 / F 6.5 / I 6.5 → overall 6.33

## Novelty (6): 
The problem is real and is named in the base papers. AnySkin names ferromagnetic interference as its inherited limitation, and eFlesh's alternating-polarity fix targets stray field and crosstalk, not a steel object pressing on the skin (search snippets of arXiv 2409.08276 and 2506.09994). Arguing from the method of images that the error scales with the field the skin projects toward the object is a neat framing. I did not find it written up for tactile skins. But the one-sided property of Halbach and sinusoidal magnetization is textbook. Sinusoidally magnetized soft skins already exist (Yan et al., Sci. Robotics 2021; Yan et al., Nat. Mach. Intell. 2024, which overlays two sinusoidally magnetized films). So an IROS reviewer could fairly call this "a known magnet trick applied to a new failure mode". The proposal also missed directly competing prior work. In April–June 2026 XELA Robotics announced "magnetic interference compensation" for uSkin, marketed specifically for handling iron and steel parts and magnetic clips, launching Q3 2026 (Robot Report, RoboticsTomorrow, NewEquipment snippets). It is closed and its method is undisclosed, but it weakens the claim that "nobody fixes this". The ferrite pre-mirror is the most original piece, and also the least supported.

## Feasibility (6.5): 
The reuse is real. I cloned both repos. eFlesh (MIT) contains the microstructure/CAD pipeline, `characterization/` (model.py, train.py, datasets), `slip_detection/` with `data/seq_*.pt`, labels and checkpoints, and MLX90393 firmware. AnySkin (MIT) has the streaming library, and its README does say to press "B" to re-zero when drift appears. Neither repo mentions steel, ferromagnetic material or Halbach anywhere. The bench study on a printer-based indenter is very doable by December. Three things weaken the plan:
1. The KiCad re-draw and two PCB spins are mostly unneeded work. The baseline only needs the existing Gerbers, or bought AnySkin boards. Only the differential variant B needs a new board, and that is a secondary arm.
2. eFlesh places sparse 3/8" or 3/16" disc magnets in lattice pouches. A 4-orientation discrete Halbach of 3 mm cubes is a different magnet geometry, so the "same lattice" comparison is confounded unless the magnet count and volume are matched.
3. Bench work, SO-101 build, two downstream tasks and a differential arm add up to too much for two part-time students. The downstream study should be cut to Task 1.

## Impact (6.5): 
Does steel matter in practice? Yes, and that is the strongest part of the case. A commercial vendor built a feature for exactly this because factory customers asked for it (XELA). A Dec 2025 paper tells users to keep 10 mm of clearance from high-permeability objects (arXiv 2512.00907 snippet). Open magnetic skins (ReSkin, AnySkin, eFlesh, OSMO) are spreading fast in learning-from-touch labs, and a printable "steel-safe" magnet layout with released STLs and a simple design rule would be cited and reused by that community. The eFlesh/AnySkin authors would care, because it extends their own design axis (magnet layout) and uses their own pipeline as the baseline. The limits: the audience is narrow (magnetic-skin users). The downstream tasks (a 3 N force-limited grasp, slip on steel cans) are modest. If the result is a partial gain, say 30–50% less ΔRMSE at some cost in sensitivity, this is an RA-L/IROS borderline or strong workshop paper, not a main-track paper others build on. A clean physics model validated against measurements is what would lift it.

## Lab appeal: 
Winncy Du (SJSU) is a plausible but weak "best fit". Her magnetic-sensor credentials come from a 2014 textbook, her public publication list appears to stop around 2016, and labs.md rates her fit as "Medium" for mechanical/sensor work. She would likely host a sensor project but adds little tactile-manipulation credibility. Tae Myung Huh's Tactile Manipulation Lab at UCSC (tml.engineering.ucsc.edu; confirmed via search, Assistant Prof. in ECE since Jan 2023, works on tactile sensors for grasping) is a much better fit and should be the primary target. Separately, email the eFlesh first author (address in the repo README) to ask for board source files and feedback. Getting them as co-authors or endorsers is the biggest single impact lever.

## Main criticism: 
The pitch says the steel problem is open and unaddressed. It does not engage with existing compensation work (XELA 2026, OSMO's differential reference) or with the fact that one-sided sinusoidal/Halbach magnetization is already used in soft skins. Its central quantitative hope (3–10× less field at the object side from a hand-placed discrete Halbach inside a deforming TPU lattice) is unsupported until simulated. So the novelty rests on a physics argument nobody has checked.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Do the magpylib mirror-model study before anything else. Report the predicted one-sidedness ratio and steel-induced error for aligned, alternating and Halbach layouts at matched magnet volume, and put that figure in the pitch.
2. Cite XELA's 2026 compensation and position against it. Argue that a passive, open, printable layout fix complements algorithmic compensation, and include a simple software baseline: a reference-magnetometer or OSMO-style differential subtraction, run on the bought board.
3. Drop the KiCad re-draw and spin 2 for the main study. Use existing Gerbers or AnySkin boards, and only build variant B if time remains.
4. Cut the downstream study to Task 1 (force-limited grasp, steel vs. PLA vs. aluminium). Keep slip detection as optional.
5. Make Huh (UCSC) the primary lab and contact the eFlesh authors early. A letter or comment from them greatly increases reuse and impact.
6. Add a sensitivity-matched comparison, so the result reads as "steel error at equal PLA sensitivity" and cannot be dismissed as "weaker magnets, less interference".

## Closest prior work found:
- eFlesh, arXiv 2506.09994 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09994) — alternating polarity cuts stray field and crosstalk ~100×. This is the base and the baseline (snippet).
- AnySkin, arXiv 2409.08276 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08276) — names ferromagnetic interference as its limitation (snippet).
- XELA uSkin magnetic interference compensation, 2026 (https://www.therobotreport.com/xela-robotics-adds-2-enhancements-uskin-sensor-family-ahead-robotics-summit/) — commercial compensation for ferromagnetic material and magnets, aimed at metal handling. Method undisclosed; missed by the proposal.
- Yan, Zermane, Pan, Kheddar, Nat. Mach. Intell. 2024, doi 10.1038/s42256-024-00904-9 (https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/lirmm-04825067) — sinusoidally magnetized films for self-decoupled 3-axis force. Uses one-sided magnetization, but not for ferrous immunity.
- Numerical investigation of Halbach-array flexible magnetic sensors, 2025 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12693847/) — simulation only. Mentions reduced interference as a side benefit.
- OSMO glove, arXiv 2512.08920 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08920) — dual magnetometers plus MuMetal, 57% noise reduction. A differential baseline (snippet).
- Magnetic tactile-driven soft actuator, arXiv 2512.00907 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00907) — quantifies ferrite interference and recommends ≥10 mm clearance from steel (snippet).

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- "Problem is unaddressed / no fix exists" is overstated: XELA announced commercial ferromagnetic-interference compensation in 2026. The claim does hold for open-source skins, where I verified that the released eFlesh and AnySkin code has no compensation path.
- Calling Yan et al. NMI 2024 a "Halbach" skin is loose. It uses two overlaid sinusoidally magnetized films, which are Halbach-like.
- "3 to 10× less field" from a discrete 4-orientation Halbach is presented as Result A without any calculation. Treat it as an unverified guess.
- Du as "best fit" because magnetic-field modelling is "exactly her expertise" is overstated. Her recent public record shows no tactile or magnetic-skin work, and labs.md rates her fit as Medium.
- I did not check the claim that the ReSkin board has only Gerbers and no editable source. I found no KiCad files in the eFlesh or AnySkin repos, which is consistent with it.
- None of the links I checked were wrong. The eFlesh and AnySkin repos exist, are MIT-licensed, and contain the files the proposal names.
