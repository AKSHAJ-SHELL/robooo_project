# M1 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 6.5 / I 5.5 → overall 5.83

## Novelty (5.5):
The core question, how much goal-pose sensing error passes through a learned GPS-conditioned policy, is a fair and under-measured one for the LogoNav/OmniVLA/CityWalker family. I confirmed the base claims in the released code. The MBRA README line ("we use our own GPS instead of the mounted GPS on Frodobot") is real (README.md L107). `deployment/LogoNav_frodobot.py` does build the goal from one raw `/data` lat/lon plus `orientation` per tick, with a 30 m clip. The `goal_pose` bug is real too: it is assigned only inside `if distance_goal > thres_dist:`, while `LogoNav_ros.py` has the `else:` branch. But the headline "non-obvious insight", a goal-error transfer gain, is close to work that already exists. IntentReact (arXiv 2603.25382, 2026) adds systematic angular bias to the direction signal of a learned waypoint policy and reports that SPL barely changes up to 20° and keeps about 95% at 30°. They also note that small biases slightly *help*, i.e. the policy blends the goal with vision. That is the same "gain below 1" measurement, done closed-loop, on a different policy and in simulation. Two more papers already treat FrodoBots compass and GPS as biased and model them explicitly in data processing: the MBRA paper's own bidirectional EKF (states include compass bias γ and GPS bias δ) and "Data Scaling for Navigation in Unknown Environments" (arXiv 2601.09444; GTSAM fusion of GPS + magnetometer + wheel RPM, policy conditioned on "noisy" goal direction). So Result B (biased heading dominates) is partly expected by the authors themselves. Contribution (c), "unlocking RTK on the onboard LC29HDA", is weakened by vendor material (see below), which says the Mini+ already supports RTK automatically. What is left that is genuinely new: a real-robot 2×2 decomposition of position versus heading for a released navigation foundation model, plus a check that an offline open-loop sensitivity predicts closed-loop error. That is a careful, useful ablation, not a new method. Note also that the authors evaluated on the ERZ, not the Mini+, so "on the authors' own platform" overstates it.

## Feasibility (6.5):
No training is needed, the weights and the deployment script are MIT, and the hardware fits the budget easily. The external LC29H(DA) + NTRIP fallback is a standard, well-trodden path (str2str, free CRTN). The risks are real, though. (1) Access depends on FrodoBots SDK/4G tokens, and the adb/UART path into the closed `frodobot.bin` is untested by the team. (2) LogoNav was trained mostly on ERZ-style data, so on a lower-camera Mini+ it may fail for reasons unrelated to sensing, which would swamp the 2×2. (3) 104 outdoor runs at 0.3 m/s during a California winter by two part-timers is a lot of weekends, and the pilot SD of 4 m is a guess. Errors in the "stock" condition could be heavy-tailed (one magnetometer excursion means a 20 m miss), which would break the paired-t power estimate. (4) The SDK's `/data` exposes no fix-quality or RTK flag that I could find (only `gps_signal`), so the team may not even know whether the stock unit is already in RTK. Doable, but the timeline has little slack before the Dec 20 first-data date.

## Impact (5.5):
The audience is real but narrow: Earth Rover Challenge teams and LogoNav/OmniVLA users on FrodoBots hardware. A clean "you need X° heading accuracy" number would get used. But if the vendor already ships RTK, the practical recommendation shrinks to "fuse the gyro for heading", which a GNC reviewer will see as textbook. The finding is also tied to one policy, one rover and one campus. Without the optional OmniVLA replication, or the transfer-gain-predicts-closed-loop result holding cleanly, it reads as a strong workshop paper or a modest CASE paper. The bug report and logged dataset have some reuse value.

## Lab appeal:
UCSC ASL (Elkaim) is a sensible fit. Heading/GNSS fusion in front of a learned planner on a $300 open platform matches their GNC and low-cost-autonomy line and SIP mentoring. The lab gains a cheap platform and a sensor-fusion student project more than a research result, so expect help with the EKF and the RTK setup, not co-authorship enthusiasm. Carpin (outdoor ROS 2 navigation) is a weaker but plausible second choice.

## Main criticism:
The two claimed novelties are both already partly covered. The "transfer gain" is essentially IntentReact's angular-bias robustness test (and the policy-blends-goal-with-vision finding) moved to a different policy. "Unlocking onboard RTK" conflicts with FrodoBots' own statements that the Mini+ supports RTK with no setup. What remains is a well-designed but incremental real-robot ablation.

## What would raise the score:
1. Before anything else, find out what the stock Mini+ actually does. Read GGA fix_quality over adb, or ask FrodoBots whether and where it streams RTCM over 4G. Reframe (c) to match: "how often is vendor RTK actually fixed on sidewalks, and what happens when it drops to float/standalone".
2. Cite IntentReact and position against it explicitly. The new parts should be (a) real hardware, (b) position versus heading *separately*, (c) bias versus noise, and (d) a test of whether the open-loop gain predicts the closed-loop slope. Make (d) the headline, with a pre-registered prediction.
3. Measure the gain for at least two policies (LogoNav and OmniVLA-edge, both offline, which is cheap). "Different foundation models have different goal-trust" is a more citable result than one model's number.
4. Report heavy-tail metrics (95th-percentile final error, success at 3/15 m) and size the study from a real pilot. Consider a blocked design that uses route sections rather than full routes, to get more samples per hour.
5. Replace the on-campus magnetometer anecdote with a measured disturbance map of the routes, logged alongside RTK course-over-ground, so the heading-error distribution is reported as data rather than assumed.
6. Say clearly that the authors used the ERZ, and either borrow an ERZ or present the Mini+ as a platform shift.

## Closest prior work found:
- IntentReact (arXiv 2603.25382, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25382) [snippet]: injects systematic angular bias into the direction conditioning of a learned waypoint policy; robust up to 20°, about 95% SPL at 30°. This is the closest match to the transfer-gain idea.
- MBRA / LogoNav (arXiv 2505.05592, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05592v2) [snippet + code read]: bidirectional EKF with compass-bias and GPS-bias states for data cleaning; evaluation used external GPS on the ERZ.
- Data Scaling for Navigation in Unknown Environments (arXiv 2601.09444, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09444) [snippet]: FrodoBots data, GTSAM fusion of GPS/magnetometer/wheel RPM, policies conditioned on noisy goal direction and distance.
- OmniVLA (arXiv 2509.19480, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19480) [snippet]: 2D-pose goals with GPS localization on the ERZ; notes the poor localization in FrodoBots-2k.
- CityWalker (arXiv 2411.17820, https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17820v2) [snippet]: states sensitivity to large iPhone location noise as a limitation; does not quantify it.
- GeNIE (arXiv 2506.17960, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17960) [snippet]: ERC 2025 winner; path fusion meant to be stable under noisy GPS and heading. A modular system, not an analysis.
- ViKiNG (arXiv 2202.11271) and UT-ADL Milrem "vision-based off-road navigation with geographical hints" (https://adl.cs.ut.ee/research/projects/vision-based-off-road-navigation-with-geographical-hints) [snippet]: learned local planner plus a global planner designed to tolerate wrong GPS. Same design motivation, no transfer-gain measurement.
- Earth Rover Challenge (https://earth-rover-challenge.github.io/) [snippet]: 15 m checkpoint tolerance "accounts for GPS noise on consumer hardware".
- FrodoBots help center (https://intercom.help/frodobots/en/articles/11774641-what-is-an-earthrover-types-of-bots-and-use-cases) [snippet]: Mini+ "supports RTK", about 50 cm, "no setup is needed".
