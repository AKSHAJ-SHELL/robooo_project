# M1 — Reviewer 2 (Methodology rigor and feasibility for THIS team)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 6 / I 5.5 → overall 5.67

## Novelty (5.5):
I cloned the MBRA repo and confirmed the quoted README line, "we use our own GPS instead of the mounted GPS on Frodobot" (README.md line 107). So the limitation is real, and the authors never quantify the stock-sensor gap. The same README line also says the platform was the "Frodobots (ERZ)", the EarthRover Zero, not the Mini+. The SDK README lists "accurate GPS positioning" for the ZERO and not for the Mini+. A Mini+ study is therefore not "on the authors' own platform": it adds a hardware confound to the very question it asks. The broad finding that GPS and compass quality drive a learned point-goal policy has already been shown in simulation (Partsey et al., CVPR 2022: perfect GPS+Compass gives about 99.8% success in realistic PointNav; removing it drops performance sharply) [search snippet]. A FrodoBot sensor-characterization and EKF report also exists (Zhang & Visser, UvA IRL-UVA-24-01) [snippet]. The proposal cites neither. The offline goal-error "transfer gain" is a clean, quotable sensitivity measurement, and I found no published version of it for LogoNav, OmniVLA or CityWalker. That is the real novelty. The 2×2 sensor ablation and the injected-bias dose-response are careful engineering ablations, and Part 2 is partly derivable on paper: a constant 35° bias on a 30 m clipped goal will curve the path.

## Feasibility (6):
The claimed code bug is real. In `deployment/LogoNav_frodobot.py`, `goal_pose` is assigned only inside `if distance_goal > thres_dist:`, and the 5 m waypoint switch sits inside that same branch, so it can never fire. Within 30 m of the goal the variable is stale, or the script raises a NameError if the rover starts that close. `LogoNav_ros.py` has the `else:` branch. However, the ROS script uses a different axis convention and `thres_update = 2.0`, so "use the ROS-script logic" means a careful port, not a paste.

The RTK claim is half right. Wiki page `06-3-GPS定位获取.md` does name the LC29HDA, says "centimetre-level in open sky", and has a section 3 ("use an RTK service for cm-level positioning") stating that the module accepts RTCM3 input. That section is a one-line stub with no code. The UART belongs to the closed `/usr/bin/frodobot.bin`, which also runs the 4G/SDK link that the policy needs, so replacing it (the only documented path) likely breaks SDK control. Quectel forum threads show users frequently stuck at fix=1 when feeding RTCM to the LC29H [snippets].

In practice the external LC29H+ESP32 board is not a fallback. It is required in every condition as the ground-truth logger, so the plan should be built around it from day one. The "zero-cost onboard fix" headline then depends on a step with perhaps a 50% chance of working.

RTK correction availability is fine. CRTN gives schools one free NTRIP account (others pay a $100 processing fee), streams RTCM 3.3 at 1 Hz, and takes at least 10 business days to issue credentials [sopac-csrc.ucsd.edu snippet]. RTK2go is a backup.

Vendor risk is real but bounded. The SDK needs an access token from my.frodobots.com after bot activation (SDK README). I could not find whether there is a fee. Neither the SDK repo nor earth-rover-mini has a LICENSE file. Retail price snippets range from $299 to $399.

Field time is underestimated. At the 0.3 m/s clip, a 150 m route takes over 8 min, so 104 runs plus walk-back resets is closer to 15–20 h than 7 h. That is still doable over 5–6 weekends if December weather cooperates.

## Impact (5.5):
A defensible "how good must your GNSS and compass be for LogoNav/OmniVLA" number, plus a bug-fixed baseline, has practical reuse value for Earth Rover Challenge teams and FrodoBots owners. The audience is narrow, and the expected main result ("biased magnetometer heading hurts; RTK fixes the final approach") is close to what a GNC reviewer would predict. One problem is built into the design. With stock GPS the stop or waypoint-switch decision uses the stock fix, so final goal error in the stock-position cells will roughly equal the stock GNSS error at the goal. The position factor's effect on the primary metric is therefore close to circular. Path-efficiency and cross-track metrics carry the informative part. This reads as a solid workshop paper or a borderline CASE paper unless the transfer-gain result generalizes across two policies.

## Lab appeal:
UCSC ASL (Elkaim) is a good, specific fit: heading and GNSS fusion in front of a learned policy on a $300 open platform matches their low-cost GNC work and their SIP mentoring. UC Merced (Carpin) is plausible but weaker. Elkaim's group could also correct the EKF and magnetometer-calibration design, which is where this team is weakest.

## Main criticism:
The statistical unit and the ground truth are both weaker than the design implies:
- **The real sample size is the number of routes.** Magnetometer disturbance and multipath are fixed properties of each place, so the 3 repeats per route repeat the same bias. For the heading factor, the effective n for generalization is 6 routes, not 18 runs per cell. The mixed model with a random route effect over 6 levels gives a poor variance estimate.
- **Ground truth fails where the effect lives.** RTK fixes and RTK course-over-ground heading degrade to float or no-fix next to buildings and cars, which is exactly the 2 "bad" routes.
- **The headline comparison is off-platform.** It is run on a different rover (Mini+) from the one the authors used (ERZ).

## What would raise the score:
1. Make the external LC29H(DA) on a mast the ground-truth receiver in every run, and treat onboard RTCM injection as a stretch goal. Measure final error by tape from surveyed, chalk-marked goals, so ground truth does not depend on an RTK fix near buildings.
2. Increase route diversity rather than repetitions: for example 10–12 shorter routes (40–80 m) × 2 repeats, and spread repeats across sessions and times of day to decorrelate GNSS errors. Analyze with route as a fixed block, or report a route-level paired test.
3. Define the stop rule explicitly. Report final error both under the condition's own position estimate and as closest approach measured by ground truth, so the position factor is not circular.
4. Make the transfer gain the centerpiece. Measure it for LogoNav and OmniVLA-edge, split it into its position component and its goal-heading (cos/sin) component (the goal vector includes goal heading, so compass error enters twice), and pre-register a prediction of the closed-loop slope.
5. Before buying, email FrodoBots about SDK token cost and terms and about whether `frodobot.bin` can share the GNSS UART. If possible, borrow or compare an ERZ, or say plainly that the study covers the Mini+ and not the authors' robot.
6. Cite and position against Partsey et al. 2022 (PointNav GPS+Compass), Zhang & Visser 2024 (FrodoBot EKF) and the 2111.14666 study of sensor usage in real-robot navigation.
7. Re-budget field time at about 10 min per run, including resets.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- **"Authors' own platform" / "authors' robot family" is overstated.** The MBRA README says the evaluation used the Frodobots **ERZ** (EarthRover Zero). The SDK README credits the ZERO, not the Mini+, with "accurate GPS positioning".
- **"I found no RTCM or NTRIP handling anywhere in the released rover software" is incomplete.** Wiki 06-3 has a section 3 on RTK with RTCM3 input. It is a stub with no code, so the inference that stock firmware runs without RTK is still plausible.
- **"Use the ROS-script logic as the intended baseline" is not a drop-in.** The ROS script uses a different goal-axis convention and `thres_update = 2.0` against 5.0 in the FrodoBot script.
- **"Magnetometer heading" for the SDK `orientation` field is an unverified inference.** The SDK README does not state its source.
- **CRTN is "free with registration" only for schools and universities.** Others pay a $100 processing fee; it is one connection per organization, and credentials take at least 10 business days.
- **"~4 min per run" is inconsistent with the 0.3 m/s cap on 60–150 m routes.**
- **The rest checks out.** The SDK README IMU quote, the HF weights link, OmniVLA as ICRA 2026 under MIT, the existence of the LeRobot EarthRover Mini+ driver [docs snippet], `adb connect 192.168.11.1`, and replacing `/usr/bin/frodobot.bin` are all correct as quoted. I did not verify the ERC 15 m tolerance.
