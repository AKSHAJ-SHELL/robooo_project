# M1 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 6 / F 6.5 / I 6 → overall 6.17

## Novelty (6)
I cloned the MBRA repo and checked the base claims. The README sentence about using "our own GPS instead of the mounted GPS on Frodobot" is real (README line 107). `LogoNav_frodobot.py` does build the goal from one raw `/data` fix and the raw `orientation` with no filtering. The `goal_pose` bug is real: the goal is only set inside `if distance_goal > thres_dist`, the 5 m waypoint switch sits inside that same branch, and `LogoNav_ros.py` has the `else:` branch. So the gap is genuine and documented. OmniVLA's paper (search snippet) says only that its 3 trials per environment "account for GPS jitter", so nobody has quantified it on these models. Still, the general question "how does GPS noise degrade a learned navigation agent" has been asked before: there is a noisy-GPS ablation in a sidewalk-simulator paper (arXiv 1910.13249), CityNav (2406.14240) reports GNSS-noise degradation, and classical sidewalk stacks (2109.05603) report the same failure. The offline "goal-error transfer gain" is a nice, portable framing. But it is a standard input-sensitivity analysis, and a main-track reviewer will read the whole study as a careful ablation rather than a new result.

## Feasibility (6.5)
No training is needed, the policy runs on a laptop, and the external LC29H(DA)+ESP32 fallback keeps the RTK condition alive even if injecting corrections into the onboard module fails. The weak points are these. (a) Getting a stable RTK fix on an LC29HDA with a small antenna near motors is often flaky: Quectel forum threads and hobby repos show float↔fix flicker. CRTN baseline distance also matters. (b) LogoNav was trained and evaluated on the ERZ, not the Mini+ (the README's FrodoBot section says ERZ). A poor stock baseline on the Mini+ would mix up embodiment gap and sensing. (c) 104 closed-loop field runs plus surveys by two part-time students in Dec–Jan is tight but doable if the vendor/SDK access question is settled first. Part 3 (offline) is cheap and safe, which gives a result even if field work slips.

## Impact (6)
The audience is real but narrow: Earth Rover Challenge teams (ERC is running again at IROS 2026 with an Urban GPS track and a 15 m checkpoint tolerance), FrodoBots/BitRobot users, and the Hirose/Shah/Levine line (MBRA → OmniVLA → AsyncVLA). Two things limit how far the result travels. First, with a 15 m tolerance, position error is rarely the binding constraint for ERC. That leaves heading, and "fuse gyro + course-over-ground instead of a raw magnetometer" is textbook. Second, the MBRA paper (search snippet) already used a bidirectional EKF fusing GPS, compass and wheel speed to smooth its training labels. So the authors know the sensors are noisy and chose external GPS for evaluation. A result of "stock sensors are fine" or "fuse heading" is unlikely to change their practice. The transfer-gain number could get cited if it were released as a one-command tool that reports a sensing spec for any GPS-conditioned policy (LogoNav, OmniVLA-edge, CityWalker). As proposed, this is a solid workshop paper or a borderline CASE paper, not an IROS main-track paper.

## Lab appeal
The best fit, Elkaim's UCSC ASL, is a good match: GNSS/heading fusion on a low-cost open ground robot is their core work. A $300 platform with a learned policy on top is attractive for SIP interns. Carpin (UC Merced) would plausibly care about localization quality versus learned outdoor navigation, but it is not central to his work. The Kobayashi link is weak. The group most likely to engage is the MBRA/OmniVLA authors themselves, through a GitHub issue reporting the bug. That gives visibility but not mentorship.

## Main criticism
The likely findings are either "heading from a raw magnetometer is bad, fuse it" or "the policy absorbs the noise". Both read as engineering ablations, and the authors have already sidestepped the problem with external GPS and EKF-smoothed labels. Without a stronger general result, the paper's reach is mostly ERC participants.

## What would raise the score
1. Make the transfer gain the headline. Measure it offline on at least 3 policies (LogoNav, OmniVLA-edge, CityWalker), using public FrodoBots-2k frames as well as your own logs, and release it as a tool that outputs "required heading/position accuracy for X% success". That makes it citable beyond one robot.
2. Test whether the gain depends on the scene (open plaza versus constrained sidewalk). If the visual prior attenuates goal noise only where the scene constrains the path, that is a genuinely non-obvious result.
3. Add a cheap "fix" arm on the policy side, such as goal-noise augmentation during fine-tuning or a goal-smoothing wrapper, so the paper contributes something beyond better sensors.
4. Run a small number of ERZ runs (or justify the Mini+ with a camera-height check) so the baseline really is the authors' setup.
5. Report results at the ERC 15 m tolerance and at a tight 3 m tolerance, and state up front which matters for whom.
6. Contact the authors early (bug report plus a short note). A reply or a shared eval protocol would strengthen both impact and credibility.

## Closest prior work found
- MBRA/LogoNav, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05592 and https://github.com/NHirose/Learning-to-Drive-Anywhere-with-MBRA — the base. Uses external GPS for evaluation and an EKF over GPS, compass and wheel speed to smooth training labels (snippet).
- OmniVLA, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19480 — 2D-pose goals on the ERZ. Its evaluation "accounts for GPS jitter" with repeated trials but does not quantify it (snippet).
- Navigation Agents for the Visually Impaired: sidewalk simulator, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13249 — explicit noisy-GPS ablation on goal offsets for a learned agent (simulation).
- CityNav, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14240 — measures success drop under GNSS noise for learned navigation (aerial, simulation).
- CityWalker, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17820 — names GPS sensitivity as a limitation.
- UrbanVLA, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23576 — route-conditioned VLA designed around noisy consumer GPS routes.
- Learning to Navigate Sidewalks, https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05603 — GPS delay and noise broke waypoint switching (classical stack).
- Earth Rover Challenge, https://earth-rover-challenge.github.io/ — 15 m tolerance for consumer GPS noise. Ongoing at IROS 2026.
- Macro-Action Topological Navigation under Noisy Localization, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23055 — RL navigation under noisy localization (different policy class).

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal
- "On the authors' own platform": the README's FrodoBot instructions are for the ERZ, and OmniVLA was evaluated on the ERZ. The Mini+ is the same family but not the same platform (different camera height and chassis). The proposal partly admits this in Risk 3, but the novelty claim (a) overstates it.
- The proposal presents heading/GPS fusion as an "unused fix". The MBRA paper already used a bidirectional EKF over GPS, compass and wheel speed, offline for training labels (snippet). The fix is unused only at deployment, and the proposal should say so.
- Otherwise the verified items check out: the README quote, the `goal_pose` bug, and the SDK README text on IMU rate and "accurate GPS positioning" for ZERO. I did not verify the open-hardware wiki's LC29HDA and adb claims or the CRTN availability.
