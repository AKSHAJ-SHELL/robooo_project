# S1 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 6 / F 6 / I 6.5 → overall 6.17

## Novelty (6):
I cloned slam_toolbox (`ros2` HEAD 33841d0, Sep 21 2026) and the proposal's main code claims hold. The decentralized node is on `ros2`/`lyrical` and not on jazzy, kilted or humble. The producer publishes `range_scan->GetOdometricPose()` with identity covariance (L85-90). The receiver runs every peer scan through its own `Mapper::Process` (L144-156). Nothing carries owner-optimized poses back to peers, and the shipped yaml has `loop_search_maximum_distance: 3.0` and `coarse_search_angle_offset: 0.349`. So the replicas really do diverge by design. The package's own doc says "If a message is dropped, peers simply miss one inter-robot constraint" and promises "a consistent shared map". Nobody has tested either claim, and that is a real gap. The idea of measuring consistency without ground truth is not new, though. Decentralized-SLAM work already uses consistency error, meaning disagreement between robots' estimates and their mean (search snippet, arXiv 2403.05962 and related). The owner-digest fix is also a standard move: broadcast the owner's corrected states, as in DDF/CI-style fusion. What is new is applying both to the replicated-scan design that the default ROS 2 SLAM now ships. A main-track reviewer will read this as "careful audit of one package plus a sensible patch". That is a 6, not a 7.

## Feasibility (6):
The core measurement needs no slam_toolbox changes, and replay on a laptop removes the hardware from the critical path. That is the proposal's strongest feature. Three problems remain:
- **Loss model.** The share topic is created with plain depth 10 (`create_publisher(..., 10)`, L45-50), which in ROS 2 means RELIABLE, KEEP_LAST 10. On real Wi-Fi with netem, Fast DDS therefore retransmits. Application-level message loss happens only when an outage outlasts about 5 s of history (10 messages at about 2 Hz), or when a 3.6 KB message, split into about 3 IP fragments, keeps failing. So a Gilbert–Elliott relay that drops 30% of whole messages does not model what stock DDS does. V1 ("add retransmission") partly duplicates what DDS already provides. Live runs may show latency and bursts rather than drops.
- **Compute load.** 1,440 replays, each running 2 to 6 slam_toolbox containers at 2x real time on one laptop, is optimistic. Six full replicas each processing everyone's scans is about 36x the single-robot work.
- **The C++ soft-prior change (V2)** in the Karto/Ceres graph is genuinely hard for this team. Stata conversion (partial download from a 2.3 TB set, ROS 1 bags) is another source of week-scale slips.

## Impact (6.5):
The audience is real and large. slam_toolbox is Nav2's default SLAM. Lyrical is an LTS release supported to 2031, so this node will be the obvious multi-robot option for years. Forums show steady demand for multi-robot slam_toolbox and map merging (ROS Answers / m-explore threads). A measured safe operating envelope (loss, fleet size, Pi 5 CPU, bytes/s) plus an upstreamable consistency logger is the kind of thing Macenski typically merges and Nav2 users actually read. A ROSCon talk is a natural outlet. On the other hand, the node is under a year old with very few users, so the immediate user base is small. The result is tied to one implementation, which limits citation beyond the ROS community. Venue fit is better for IROS or an ICRA multi-robot/comms workshop than for CASE, whose automation focus fits a SLAM-package audit less well. Likely outcome: a strong workshop paper or a borderline main-track paper with real reuse value.

## Lab appeal:
Mehta's LEMUR is the best intellectual fit. Chang, Chen and Mehta (T-RO 2022, verified via arXiv 2108.08789 snippet) is precisely about keeping multi-robot estimates consistent when communication is imperfect. A CI-weighted digest on real ROS 2 robots is a concrete hook, though their published evaluation is on cooperative localization, not SLAM back ends. Carpin's fit is weaker than the proposal claims. His multi-robot work leans toward planning, orienteering, map learning and agricultural Nav2 (Fast map merging is old work), so he would be a useful CASE/RA-L sounding board but probably not an active helper. Kitts's multi-robot work is cluster-space formation control of rovers and boats (search snippets), largely GPS-localized, so the fit is weak. Elkaim's ASL (Pi + ROS 2 + Nav2, HS mentoring via SIP) would like the hardware and tooling but has no multi-robot SLAM work I could find.

## Main criticism:
The experiment's central independent variable, "packet loss", is not what the stock system experiences. The share topic is RELIABLE with depth 10, so real Wi-Fi loss becomes retransmission latency, and message drops only happen during multi-second outages. Meanwhile the proposed digest is not actually "sub-1 kB/s". At 16 B per node, 1 Hz, and about 2 nodes/s per robot, a 10-minute run reaches about 1,200 nodes, so a full digest is about 19 KB/s per robot. That is more than the about 7 KB/s of scan traffic it is meant to economize, unless it is windowed or delta-coded. Both points go to the heart of the paper's claims and must be fixed before any lab or reviewer takes the numbers seriously.

## What would raise the score:
1. Model loss the way the stack does. Run netem under real Fast DDS and Zenoh with stock QoS (RELIABLE, depth 10) as the primary arm, and report message-level loss, latency and reordering that actually reach the callback. Keep the application-level relay only as a stress test or a BEST_EFFORT arm, and say so.
2. Redesign the digest as windowed or delta-coded (recently changed nodes only, plus a periodic rolling checksum) and re-derive the bandwidth. Then test the PR author's claim that bandwidth is the first bottleneck against replicated CPU on a Pi 5 with N = 2 to 6.
3. Cite and read Athukorala et al., "Multi-Robot Collaborative SLAM (Multi-SLAM) With Distributed Lightweight Predictive Frontier Exploration (LPFE)", IEEE RA-L 11(2):2274-2281, 2026, DOI 10.1109/LRA.2025.3645990 per a search snippet. It appears to be the PR author's own paper on this framework. Use its reported setup and metrics as the "original team's" baseline, and contact the author (SUTD), who listed lossy Wi-Fi as future work.
4. Drop V2 (C++ soft priors) from the main plan. Make the paper "audit + V1/re-seeding + upstreamed logger", with V2 as optional. This raises feasibility without hurting impact much.
5. Shrink the grid. Run the bulk at N = 2 and 4 and use 6 only for the scaling plot. Get a pilot SD by early November and re-run the power analysis.
6. Retarget the pitch. Lead with LEMUR (Chang/Mehta CI consistency) and the slam_toolbox maintainers via an upstream issue or PR early (November). Pitch Carpin with a one-plot email asking about evaluation design, and list Kitts as a low-probability contact.
7. Add one downstream consequence metric, for example the disagreement between replicas about where robot k is, and whether it would cause a Nav2 collision or deconfliction error. This turns "cm of disagreement" into something Nav2 users care about.

## Closest prior work found:
- Athukorala, Lau, Pongsirijinda, Yuen, Tan, "Multi-SLAM with Distributed LPFE", IEEE RA-L 2026 (dblp/OpenAlex search snippet; SUTD repository blocked). Same authors and lab as PR #592, describing distributed multi-robot SLAM that generates "globally optimized maps in a network bandwidth efficient manner". Very likely the paper behind this node, and **missing from the proposal**. Its communication-loss evaluation is unknown (not opened).
- slam_toolbox `docs/decentralized_multi_robot_slam.md` (read from the clone). Claims robustness to dropped messages and "a consistent shared map". This is the hypothesis being tested.
- Lajoie et al., "Multi-Robot Decentralized Collaborative SLAM in Planetary Analogue Environments: Dataset, Challenges, and Lessons Learned", arXiv 2601.21063 (snippet). Covers limited and intermittent comms with 3 robots and releases peer-to-peer throughput and latency measurements, so trace reuse looks plausible. It uses a different (sparse, 3D) architecture.
- Chang, Chen, Mehta, T-RO 2022, arXiv 2108.08789 (snippet). Consistency under imperfect communication via covariance intersection. The natural theory partner.
- Lajoie & Beltrame, Swarm-SLAM, arXiv 2301.06230 (snippet). Sparse decentralized C-SLAM, budgeted communication.
- Consistency-error metric (disagreement between robots' estimates and their mean) in decentralized SLAM / belief-space planning, e.g. arXiv 2403.05962 (snippet). Prior art for the "GT-free metric".
- ROS 2 DDS over lossy wireless: work identifying IP fragmentation, retransmission timing and discovery bursts as loss causes (arXiv 2508.10413 and 2508.11366, snippets). Directly relevant to the loss model.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- "No message carries sequence numbers, retransmission…" is misleading. The topic uses default RELIABLE QoS (depth 10), so DDS already retransmits lost samples. What is missing is application-level gap repair after history overflow, and any exchange of optimized poses.
- The "sub-1 kB/s owner digest" (16 B per node at 1 Hz) is not sub-1 kB/s for any realistic graph size unless it is windowed or delta-coded (about 19 KB/s at 1,200 nodes).
- The closest prior work list omits the PR author's own RA-L 2026 paper (above).
- The PR is described as shipping a node in `lyrical` "and newer only". That is correct (verified absent from the jazzy and kilted branches, present on lyrical and ros2). The PR page itself says the separate node was named `multirobot_slam_toolbox_node` at the time, while the code now uses `decentralized_multirobot`. This is a minor naming difference, not an error.
- "Carpin… multi-robot map learning" is a fair citation (ICRA 2025, from labs.md), but presenting Carpin as the best-fit lab overstates the match. His work is not on SLAM consistency.
- Kitts's work is described as "multi-robot rovers". It is accurate, but it is formation control, not mapping, so this lab adds little.
