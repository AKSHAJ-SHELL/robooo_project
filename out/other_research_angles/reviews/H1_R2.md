# H1 — Reviewer 2 (Methodology rigor and feasibility: magnet physics, fabrication, reuse, statistics, timeline/budget)
Scores: N 6 / F 5 / I 5.5 → overall 5.50

## Novelty (6): 
Using a Halbach layout to make a magnetic tactile skin robust to *contacting* ferrous objects is not something I found in the tactile literature. Yan et al. (NMI 2024) use Halbach films for force decoupling, and PMC12693847 uses them for range and sensitivity, not ferrous immunity. The idea is less new than the proposal says, for three reasons. (a) Using a Halbach "working side vs non-working side" to reduce susceptibility to ferrous surroundings is standard practice in magnetic position encoders (e.g. US 2023/0082380, seen as a search snippet). (b) XELA Robotics announced in 2026 a uSkin compensation that "removes complex magnetic interference from nearby magnets or ferromagnetic materials" for metal handling (press-release snippet; method not disclosed), so the claim that the problem is unaddressed is overstated. (c) My own magpylib check (below) suggests the core layout comparison can largely be predicted analytically, which runs into Lesson 1. The real empirical uncertainty is in fabrication: magnet rotation inside soft TPU, preload, end leakage. The ferrite "pre-mirror" is the more original element, but it is also the weakest physically (see Feasibility).

## Feasibility (5): 
**Physics.** I checked the mirror argument. For a μ→∞ plane, the image magnetization is the pseudovector reflection (−Mx, −My, Mz), so a layout whose weak side faces the object does give an image whose weak side faces the chips. The hypothesis is sound. I ran a quick magpylib image model (8×8 grid of 3 mm N52 cubes, 5 chips at the ReSkin positions, magnet centres 4–6 mm above the chips, 2–4 mm below the outer surface; script saved as h1r2_mirror.py in my session scratchpad). Phantom-to-signal ratio for a 0.3 mm press came out at about 7–10 for aligned, 0.35–1.7 for a 1-D alternating array, and 0.24–0.32 for the correct-handed Halbach. That is roughly 30× better than aligned but only about 1.5–5× better than alternating, and the reversed-handed Halbach is about 7×, so magnets inserted the wrong way round are catastrophic. 

**Saturation.** The same model puts 70–200 mT at the chips on the strong side. The MLX90393 is linear only up to about 50 mT (datasheet/Adafruit snippet). The "strong side toward the magnetometers" design will therefore saturate the board unless the team adds standoff or uses fewer magnets, and both cost the sensitivity the proposal says it keeps. The proposal never mentions this. It also changes the magnets themselves: eFlesh uses 3/8"×1/8" discs, the proposal uses 3 mm cubes. Variants that differ in magnet size, count and orientation all at once are confounded unless total moment or baseline signal is matched.

**Fabrication.** Hand-placing about 64 tightly packed 3 mm N52 cubes in four orientations inside a soft TPU lattice is the hardest step. Neighbouring cubes exert torques and forces comparable to the measured contact forces, so they will flip, migrate or pre-strain the lattice unless a rigid printed jig and adhesive are used. That changes the mechanics compared with the eFlesh baseline. 

**Ferrite sheet.** Thin (0.1–0.2 mm) NFC ferrite sheets will likely saturate next to N52 magnets [memory: sintered ferrite B_sat about 0.3–0.5 T]. SoftMag (arXiv 2512.00907, snippet) measured only about 0.4 G at contact from a ferrite-skinned object, which suggests such sheets are weak mirrors. The sheet will also attract the magnets and preload the skin. 

**Scope.** The plan has two PCB spins, a KiCad re-draw, a differential board, four skins, a printer indentation rig, an SO-101 build, instrumented objects, a force-limited grasp task and a slip task. That is too much for two part-time students in about 15 weeks. The core bench question needs none of the PCB work: the existing Gerbers + BOM + pick-and-place can go straight to JLC PCBA. 

**Statistics.** The power analysis treats 75 location×depth points as independent pairs. It ignores the biggest variance source, which is print-to-print and magnet-placement variation, because there is one skin per variant. ΔRMSE is also a single statistic per skin, so the mixed model as written cannot separate "design" from "this particular print". At least 3 replicate skins per variant are needed. Budget (about $700–880) is realistic.

## Impact (5.5): 
A tested, open design rule ("use correct-handed Halbach with standoff X; alternating gets you Y; here is the saturation trade-off") would be useful to the ReSkin/AnySkin/eFlesh/OSMO user base. The downstream steel-can grasp is a good hook. But the main result may be partly predictable from simulation, and the 8 mm steel ball indenter may produce perturbations near the noise floor for every variant (SoftMag saw <0.1 G at 3.5 mm for ferrite). That floor effect could make the bench comparison uninformative. With a strong result this is a solid workshop paper or an outside chance at CASE; it is unlikely to be a main-track IROS paper.

## Main criticism: 
The method is not built around the physics that decides the outcome. The Halbach benefit over eFlesh's own alternating layout is modest in an ideal model (about 1.5–5×). It depends on magnets being placed and kept at the correct handedness inside a soft lattice. The strong-side field saturates the MLX90393. And there is one print per variant, so the experiment cannot tell a design effect from fabrication variance. Meanwhile, half the schedule goes on PCB re-draws and a robot study that the core question does not need.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Run the magpylib study first and include it in the proposal. Model eFlesh's actual disc sizes and spacing, the MLX90393 50 mT limit, realistic steel objects (an 8 mm ball, a 1 mm sheet, a flat block or can) rather than a half-space, and ±10–20° magnet tilt from lattice deformation. Show where the predicted advantage over *alternating* survives.
2. Match the variants on baseline signal or total magnet moment, so that "Halbach vs alternating" is not confounded with magnet size and count.
3. Build ≥3 replicate skins per variant. Analyse with skin as a random effect and size the experiment on that variance (pilot it in November).
4. Drop the KiCad re-draw and the differential board. Order the released Gerbers + BOM via JLC PCBA, converting the Altium pick-and-place to CPL. Keep the downstream study to Task 1 only.
5. Add a software-only baseline: re-zero on approach, plus a 5-chip spatial-pattern compensation or detector trained on non-contact steel approach. That shows whether hardware is needed at all.
6. Design a printed placement jig plus glue for the Halbach cubes, and verify each skin's orientation with a Hall-probe scan before testing.
7. Treat the ferrite sheet as an exploratory arm. Measure its DC permeability and saturation near the magnets before building on it.

## Closest prior work found: 
- eFlesh, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09994 / https://github.com/notvenky/eFlesh: alternating polarity cuts stray field and crosstalk about 100×. This is the baseline and already partly reduces the field reaching the object.
- AnySkin, https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08276v2: names ferromagnetic interference as a limitation; no fix.
- SoftMag, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00907: ferrite-object interference up to 0.4 G at contact, below 0.1 G at 3.5 mm; recommends ≥10 mm clearance (snippets).
- OSMO, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08920: MuMetal plus dual-magnetometer differential sensing against crosstalk and external fields.
- Yan et al., NMI 2024, doi 10.1038/s42256-024-00904-9 (HAL lirmm-04825067): Halbach / sinusoidal films for self-decoupled 3-axis force, not interference.
- PMC12693847 (Sensors 2025, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12693847/): FEM-only Halbach flexible sensor for range and sensitivity.
- XELA uSkin 2026 interference compensation, https://www.roboticstomorrow.com/news/2026/04/29/xela-robotics-deepens-us-market-commitment-with-plug-and-play-investment-and-new-sensor-capabilities-to-be-introduced-at-the-robotics-summit-and-expo-in-boston/26491/: commercial compensation for ferromagnetic materials and magnets (method undisclosed). This is a missed piece of prior art.
- Halbach position encoder patent, https://patents.justia.com/patent/20230082380: working/non-working side, with a ferrous holder to reduce external interference. Standard trick in position sensing.
- Peng et al., "Interference-resistant multimodal flexible e-skin", ROBOT 2025 47(1):55-63, https://robot.sia.cn/en/article/doi/10.13973/j.cnki.robot.240194: anti-magnetic-interference e-skin, adjacent.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal: 
- "eFlesh's released LSTM slip detector": the released checkpoint is `slip_detection/checkpoints/eflesh_linear.pkl`, a linear model, and `run_handover_demo.yaml` points to it. An LSTM class (`model/lstm_model.py`) and `train.py` do exist, so retraining is possible. The slip data (`seq_*.pt`, 138 files) was recorded on a Hello Stretch with the original magnet layout. It cannot train detectors for new layouts on an SO-101, so the claimed saving of a dataset is overstated.
- eFlesh firmware licence: the repo LICENSE is MIT, but `arduino/5X_eflesh_stream.ino` carries a Beerware header. This is minor.
- "A KiCad re-draw is required because there is no editable source": this is true only for the differential Variant B. Variant A can be fabricated and assembled directly from the released `5X_ReSkin.Zip` (Gerber, NC drill, pick-and-place) plus `5X_ReSkin_BOM.xlsx`.
- "Halbach … without losing sensitivity / strong side toward the magnetometers": this ignores MLX90393 saturation at about 50 mT. An ideal-model check gives 70–200 mT at 4–6 mm standoff for packed 3 mm cubes.
- "Far-field cancellation (eFlesh alternating) reduces the wrong quantity": this is overstated. Alternating poles at small pitch also decay quickly toward the object. In my image model, 1-D alternating already gives about 6–25× less phantom-to-signal than aligned.
- The implied claim that no one addresses ferrous interference: XELA uSkin (2026) markets a compensation for exactly this.
- The SoftMag 0.4 G figure is for a ferrite-skinned object, not steel. The proposal cites it correctly as "ferrite", but uses it as evidence about steel.
- Confirmed correct: AnySkin README "press B to recalibrate"; no compensation path in the AnySkin or eFlesh firmware or library; eFlesh `characterization/train.py` is the 2-layer, 128-unit MLP.
