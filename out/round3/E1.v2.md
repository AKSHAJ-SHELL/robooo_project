**Research question.** A sidewalk robot carries its camera at 30 cm. When a phone-scan Gaussian-splat twin of a sidewalk is rendered at that robot's exact poses, how far do a navigation foundation model's outputs move compared with the real frames? Is the shift larger when the twin was captured at human height (the Vid2Sim / EmbodiedSplat practice) than at robot height? And do the image metrics people use to judge twins (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DINOv2 feature distance) track that shift, or a closed-loop twin-vs-real disagreement on a real robot?

**Who benefits.**
- **Builders of splat-based navigation benchmarks:**
  - NavBench-GS / S2E, from VAIL-UCLA;
  - Wanderland / wanderland-lab, from NYU AI4CE;
  - NavArena (more than 2,000 splat scenes [S]);
  - Neverwhere (more than 60 splat scenes [S]);
  - SidewalkBench (Isaac Sim, 9 models [S]);
  - EmbodiedSplat (iPhone scans).

  All of them render policies at camera heights or viewpoints that differ from their capture rigs. Wanderland's own simulated camera sits at about 1.1 m [G]. We give them a measured answer for low cameras, plus a per-policy fidelity check that needs only one teleoperated drive.
- **Small-robot builders, start-ups and research groups who want to test a sidewalk robot in a phone scan of their own street:** a capture rule (height), and evidence on whether checking LPIPS is enough.
- **Evaluation methodology:** the first SRCC-style numbers for outdoor sidewalk navigation that come with a real-vs-real reliability ceiling.
- **Release:** paired real/twin data. This means 16 routes, 32 splats, synchronised robot logs (camera, lidar, odometry), 64 real episodes plus their twin replays, and the code. Episode start and goal files are also given in wanderland-lab's `episodes.json` schema, so others can rerun them.

**The assumption tested and who relies on it.** The assumption: "A photoreal reconstruction captured from human-viewpoint video is a faithful evaluator for a low robot's visual policy."
- **Vid2Sim** (CVPR 2025 [S]): monocular hand-held video. Its authors add screen-space covariance culling because rendering degrades "when the agent's camera trajectory deviates substantially from the training views" [S].
- **NavBench-GS / S2E:** scenes from `vid2sim_raw` captures [G: S2E README]; robots are the COCO delivery robot and a Unitree Go2.
- **Wanderland** [G]. The pose generator defaults to `cam_height` 1.2 m (`navigation/generate_cam_poses.py`). The wanderland-lab agent is a 0.2 m-radius sphere with its camera offset 0.9 m above the centre, so the camera sits about 1.1 m above the ground (`envs/scene.py`). Dataset start poses are "a recording-rig camera pose roughly 1 m above the street" (`docs/reproducing_baselines.md`).
- **EmbodiedSplat** (ICCV 2025 [S]): high sim-real correlation indoors, with capture height roughly matched to the robot. The 1.31 m Stretch camera height is unverified [R].
- **R2S-EGO** (2608.06827 [S]): "sparse human capture can leave behaviour-scoped robot views under-supported." It offers a training fix, not an evaluation-validity test.

A finding in their own docs supports our emphasis on parity checks [G: `docs/reproducing_baselines.md`]. Without the goal row, CityWalker "emits a ~0 waypoint, and the agent freezes". The doc says "the legacy reference server had exactly this defect, which is why early Wanderland runs looked like an out-of-distribution model failure rather than a port bug." We use their fixed port as our CityWalker reference.

**How E1.v2 sits with Wanderland.** Wanderland argues that image-only phone and video twins are unreliable, and fixes this with expensive multi-sensor capture at about human height. It does not test the case most low-cost robot builders actually face: a phone twin, evaluated at a camera far below the capture height, against a real robot. A null result for capture height would mean phone twins at matched or mismatched height are equally (un)reliable for these policies. A positive result gives a cheap fix ("capture at deployment height") that complements the lidar fix. Either answer is useful to the Wanderland and Vid2Sim lines. We do **not** add a MetaCam-class capture condition: there is no budget for it, and it would bloat the study.

**Why the outcome is uncertain.**
- **Frozen DINOv2-class encoders may ignore splat artefacts.** CityWalker uses a frozen DINOv2-B; S2E uses DINOv3. If so, the output divergence D would be small and similar for both capture heights, even where LPIPS is poor.
- **Small CNNs may be insensitive at low resolution.** LogoNav uses EfficientNet-B0 at 96×96, so it may also be insensitive. Or it may react strongly to smeared near-field pavement, where a 30 cm camera spends most of its pixels.
- **A 1.4 m capture sees the ground 0.5–3 m ahead of a 30 cm camera only at grazing angles.** That is where floaters and smeared curbs appear (Mode-GS documents splat drift in ground-robot trajectories [S][R]).
- **Image metrics may or may not track policy shifts.** Lindström et al. found FID and LPIPS to be strong indicators of the real-to-sim gap for detectors and online mapping [S]. So "image metrics are enough" is a live outcome, not a straw man.

**Possible results** (each is informative):
- **(A) No height effect, and LPIPS tracks D.** Human-height phone twins are as good as robot-height ones for these policies at 30 cm, and pixel metrics suffice. This is a bound with a stated minimum detectable effect, useful to Vid2Sim-style pipelines.
- **(B) Height effect, which LPIPS misses.** This gives a capture rule, and shows that fidelity must be checked in output space.
- **(C) Both captures far above the real-vs-real floor.** Phone twins at any height are unfit at 30 cm, which supports Wanderland's move to lidar-grounded capture from an independent, low-viewpoint angle.

**Non-obvious insight.**
- **Counterfactual open-loop divergence.** We render each twin at the exact poses of a teleoperated drive. We feed real frames and rendered frames to the same frozen policy and measure the waypoint difference D. This costs no extra robot time and gives thousands of paired frames.
- **The real-vs-real floor.** The same policy is applied to two real teleoperated passes, matched by pose. This floor is what makes D interpretable. A twin cannot be expected to agree better than the real world agrees with itself.

**Closest prior work and exactly what is new.**
1. **Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity" (RA-L 2020, arXiv 1912.06321 [S]).** Defines the Sim-vs-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) for PointGoal navigation (Habitat vs LoCoBot). SRCC was 0.18 for success in the CVPR19 challenge setup [S], and 0.844 after tuning [R]. *We adopt SRCC.* Differences: we test an outdoor sidewalk with a low camera, manipulate capture height, and add a test-retest ceiling. Our SRCC is computed over route × policy cells, because two policies are too few for Kadian's across-agent version; we state this.
2. **Wanderland** (CVPR 2026 [S][G]); see the table above.
3. **EmbodiedSplat** (ICCV 2025 [S]): indoor ImageNav, high sim-real correlation, no height gap.
4. **NavDP** (arXiv 2505.08712 [S]). Real-to-sim GS fine-tuning raises real success by about 30% [S]. R3 reports a snippet claiming its real-to-sim evaluation is "consistent with real-world evaluation" [R]; I could not confirm that sentence. It uses RGB-D, has a CC-BY-NC-SA licence, and is marked `unavailable` in VisNavKit [G], so it is not used here.
5. **Cam2Sim** (arXiv 2607.04770 [S]). Real-vs-GS-vs-CARLA closed-loop comparison for a DAVE-2 steering model on a Munich research vehicle. Behaviour-space fidelity for driving at car height. We add low capture and evaluation height, navigation foundation models, and a test-retest floor.
6. **Lindström et al., "Are NeRFs ready for autonomous driving?"** (CVPRW 2024, arXiv 2403.16092 [S]). Relates detector and mapping real-to-sim gaps to image metrics, and finds FID and LPIPS strong. We ask the same question for navigation-policy outputs, where the answer may differ.
7. **PolaRiS** (arXiv 2512.16881 [S]) and **Real-to-Sim Policy Evaluation with GS** (2511.04665 [S]). Manipulation; Pearson r and MMRV between sim and real.
8. **NavBench-GS / S2E** [G]. Its benchmark `envs/metrics/scenarios` modules are empty, and "Benchmark detail settings" is unreleased. It is not used as a proxy.
9. **"Measuring Asset and Scene Reconstruction Effects in Real-to-Sim Robot Evaluation"** (arXiv 2610.00731 [R]). Surfaced by R3. My exact-title search did not return it, and I have not read it. It must be read before submission, because its title suggests a factor study close to ours.

**What is new (claimed narrowly):**
- (i) A controlled capture-height manipulation of splat twins, evaluated at a 30 cm camera on real outdoor routes.
- (ii) Whether image or feature metrics track navigation-policy output divergence, measured against a real-vs-real floor.
- (iii) SRCC for sidewalk navigation foundation models, with a test-retest ceiling.

We do not claim to be the first sim-vs-real study, the first behaviour-based fidelity metric, or the first outdoor splat evaluation.

**Main hypothesis (single, pre-registered on OSF before any robot data, by Dec 1).**
- **H1.** For robot poses on real sidewalk routes, the per-frame policy-output divergence D is larger for twins captured at 1.4 m (T_H) than for twins captured at 0.3 m (T_R).
  - D is the L2 distance between the first k predicted waypoints, in metres in the robot frame, for the policy on a real frame vs on the render at the same pose.
  - Test: one-sided site-level sign-flip permutation on route-averaged log D (both policies averaged), 16 routes at 8 sites, α = 0.05.
  - H1 can fail. Frozen-encoder invariance would make D_TH ≈ D_TR.

**Pre-registered secondary analyses** (estimation with CIs, no extra confirmatory claims):
- **S1 (part of the primary contribution, guaranteed).** How much of the frame-level and 2 m-segment-level variation in D do PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS and DINOv2 feature cosine distance explain? Within-route Spearman; segment R² with a route-cluster bootstrap. Does capture height still predict D after adjusting for LPIPS?
- **S2.** Is the H1 effect present in each policy separately?
- **C1 (closed-loop confirmation).** SRCC between twin and real closed-loop outcomes, for each capture. Is the |twin − real| gap larger for T_H (route-clustered paired test)? Does episode-level D predict the closed-loop gap better than LPIPS or DINO distance? All correlations are reported raw and corrected for real-run reliability.
- **S3 (scene change).** Does D spike within ±2 m of a moved cart, scored by AUC?

**Experiment design.**

- **October floor check (zero cost, before ordering parts).**
  - **Video.** One student walks 3 home-street sidewalk segments of about 20 m each, with cardboard boxes as obstacles. The phone is held twice: on a wagon or skateboard mount at 0.3 m, and by hand at 1.4 m. A tape-measured baseline gives scale.
  - **Trajectory.** COLMAP recovers the path.
  - **Policies run open-loop on Colab:**
    - CityWalker, through the wanderland-lab HF port (`ai4ce/citywalker`, goal row on, `target_horizon_m: 5.0`, as in `configs/benchmark/policy/citywalker.yaml` [G]);
    - LogoNav, through upstream `deployment/` code [G];
    - S2E-BC, through the zoo `S2ENavigator` [G].
  - **Scoring.** First-waypoint heading error against the walked path, compared with a "go straight" baseline, and whether the policy passes boxes on the free side.
  - **Kill rule.** At 1.4 m, a failure means a port bug: fix it, using the Wanderland goal-row lesson. At 0.3 m, if a policy is no better than go-straight, drop it.
  - **Gate for S2E.** S2E becomes the third policy only if it passes the floor check *and* matches VisNavKit's `model=s2e` open-loop outputs on the same frames.
  - **Second purpose.** The same videos test whether the 0.3 m capture registers in COLMAP jointly with the 1.4 m capture (R2's concern).
- **Policies.** CityWalker and LogoNav GPS-goal. Both are deterministic regressors, so twin rollouts are repeatable. S2E-BC is the optional third, under the two gates.
- **Robot.** Hoverboard base (reused from the bin robot) with:
  - a Jetson Orin Nano Super;
  - one calibrated wide-angle camera at 0.30 m;
  - an LD19 2D lidar, which is never a policy input;
  - hall-sensor wheel odometry;
  - a foam bumper with microswitches.

  ROS 2 runs slam_toolbox for the per-site map.
- **Sites and routes.**
  - 8 sites: distinct street blocks or a school campus, at least 200 m apart. Each site has 2 routes of 15–25 m, so 16 routes in all.
  - Static obstacles on every route: boxes and planters, plus refuse carts on collection day.
  - Runs happen early in the morning. If a pedestrian approaches, the run is paused and annotated. No human-subjects data is collected, and faces are blurred before release.
- **Per-route session (about 75 min, dry pavement):**
  1. Place 4 AprilTag stakes.
  2. T_H phone walk at 1.4 m and T_R pole walk at 0.3 m, both following the robot path, with the same phone and locked exposure.
  3. 2 teleoperated robot drives: the centreline, then a ±0.5 m weave to cover off-nominal poses.
  4. 2 policies × 2 repeats from the same start = 4 real episodes.
- **Totals:**
  - 32 teleoperated drives;
  - **64 real episodes**, or 96 with S2E;
  - 32 splats (gsplat on a free Colab T4, at reduced resolution).
- **Scale, alignment, rendering.**
  - **One coordinate frame for everything.** One COLMAP model per route registers all images: T_H, T_R and the robot frames. Each splat then trains only on its own capture subset, so both twins and the robot share one frame.
  - **Metric scale.** Sim(3) (Umeyama) from the COLMAP centres of the robot frames to the slam_toolbox poses, using the calibrated camera extrinsic.
  - **Check and fallback.** AprilTag stake positions give an independent check. The pre-set acceptance threshold is 5 cm RMSE. If the robot frames do not register, the AprilTags give the fallback Sim(3).
  - **Rendering.** Renders use the robot camera's calibrated intrinsics, and real frames are undistorted. Appearance embeddings are off, and captures happen within 1 hour of the runs.
- **Goals.** Point goals in the slam_toolbox map frame (no GNSS). They are converted to each policy's local goal from robot odometry: for CityWalker, the goal row, clamped to the 5 m horizon; for LogoNav, its waypoint spacing with `metric_waypoint_spacing=0.25` [G]. The twin uses identical conversions.
- **Open-loop (primary).**
  - Each policy runs on every teleoperated frame, at its own cadence: CityWalker at 1 Hz, as in `config/finetune.yaml` [G]; LogoNav at its deployment rate.
  - For each frame we compute D_TH, D_TR, the four image metrics, and the real-vs-real D floor between the two drives (pose match within 10 cm and 3°).
  - This yields about 200 frames per route per policy per capture.
- **Closed-loop twin replay (confirmatory).**
  - Each real episode is replayed from the same start in T_H and in T_R: render at pose → policy → unicycle model with the robot's measured velocity limits and logged latency.
  - **Collision uses the robot's own 2D lidar occupancy map.** This is simpler than v1's Gaussian-density collision and follows Wanderland's geometry-from-lidar lesson.
  - **Outcomes (continuous first):**
    - mean lateral distance between the twin and real trajectories;
    - route progress fraction;
    - minimum clearance;
    - collision.
- **Scene change (S3).** On 4 pre-randomised routes, after the main runs, a refuse cart is moved to a spot drawn from a Scenic program (sidewalk edge, passable gap of at least 0.9 m). We then do 1 teleoperated drive and 1 episode per policy against the now-stale twins.

**Power (cluster-aware simulation, `sim/e1v2_power.py`; all variance components are assumptions, to be re-estimated from the December pilot).**
- **H1 model.** log D has site, route and policy effects, AR(1) frame noise (ρ = 0.9), and heterogeneity of the height effect across routes (SD 0.15) and sites (SD 0.10). 2,000 simulations; one-sided α = 0.05.

| Design | True D_TH/D_TR | Naive frame t-test | Route-level paired t | **Site-level permutation (pre-registered)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 routes / 4 sites (v1 layout) | 1.00 (null) | **0.37 false positives** | 0.06 | 0.00 (4 clusters cannot reach p < 0.05) |
| 14 routes / 6 sites | 1.22 | 0.99 | 0.85 | 0.60 |
| **16 routes / 8 sites (v2)** | 1.00 (null) | 0.39 | 0.06 | 0.04 |
| **16 routes / 8 sites (v2)** | 1.22 | 1.00 | 0.91 | **0.82** |
| **16 routes / 8 sites (v2)** | 1.35 | 1.00 | 1.00 | **0.99** |

  - **Minimum detectable effect** at 0.8 power: D_TH ≈ 1.22 × D_TR, with site-level inference.
  - **Per-policy claim (S2)** for both policies, using route-level t-tests: 0.65 power at 1.22 and 0.95 at 1.35. That is why it is secondary.
- **Main lesson:** frames are not independent. The v1 analysis would have produced false positives. Clusters (sites and routes), not episodes or frames, set the power. This is why v2 uses more routes, fewer starts and 2 routes per site.
- **C1, closed loop.** The outcome scale is the SD of cell means = 0.15. Real-run noise SD is 0.10, and T_R's twin discrepancy SD is 0.10.
  - The v1 layout (8 routes × 3 starts, no retest) has a naive episode-level false-positive rate of 0.09. Its route-clustered power is 0.59 when T_H adds 0.10 SD of discrepancy.
  - The v2 layout (16 routes × 1 start × 2 repeats) has power 0.84 with a correct 0.04–0.05 false-positive rate.
  - The 95% range of SRCC(T_R) is [0.57, 0.88] (v1: [0.49, 0.92]). The two-run real mean has median reliability of about 0.84 under these assumptions. That is the ceiling used for attenuation correction.
  - C1 is still labelled confirmatory, because its variance assumptions are the least certain.
- **Pilot rule (pre-registered).** If the December pilot gives a variance ratio more than 1.5× the assumed one, the number of routes is raised within the same site budget, up to 3 routes per site. The hypotheses are never changed.

**Strongest fair baseline.**
- **Capture practice the benchmark authors use themselves:** human-height hand-held monocular phone video (Vid2Sim / EmbodiedSplat). This is T_H.
- **Fidelity metrics:** the image metrics the field uses (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) plus DINOv2 feature distance, which is the strongest cheap proxy for DINO-based policies.
- **Policy settings:** each policy runs with its authors' own deployment parameters. CityWalker uses Wanderland's fixed port (goal row, `query_every_n`-equivalent cadence); LogoNav uses `deployment/LogoNav_ros.py` [G].
- **Reliability ceiling:** the real-vs-real floor and ceiling, which are measured, not assumed.

**Measurements and analysis.**
- **Open-loop:**
  - D in metres (first 1 and first k waypoints);
  - the four image metrics per frame and per 2 m segment;
  - the real-vs-real floor.

  H1 uses the site permutation, with a mixed model (route and site random effects) reported alongside.
- **Closed-loop:**
  - trajectory lateral divergence, progress, clearance, bumper collisions and interventions;
  - SRCC per capture (Kadian form, over route × policy cells), raw and attenuation-corrected;
  - Cohen's κ for collision agreement;
  - the gap tests in C1.
- **Analysis code** is written and run on the pilot data before the freeze. Deviations from the pre-registration are reported.
