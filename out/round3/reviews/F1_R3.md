# F1 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 6.5 / F 5 / I 6 → overall 5.83

## Novelty (6.5):
The specific quantity proposed, a measured session/site/unit variance decomposition and rank-flip probability for outdoor ground-robot navigation, does not appear in what I could find. Searches for mixed-effects or ICC analyses of field navigation trials found only long-term deployments that report per-condition success (NORLAB's subarctic teach-and-repeat work, Krajník-style VT&R lighting studies). They do not report variance components. The closest neighbours are all manipulation-side evaluation-methods papers: Kress-Gazit et al. 2024, STEP (Snyder et al., RSS 2025), N-SCORE (Snyder et al., RSS 2026, continuous-metric anytime-valid tests), PhAIL 2026 and RoboChallenge (which notes run-to-run swings for the same model). So the question "do these tools survive clustering by session?" is a real and timely gap. However, the statistics are textbook (design effect, LMM, bootstrap over clusters), and the proposal itself says the ICC "depends on the stack, the metric and the site". The new thing is therefore one empirical data point on one platform class, not a general finding. A main-track reviewer would call it a new measurement in an active area, not a new question.

## Feasibility (5):
The plan is about 32 outdoor sessions of 75 min (plus travel and setup), about 770 trials, two robots built from used hoverboards in three weeks, three stacks including NoMaD on a laptop, and a new checkpoint-mat ground-truth reader that must be validated to 5 mm. That is a lot for two part-time high-school students in Nov–Feb, with short days, rain and school exams. Specific technical risks that the proposal does not address:
- LD19-class lidars (about 12 m range) on open lawn or blacktop give AMCL and SLAM Toolbox very little structure. S1 and S2 may both fail often, which makes the "continuous metric" plan collapse into success/failure.
- Midday sun interference on cheap triangulation/ToF lidars is itself a session effect. It is interesting, but it can also wreck sessions.
- Reading AprilTags from a downward camera at walking speed needs global shutter and good exposure. Global shutter is budgeted, but the mats can get wet or dirty.
- Two hobby units will differ more than two Jackals, which inflates the "unit" component.

The fallback (about 20 sessions, drop S3) is sensible and keeps a paper alive. But the stated first-data-by-Dec-20 target of about 190 trials requires everything to work on the first try.

## Impact (6):
If the result is "session ICC is large and rankings flip", the paper is uncomfortable and quotable. The evaluation-methods crowd (Kress-Gazit/TRI, Snyder/Majumdar/Itkina, PhAIL) would plausibly cite it as the first navigation-side number. The literature audit (sessions per paper, CIs) is cheap and may end up the most-cited part. The impact ceiling is set by external validity. An IROS reviewer will ask whether a hobby hoverboard with an LD19 on a schoolyard tells a Husky or Spot lab anything about its own ICC, and will suspect a large ICC reflects platform flakiness rather than "outdoor navigation". In the "small ICC" branch the paper is a modest calibration note. Field-robotics practice ("repeat on several days") is already folk wisdom, so changing behaviour needs a striking number on hardware people recognise. CASE fit is weak (it is not automation science). IROS fits as an evaluation/dataset paper, and an ICRA/RSS evaluation workshop is the most likely realistic home. My read is a strong workshop or borderline main-track paper with reuse value (protocol plus dataset), not a likely IROS main-track paper.

## Main criticism:
The headline number does not transfer. A session ICC measured on two hobby hoverboard robots with 12 m lidars on 50–70 m schoolyard routes is easy for the target audience to dismiss as platform-specific. The proposal concedes that no theory links it to other stacks and sites, and it has no plan to show the number generalizes beyond the team's own rig.

## What would raise the score:
1. Get one externally recognisable data point. Ask a lab (Elkaim, or Kitts at SCU) to run the identical protocol for 4–6 sessions on their own robot. Even a small second platform turns "our robot's ICC" into "ICCs across platforms".
2. Reanalyse existing multi-session public data to show the problem exists outside your rig. Candidates: the visual-navigation-model evaluation dataset of Guerrier et al. 2026, which the authors say they will release; NORLAB long-term teach-and-repeat logs; and Earth Rover Challenge logs, if session-tagged. This adds generality at near-zero hardware cost.
3. Replace STEP as the "recommended practice" baseline with N-SCORE (Snyder et al., RSS 2026), which handles continuous metrics, and test both under clustering. That is the strongest fair baseline for continuous metrics.
4. Cut scope: 2 stacks (S1, S3, i.e. lidar vs camera, where a session effect is most plausible) × 1 unit for the main study. Use the second unit only in a short unit-effect sub-study. Aim for about 20 sessions with longer per-session blocks.
5. Pre-register the analysis and the decision rule for "practice should change" (e.g. the minimum number of sessions for a given minimum detectable effect), so both outcomes give a concrete recommendation.
6. Make the literature audit a first-class contribution (widen to 100+ papers, include RA-L/Field Robotics/T-FR), because it carries impact regardless of the measured ICC.

## Closest prior work found:
- [Kress-Gazit et al. 2024, Robot Learning as an Empirical Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09491): evaluation best practices for manipulation; no session/site decomposition.
- [Snyder et al., STEP, RSS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10966): sequential binary-outcome policy comparison, assumes i.i.d. trials.
- [Snyder et al., N-SCORE, "Beyond Binary Success", RSS 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13616): anytime-valid sequential comparison for continuous/partial-credit metrics; the proposal misses this and it is the correct baseline for its continuous metrics.
- [PhAIL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29710): distributional time-to-success methodology, single fixed Franka cell, no session factor.
- [RoboChallenge (2510.17950)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17950): reports large run-to-run variation in real-robot manipulation evaluation, attributed largely to tester/reset; qualitative motivation for clustering, manipulation only.
