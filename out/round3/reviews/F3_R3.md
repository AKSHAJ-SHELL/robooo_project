# F3 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 5.5 / I 6.5 → overall 5.83

## Novelty (5.5): 
Asking whether simulation results predict real-world failures has been studied more than the proposal admits. Fremont et al. (ITSC 2020) and Stocco et al. ("Mind the Gap", TSE 2023) are cited. Two close papers are not. First, Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity" (RA-L 2020). They ran a physical robot against Habitat, defined a sim-vs-real correlation coefficient (SRCC) and found it was 0.18 for success. They then tuned simulator parameters to raise SRCC to 0.844 (search-result snippet). That is already the proposal's "non-obvious insight": fix the real outcomes, then repair predictivity by changing simulator parameters. Second, Sotiropoulos et al., "Can robot navigation bugs be found in simulation?" (QRS 2017). They ran simulation and field tests side by side on a navigation robot and found that most field failures showed up in low-fidelity simulation, plus some sim-only failures (snippet). The ANYmal/Surrealist industrial study (ASE 2025) also reports that search-generated sim failures predicted real navigation failures. What remains new is narrow: calibrating STL robustness ρ against failure probability for a Nav2 lidar stack, with repeated trials per scenario and a component-by-component fidelity ablation. Formal-methods reviewers would call that "known method, new setting, more careful statistics." It is incremental, not a new question.

## Feasibility (5.5): 
- **Toolchain.** I read the current Scenic repo (HEAD dated Sep 2026). It has a generic Webots supervisor interface and a "vacuum" robot example, but no ROS, ROS 2 or Nav2 interface anywhere under `src/scenic/simulators`. Scenic's ROS/Gazebo integration is only described as "being interfaced" (RoSCon 2024 talk). So "Scenic → Webots → webots_ros2 Nav2 → VerifAI loop" is a new integration the students would build themselves: a Scenic supervisor that spawns obstacles, a winch-trolley behaviour, and resetting and relaunching Nav2 for each run across 5,000 runs. That is weeks of work, not a "confirm in week 1" step.
- **Localization on the course.** AMCL with an LD19 on an open 15 × 10 m blacktop has few lidar features unless there is a fence or wall. Localization jumps could dominate both sim and real failures and swamp the effect being measured.
- **Base robot.** It is not yet built: a hoverboard conversion plus bumper plus winch all need to work by about Nov 22, before any data exists.
- **Sample size and timeline.** 300 trials with careful re-placement is realistic. But only 60 scenarios, one site and one robot, with first data on Dec 20 at the earliest, leaves no slack if the integration slips. The fallback (Scenario Execution plus Gazebo, ρ computed offline) is sensible, but it drops the "Fremont's own pipeline" hook.

## Impact (6.5): 
If the integration works, the output is a paired sim/real dataset: 60 scenarios × 5 real repeats, scored under several simulator variants. The formal-methods-for-robotics community (Scenic and VerifAI users, RoboVAST and Scenario Execution users) would find this useful. Very few public datasets attach real repeat outcomes to falsification-selected scenarios. The "falsification vs. random yield per physical trial" number is the result most likely to be cited.

The ceiling is limited because the result comes from one robot, one paved lot, static boxes plus one cart and a frozen Nav2 config. IROS reviewers will ask whether the calibration curve or the ablation ranking carries over to any other stack, and the design cannot answer that. Most likely outcome: a solid CASE paper or a strong IROS/ICRA workshop paper (e.g. a sim-to-real or formal-methods-in-robotics workshop). An IROS main-track paper is possible but less likely. A software-testing venue (ICST/ISSTA/ASE workshops, where Stocco and Sotiropoulos published) may actually fit better than IROS.

## Lab appeal: 
**Fremont.** This is the best lab hook in the brief. He co-created Scenic and VerifAI and wrote the ITSC 2020 sim-to-real paper. The Seshia/Fremont group has a stated sim-to-real interest: Seshia's VerifAI sim-real talk, and the Fremont et al. paper "Querying Labelled Data with Scenario Programs for Sim-to-Real Validation". A real-world benchmark on a ROS robot also fits their push to bring Scenic to ROS. But that push targets Gazebo, not Webots, so matching their direction would make the work more useful to them. His listed SIP 2026 project is on RL robustness, not physical testing. Expect advice on Scenic and specs, not equipment.

**Elkaim (ASL).** The fit is weaker than claimed. The match is the platform (a Pi + ROS 2 + Nav2 differential-drive robot in the SIP projects), not the research question. ASL's public publication record is mostly pre-2012 and shows no falsification or formal-methods work. "Co-advise through SIP" is speculative, and SIP 2027 runs after the paper deadline.

## Main criticism: 
The core idea has largely been done: measure whether sim results predict real ones on a physical navigation robot, then tune simulator fidelity to restore predictivity. Kadian et al. 2020 (SRCC 0.18 → 0.844 by tuning simulator parameters) did it, and Sotiropoulos et al. 2017 ran parallel sim and field tests of navigation bugs. Neither is cited. On top of that, the "Scenic + Webots + Nav2" integration the plan assumes does not exist in released Scenic code.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Cite Kadian et al. 2020, Sotiropoulos et al. 2017 and the ANYmal/Surrealist ASE 2025 study. Restate the contribution as what they lack: ρ-to-probability calibration with real repeat noise, and falsification-selected (not random) scenarios.
2. Make the headline result **falsification yield vs. random sampling per physical trial**, with enough random-arm scenarios (more than 20 × 3) to power that comparison. This is what practitioners and the Scenic group would cite.
3. Spend October building the minimal Scenic-to-Nav2 loop and report runs per hour. Choose Gazebo through Scenario Execution or RoboVAST if Webots is not working by Oct 31. This also matches the Scenic team's ROS/Gazebo direction, which strengthens the Fremont hook.
4. Add a second stack configuration (e.g. DWB vs. MPPI, or two Nav2 parameter sets) on the same scenarios. That tests whether the fidelity ranking generalizes, which is the main IROS objection.
5. Guarantee lidar features for AMCL: run along a fence or wall, or add fixed vertical boards. Report localization-caused failures separately.
6. Release the paired sim/real dataset in RoboVAST or Scenario Execution format so others can score their own simulators against it.

## Closest prior work found: 
- Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity: Does Evaluation in Simulation Predict Real-World Performance?", RA-L 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321. A physical robot against Habitat; defines the SRCC metric; raises SRCC from 0.18 to 0.844 by tuning simulator parameters. This is the closest match to F3's fidelity-repair idea.
- Sotiropoulos, Waeselynck, Guiochet, Ingrand, "Can robot navigation bugs be found in simulation? An exploratory study", QRS 2017 — https://homepages.laas.fr/felix/drupal/node/38. Parallel sim and field testing of a navigation robot; most field failures appeared in low-fidelity sim (search-result snippet).
- Fremont et al., ITSC 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07739. The same Scenic → VerifAI → track pipeline on a full-size AV.
- Stocco, Pulfer, Tonella, "Mind the Gap!", TSE 2023 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.11255. Virtual vs. physical failure transfer on a Donkey Car.
- "Bridging Research and Practice in Simulation-based Testing of Industrial Robot Navigation Systems" (ASE 2025 industry track) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09396. Search-based sim test generation for ANYmal; engineers reported that sim failures predicted real navigation failures (snippet).
- RoboVAST (2026) — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06248. Large-scale scenario variation for TurtleBot 4 + Nav2 in sim with obstacle and sensor-noise variation: 5,480 scenarios and over 100k runs, sim only. It is a natural host for F3's real-outcome data.
- Scenic for ROS (RoSCon 2024) — https://roscon.ros.org/2024/talks/Scenic_for_ROS_-_A_Probabilistic_Programming_for_World_Modeling_and_Data_Generation_for_Robotics.pdf. Shows the Scenic team targets Gazebo + ROS.
- Webots Robotino + Nav2 sim-vs-real comparison (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09441. Reports close sim/real agreement in execution time for Nav2 runs.
