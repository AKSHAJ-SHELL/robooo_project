# D2 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 6 / F 6 / I 5 → overall 5.67

Search note: about 15 web searches. Most paper and lab sites (OpenAlex, robotics.ucmerced.edu, sites.google.com) were blocked by egress, so the claims below come from search-result snippets unless I say otherwise.

## Novelty (6):
I found no public dataset or paper that relates per-cart service time, re-grabs or post-dump displacement to cart pose (distance, yaw, spacing). In that narrow sense the question is open, and the "is it spacing/stop decisions rather than curb distance" angle is a real hypothesis. But the proposal missed the closest academic work. "Influence of the Municipal Solid Waste Collection System on the Time Spent at a Collection Point: A Case Study" (Sustainability 11(22):6481, 2019) measured time at the collection point across six collection systems with different trucks and bin designs. That is the direct ancestor of this study, closer than Everett 1996. Also, OEM patents (e.g. Oshkosh "Refuse vehicle control system", US11521385 family) already define an "aligned zone" in which the arm can engage a container. So the reach part of the tolerance region is known to manufacturers and partly derivable from geometry. That leaves only the driver and stopping-behaviour component uncertain. To an IROS/CASE reviewer this reads as a time-and-motion study with a lidar counter, not a new robotics finding. It is a known method in a new setting with a new covariate, which puts it at 5-6, not 7.5.

## Feasibility (6):
The hardware is easy (2D lidar + Pi), and lidar footprint fitting of a cart from a yard at 40 cm height is realistic. Three statistical problems make the study weaker than the sizing suggests:
- **Few clusters.** There are about 7-9 truck-driver clusters, so the random effect is estimated from about 8 levels. The held-out "site" may share a hauler or even a driver with the training sites.
- **Narrow natural variation.** Most carts in compliant neighbourhoods sit in a small region of pose space. The n≈1,300 observational arm therefore mostly samples the region where nothing happens. The GAM's thresholds would come from the 150 designed events, which the proposal itself says resolve only about 1.7 s effects.
- **Behavioural confounds.** Drivers will notice repeated oddly placed carts on the same block (Hawthorne effect / adaptation). Extreme designed poses may simply make the driver get out or skip the cart, which turns a continuous outcome into rare binary ones.

Recruiting 3-4 households plus neighbour consent by end of October, while running only one collection day per site per week, is a real schedule risk. The IRB question about drivers is not trivial for a high-school team either. The Dec 20 target (about 300 events) is plausible. Reaching a defensible tolerance curve by Feb 14 is not certain.

## Impact (5):
Audience check:
- **Haulers.** They already have far better data than this rig. ASL telematics timestamp every lift from hydraulic-pressure or arm micro-switch sensors with GPS, and many fleets run 360° camera systems (e.g. 3rd Eye ASL documentation). A 9-driver, 3-street study will not change a hauler's or city's rule, which the hauler can audit from its own logs.
- **OEMs (CartSeeker).** They own their recognition envelope and won't benchmark against a student dataset.
- **Cities.** They might cite it, but placement rules exist mainly for driver convenience and liability (mailboxes, cars), not seconds.
- **Bin-robot designers.** This is the most real user: "how accurate must I park the cart" is a useful spec. But that audience is a handful of startups and hobbyists, the bin-robot-specific impact ceiling the brief warns about.

Venue fit: CASE could accept it as an application/data paper on service automation. IROS would not. Most likely outcome: a CASE short paper or workshop paper with modest citations. A later *Waste Management* paper would fit the content better but is outside the brief's venues.

## Lab appeal:
- **Carpin.** The proposal's link is weak to moderate. The CASE 2025 GNN-MCTS stochastic orienteering paper with Zuzuárregui is real (CASE 2025, pp. 2606-2613). The CASE 2018 Best Paper (vineyard multi-robot routing) is also real. But his stochastic models are about travel cost in vineyards. His closer work is the stochastic *vertex*-cost aisle graph (RA-L 2021 task planning), but a few hundred trash-cart service times on three streets is not a benchmark his group needs. He would probably give advice, not co-author.
- **Wu.** The fit is a stretch. The CACRE 2021 parking paper is a 1/20-scale Jetson car running hybrid A*, not a pose-tolerance study. Her NSF RINGS cooperative-perception project is about joint sensing, communication and edge AI (listed 2022-2025). A single 2D-lidar curb logger gives her students little.

## Main criticism:
The study's value depends on being the only source of pose-versus-service-time data. But haulers and OEMs already log every lift (and often video). The student rig can only reach about 8 drivers on 3 streets, mostly sampling compliant poses. The result is too thin to change rules and too non-robotic to excite CASE/IROS reviewers or the named labs.

## What would raise the score:
1. Get a hauler or city partner, e.g. a Bay Area or Santa Cruz hauler, to share anonymised lift-arm timestamps and GPS for a route. Use the yard lidar stations only to supply the pose covariate for carts in view. Many more drivers at almost no cost turns this into data nobody else has published.
2. Cite and position against the Sustainability 2019 collection-point timing study and the OEM "aligned zone" patents. Then state explicitly which part (driver/stop strategy) geometry cannot settle.
3. Make the robotics contribution explicit. For example, close the loop: the bin robot places carts at a chosen pose and the study measures the service outcome. That gives the "required placement accuracy versus sensing stack" design rule as the headline.
4. Drop the Wu pairing, or replace it with a lab whose current work uses roadside or infrastructure sensing. Give Carpin a concrete deliverable, e.g. a released stochastic service-time model plugged into one of his orienteering or aisle-graph benchmarks.
5. Pre-register the designed arm around the one sharp hypothesis (spacing/pairing changes the number of truck stops) and power the study for that, rather than a three-factor L9.

## Closest prior work found:
- [Sustainability 11(22):6481, 2019, "Influence of the MSW Collection System on the Time Spent at a Collection Point"](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/11/22/6481/html): measured time per collection point across six truck/bin systems. This is the direct precedent, and it does not condition on cart pose. Missing from the proposal.
- [Everett et al. 1996, Curbside Collection of Yard Waste (TRID)](https://trid.trb.org/View/458193): route-time model with constant stop time, used as the proposal's baseline.
- [Oshkosh "Refuse vehicle control system" US11521385](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11521385): an object-detection "aligned zone" where the arm can engage the cart, i.e. a manufacturer-defined reach tolerance.
- [Refuse collection vehicle controls US11332307](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11332307): auto-positions the arm from the detected container position.
- [Fleet telematics description](https://fleetrabbit.com/industry/waste-management-software/automated-side-loader-gps-tracking-software-cut-fuel-and-miles) (low authority) and [3rd Eye ASL docs](https://esg.helpjuice.com/3rd-eye-support-docs_061725/asl): per-lift timestamps from arm sensors plus cameras already exist privately at haulers.
- [McNeilus CartSeeker](https://mcneilusgarbagetrucks.com/cartseeker): vendor claims without public pose data (as the proposal says).
- [DeKalb County ASL FAQ](https://www.dekalbcountyga.gov/sites/default/files/users/user291/ASL%20-%20FAQ%20-%20FINAL.pdf) / [Lawrence KS handout](https://assets.lawrenceks.org/agendas/cc/2011/01-11-11h/study_session_equipment_info_handout.pdf): 8-18 s dump cycle per cart; 800-1,200 homes per route-day.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- **Wu fit overstated.** The CACRE 2021 paper ([SJSU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/faculty_rsca/2797)) is a 1/20-scale parking car using hybrid A*, not a "pose-tolerance docking problem". The NSF cooperative-perception project (RINGS #2148353) is about sensing, communication and edge AI, listed as 2022-2025. Describing it as "roadside sensing" is unverified.
- **Novelty claim incomplete.** "Nobody has published which rule terms actually cost time" holds only for pose terms. Collection-point timing by system design has been published (Sustainability 2019), and haulers hold per-lift telematics privately.
- **Cycle time.** The 8-12 s figure comes from a low-authority blog. Municipal sources (DeKalb) give 8-18 s. This is minor, but the range is wider than stated.
- **Checks that held.** The Carpin CASE 2025 citation and the CASE 2018 Best Paper both check out. The Amarillo refusal policy also checks out (a 2,400-home pilot; 3 ft clearance).
