# E1.v2 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 6.5 / F 6.5 / I 6.0 → overall 6.33

## Novelty (6.5): 
The combination is new: a controlled capture-height factor (1.4 m vs 0.3 m phone capture), evaluation at a real 30 cm camera, fidelity measured in policy-output space, and a real-vs-real floor. I found nothing that runs exactly this. Each piece has close precedent, though. Kadian et al. defined SRCC. Lindström et al. tied image metrics to downstream real-to-sim gaps. PolaRiS reports Pearson r and MMRV between sim and real. Cam2Sim does behaviour-space real-vs-GS comparison. Wanderland ([GitHub](https://github.com/ai4ce/wanderland), CVPR 2026 Highlight) already argues that video-3DGS twins make navigation evaluation unreliable. R2S-EGO ([arXiv 2608.06827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06827), snippet only) names the exact mechanism: "sparse human capture can leave behaviour-scoped robot views under-supported". Its own policy-level evidence is also real-robot (real-G1 success). H1's direction is close to predictable from novel-view-synthesis basics. A twin trained on frames taken along the robot's own 0.3 m path will render those poses better than a 1.4 m twin will. Mode-GS ([arXiv 2410.04646](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04646v1), snippet) already documents ground-view splat drift. So the open part is narrower than the headline suggests: do frozen-DINO and small-CNN policies care, and do image metrics track that? This reads as a new result in an active area, closer to 6-7 than to 8.

## Feasibility (6.5): 
The v2 restructuring is sound. The primary result needs only teleop drives plus open-loop inference. The October phone-only floor and registration check comes before any money is spent. The site-level permutation is the right unit of inference, and the cluster-aware power simulation is a real improvement. What remains is heavy for two part-time students:
- a new robot built in 4 weeks (Jetson, ROS 2, slam_toolbox, calibration, logging);
- 32 outdoor splats on free Colab T4 at reduced resolution;
- joint COLMAP registration of 0.3 m pole video with 1.4 m video and robot frames;
- a 5 cm Sim(3) acceptance threshold;
- 16 sessions of 75 minutes each at 8 sites in winter;
- a closed-loop twin replay loop on top of all that.

Reduced-resolution Colab splats may add enough artefacts to swamp the height effect, and that confound is not addressed. I could not verify LogoNav through search (no hits for "LogoNav" or `LogoNav_ros.py`). The proposal marks it [G], so I do not call it wrong, but it is the less-known of the two policies and it carries half the H1 average.

## Impact (6.0): 
Through the venue lens, an IROS main-track reviewer would see a careful but small measurement study: one robot, one region, 2 policies, 16 routes and 64 real episodes. The most likely headline is "matched-height capture renders better at that height". That is a plausible strong-workshop or CASE paper, not a practice-changing IROS paper.

The actionable output is a capture rule ("capture at deployment height"), and it does not reach the main benchmark builders:
- Vid2Sim / NavBench-GS reconstruct from web-sourced or hand-held videos ([Vid2Sim, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Xie_Vid2Sim_Realistic_and_Interactive_Simulation_from_Video_for_Urban_Navigation_CVPR_2025_paper.html)), so they cannot recapture at 30 cm.
- Wanderland has already moved to a lidar-grounded multi-sensor rig and simulates at about 1.1 m.

So the rule mainly helps the niche of small-robot builders who make their own phone twins. The more broadly citable piece is S1: do LPIPS or DINOv2 distances predict navigation-policy output divergence against a real-vs-real floor? Other benchmark authors could adopt that as a cheap fidelity check, and the paired real/twin release with robot logs at 30 cm is data nobody has. Those two pieces lift this above workshop level. The narrow robot, site and policy scope keeps it below 7.

## Lab appeal: 
The two reachable labs fit only weakly. Fremont's link is his 2020 work on sim-to-real transfer of formal scenario tests. My search found no recent Fremont work on neural or splat simulators, and a Scenic program that places a refuse cart on 4 routes is a token hook, not his research line. Elkaim's public profile is GNC, GPS, sensor fusion and UAVs, with nothing on learned visual policies or 3DGS. His appeal is SIP mentoring on a ROS 2 rig, not the scientific question.

The labs that would actually want this are NYU AI4CE (CityWalker and Wanderland: a low-camera, real-robot check of their own model and their claim about twin reliability) and VAIL-UCLA (Vid2Sim/S2E capture practice). Both are named honestly as unreachable stakeholders, so the brief's "a reachable lab should want to help" criterion is only partly met.

## Main criticism: 
The practical lesson ("capture at the robot's height") has a predictable direction. It also cannot be applied by the communities the proposal says benefit most: web-video Vid2Sim-style pipelines cannot recapture, and Wanderland has already replaced phone capture with lidar rigs. The broadly useful contribution, whether image or feature metrics predict policy-output divergence above a real-vs-real floor, is the secondary S1, and Lindström et al. partly pre-empt it for detectors. As framed, the headline audience is narrow, and the reachable labs have no active line this feeds.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Promote S1 to the headline. Frame the paper as a cheap, policy-aware twin-fidelity check, using "one teleop drive gives output divergence D relative to the real-vs-real floor". Show that it predicts closed-loop twin-vs-real gaps better than LPIPS or DINO distance, and make capture height the manipulated stress factor rather than the main claim. Every splat benchmark could adopt that.
2. Add a rescue condition that the main pipelines could actually use. Example: the 1.4 m capture plus generative or diffusion novel-view repair, or Mode-GS-style depth anchoring, rendered at 0.3 m. Then the result says whether existing human-height or web-video twins can be fixed, not just "recapture".
3. Run one policy at Wanderland's camera height as well (a 1.1 m mast on the same robot, open-loop only). This turns the study into a height × capture interaction that maps directly onto Wanderland/NavBench settings and gives AI4CE and VAIL a reason to engage.
4. Before submission, read the Wanderland paper and arXiv 2610.00731, and contact AI4CE early (October, not January) with the floor-check plot. A co-signed parity check of the CityWalker port is the cheapest route to a real collaborator.
5. Give Fremont a substantive role: Scenic/VerifAI falsification run *in the validated twins*, with real re-runs of the falsified cases. That is his actual research question (does sim falsification transfer?). Moving one cart is not.
6. Control for the reduced-resolution Colab training artefact. Train at least the pilot splats at full resolution, so that the height effect is not confounded with compute limits.

## Closest prior work found: 
- [Wanderland, CVPR 2026 (arXiv 2511.20620)](https://github.com/ai4ce/wanderland): argues video-3DGS twins make navigation evaluation unreliable; multi-sensor capture; sim camera about 1.1 m. Snippets do not show real-robot SRCC; paper PDF blocked for me too.
- [Vid2Sim, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Xie_Vid2Sim_Realistic_and_Interactive_Simulation_from_Video_for_Urban_Navigation_CVPR_2025_paper.html): monocular and web-video urban twins, with a real-world sim-to-real gain. This is the capture practice being tested.
- [R2S-EGO, arXiv 2608.06827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06827) (snippet): names under-support of robot-view poses from sparse human capture and offers a refinement fix, with real-G1 policy evidence.
- [PolaRiS, arXiv 2512.16881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16881) (snippet): real-to-sim splat policy evaluation with Pearson r and MMRV (manipulation).
- [Mode-GS, arXiv 2410.04646](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04646v1) (snippet): splat drift in ground-robot trajectory views.
- Kadian et al. 2020 (SRCC) and Lindström et al. CVPRW 2024 (image metrics vs downstream gap): adopted or cited correctly in the proposal; not re-checked beyond snippets.
- [RAD, arXiv 2502.13144](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13144v2) and DecoupleGS (snippets): 3DGS closed-loop evaluation and training for driving policies. These are the driving-side neural-simulator line that the proposal still owes a search on.
