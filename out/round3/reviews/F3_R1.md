# F3 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 5.5 / F 5.5 / I 6 → overall 5.67

## Novelty (5.5):
The core question, "does simulation predict real-world outcomes, and can tuning the simulator make it predict better?", has been asked and answered with physical trials before. The proposal misses the closest one. Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity" (IEEE RA-L 2020), ran matched sim and real LoCoBot navigation episodes and defined a sim-vs-real correlation coefficient (SRCC). They then tuned simulator parameters against the fixed real outcomes and raised SRCC from 0.18 to 0.844 (from search snippets). That is the proposal's "non-obvious insight" (use fixed real outcomes to score many simulator variants) already carried out. Biagiola, Stocco, Riccio and Tonella, "Two is Better Than One: Digital Siblings" (EMSE 2024), already treats simulator output as a predictor of failure probability against a physical-car digital twin. Gervet et al. (Science Robotics 2023) already showed that modular navigation transfers sim-to-real much better than end-to-end, and that sim and real error modes differ in sensor-specific ways. That pre-empts the "modular may behave better than Mind-the-Gap's DNN" angle. Fremont's own group (Querying Labeled Time Series Data with Scenario Programs, NFM 2025, arXiv 2511.10627) works on whether Scenic sim failures reproduce on real data. What remains new is narrow: robustness (ρ) from STL falsification mapped to P(real failure) for a lidar Nav2 stack, plus a falsification-vs-random yield comparison on hardware. That is a known method in a new setting, which is incremental.

## Feasibility (5.5):
- **Toolchain.** Scenic's Webots interface is real. I read `src/scenic/simulators/webots/simulator.py` in the Scenic repo: it runs inside a Webots supervisor controller, and there is a `vacuum` ground-robot example. Coupling it with webots_ros2's own robot driver, resetting Nav2/AMCL between 5,000 runs and feeding ρ back to VerifAI is unproven glue code. The "free Colab runs Webots headless with ROS 2" assumption is optimistic. At realistic Nav2 sim speeds, 5,000 runs is tens of laptop-hours.
- **Course.** The test site is a bigger problem. AMCL on a 2D LD19 map of an open 15 × 10 m blacktop has almost no static structure to localize against, and a chain-link fence gives sparse returns. Localization failure would likely dominate the real outcomes, and swamp the obstacle-interaction effects being studied.
- **Placement.** Reproducing sampled continuous poses to ±1 cm/±2° across 300 trials, plus a winch timed to ≤0.1 s, is doable but slow for two part-time students.
- **Statistics.** I recompute the Hanley–McNeil SE at about 0.074 (18 positives, 42 negatives, AUC 0.75), not 0.065. Its 95% CI of roughly ±0.15 cannot separate 0.75 from 0.85.
- **Ablation power.** The fidelity-ablation ranking compares correlated AUCs of maybe 5 sim variants on the same 60 scenarios. The differences will be about 0.03–0.08 and mostly noise. The headline "ranked list of sim components" is the least supported claim.

## Impact (6):
A clean, released dataset of 60 scenarios × 5 real repeats with sim ρ would be a useful data point for the scenario-based-testing community (Scenic/VerifAI, Scenario Execution, RoboVAST users). But one robot on one paved lot, with scenarios built only from boxes and cones, gives a single calibration curve with little external validity. The extremes-stratified design (20 clear counterexamples, 20 clearly safe) inflates AUC through spectrum bias. The resulting "calibration" will not reflect how falsification is used in practice. Realistically this is a CASE paper, or a strong ICST/SBFT-style workshop paper. It is not an IROS-main sim-to-real paper, given that RA-L 2020 already set the SRCC paradigm.

## Lab appeal:
Fremont is the right person. A physical test of Scenic/VerifAI on a new robot class costs him little and gives his group an external validation, so he would plausibly advise. ## Main criticism:
The distinguishing contribution, "score many simulator variants against fixed real outcomes to find which fidelity knobs restore predictivity", was already done by Kadian et al. (RA-L 2020), which the proposal does not cite. What remains (ρ calibration for one Nav2 robot on one lot) is underpowered for ranking fidelity components, and is confounded by poor AMCL localization on an open blacktop.

## What would raise the score:
1. Cite and position against Kadian et al. 2020 (SRCC), Biagiola et al. 2024 (digital siblings) and Gervet et al. 2023. Use SRCC/failure-probability prediction as the metric, and state precisely what falsification-ρ adds.
2. Make the question one those works cannot answer. Example: does falsification find real failures more efficiently than random sampling per physical trial (yield), measured with a pre-registered power analysis on yield rather than AUC?
3. Sample physical scenarios from the natural (falsifier-proposal) distribution, or report calibration with spectrum-bias correction. Do not use extremes-only strata.
4. Move to a structured indoor or walled course (gym, hallway, parking garage level). Otherwise use a localization-independent setup (motion capture via overhead AprilTag camera, or odometry-only short courses), so that localization noise does not dominate failures.
5. Cut the fidelity ablation to 2 pre-specified mechanisms (lidar dropout on dark/thin objects; wheel slip). Test each with a paired DeLong/bootstrap test and a stated minimum detectable ΔAUC.
6. Build the Scenic–Webots–Nav2 loop in the first two weeks and publish it as a reusable tool. That alone has reuse value.

## Closest prior work found:
- [Kadian et al., Sim2Real Predictivity, RA-L 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321v2): matched sim and real LoCoBot navigation, SRCC metric, simulator parameters tuned to raise predictivity from 0.18 to 0.844. Overlaps the core insight directly.
- [Biagiola, Stocco, Riccio, Tonella, Two is Better Than One: Digital Siblings, EMSE 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08060): multi-simulator ensemble predicting failure probability of a physical-car digital twin. Overlaps the idea of using simulator output to predict failure probability.
- [Stocco, Pulfer, Tonella, Mind the Gap!, IEEE TSE 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.11255): virtual vs physical failure exposure on Donkey Car. Cited correctly.
- [Fremont et al., ITSC 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.07739): Scenic → VerifAI → track, with sim-vs-real trace comparison (the Skorokhod metric is acknowledged). I could not open the full text to check the test-case count.
- [Gervet et al., Navigating to Objects in the Real World, Sci. Robotics 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00922): modular vs end-to-end sim-to-real. Sim and real error modes differ, and modular transfers better.
- [Querying Labeled Time Series Data with Scenario Programs (Berkeley/Korea U./Chalmers/UCSC, arXiv 2511.10627)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10627v1): validates Scenic sim failures against real data, from Fremont's own circle.
- [Khatiri, Panichella, Tonella, SURREAL, ICST 2023](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icst-2023/icst-2023-papers/17/Simulation-based-Test-Case-Generation-for-Unmanned-Aerial-Vehicles-in-the-Neighborhoo): sim tests anchored to real UAV flights (real-to-sim replay, then search).
- [RoboVAST, arXiv 2607.06248](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06248) and [ROSCon 2025 slides](https://roscon.ros.org/de/2025/img/slides/S2_6__Pasch_Wiest__RoboVAST.pdf): scenario-based Nav2 validation at scale (100k runs), using field-testing data. A tooling competitor for the release.
- [Sim-to-Sim-to-Real for small AVs (Duckietown)](https://duckietown.com/?p=111587): asks whether a low-fidelity sim predicts real performance.
