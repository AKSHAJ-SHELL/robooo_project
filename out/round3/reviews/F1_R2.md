# F1 — Reviewer 2 (Methodology rigor and feasibility for this team)
Scores: N 6.5 / F 4.5 / I 6 → overall 5.67

## Novelty (6.5):
I found no published measurement of session, site or unit variance components (ICC, design effect) for outdoor ground-robot navigation metrics, so the empirical quantity is plausibly new. The closest work I found evaluates across conditions without breaking the variance down. Guerrier et al. 2026 ("Can Vision Foundation Models Navigate?", arXiv 2603.25937) test 5 VNMs on 2 robots in 5 environments and report robustness to perturbations, not clustering. The Michigan Tech MUONS paper (arXiv 2509.07321, JFR 2025) separates tuning-parameter variance in 30k simulated trials and checks it in the field, but has no session factor. Kress-Gazit et al. 2024 and STEP (Snyder et al., RSS 2025) give the statistical framing for manipulation. The method is entirely standard: mixed models, ICC, bootstrap, and the "statistical precipice" critique already made in deep RL (Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021, rliable; from my own knowledge, not re-searched this session). So the novelty is a number, and as the proposal itself says, that number "depends on the stack, the metric and the site." A main-track reviewer will ask why one California team's ICC, from one hobby platform in one winter, should transfer to anyone else's robot. That keeps this at "new result in an active area" with a real ceiling.

## Feasibility (4.5):
**Identifiability.** With 2 sites and 2 units, the site and unit "variance components" cannot be estimated. A variance needs many levels. With two levels you can only estimate a fixed difference with a CI. The headline claim to "put a number on build-to-build variance" with n = 2 units, both built by the same two students, is therefore unsupported. Site is also confounded with whatever else differs between the two locations.

**The headline ICC is the wrong quantity for the ranking-flip question.** Stacks are interleaved within each session, so the session main effect cancels in every within-session comparison. Only the session × stack interaction can flip a ranking. The "20 trials carry the information of 4" design-effect argument applies to absolute levels, not to paired stack comparisons. The power analysis is done on the main-effect ICC (I reproduced the Fisher SE: 0.070 at k = 28 and 0.083 at k = 20, so that arithmetic is right). The interaction variance is much less precise. In my quick simulation (28 sessions, 8 trials per stack per session, interaction variance about 0.07 of the residual), the 95% range of the moment estimate runs from 0 to about 0.18, and about 9% of replications estimate it as exactly zero. The rank-flip bootstrap over 28 sessions inherits that imprecision.

**Confounds that inflate "session" variance:**
- If the checkpoint mats are re-laid each session, their placement error relative to the map frame becomes a session-level error in gate deviation. This could produce a large ICC for gate error that has nothing to do with navigation. The mats need surveyed permanent reference marks, with placement error measured every session.
- Monotonic drift over Nov–Feb: leaf fall, grass growth, wet ground and map/topomap staleness, plus tire wear and operator practice. These are trends, not day-to-day noise, and the model needs a calendar-trend term to separate them.
- The LD19 is rated to resist only 30,000 lux (Waveshare/DFRobot spec), while direct sun is much brighter. S1/S2 may degrade at noon for sensor reasons, not stack reasons. That is still a session effect, but it should be logged and modelled.
- Weekday sessions can only run on short late afternoons, so time of day will be confounded with weekday versus weekend and with calendar date.

**Workload is underestimated.** A 60 m route at Nav2 speeds of 0.3–0.5 m/s, plus driving back, interventions and S3 failures, is about 4–5 min per trial. That makes 24 trials closer to 2 h than 75 min, before setting up five mats, weather sensors and localization. At 32 sessions × ~3 h with both students present, that is roughly 100 h of joint field time in winter (sunset around 5 pm). Dec 1–20 has only six weekend days for the eight planned sessions.

**S3 is not ready as specified:**
- The build list has one Pi Camera Module 3 and one onboard laptop, so S3 can run on only one unit. That breaks the 2 units × 3 stacks crossing.
- The released visualnav-transformer deployment code is ROS 1 Noetic for LoCoBot/Jetson Orin Nano and recommends a wide-angle (fisheye) camera, per its README, which I checked. A standard-FOV Camera Module 3 is out of spec, and the code has to be ported to ROS 2.

**Budget.** The Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB was reported at $125 (Feb 2026) and then $175 (April 2026), not $95. Two Pis add about $160, which puts the build over $1,000.

**What works.** The fallback (drop S3, fewer sessions) keeps a publishable core. Continuous metrics, frozen stacks and randomized interleaving are good design choices.

## Impact (6):
If the session × stack interaction turns out large and well estimated, the paper is a useful caution that would get cited in evaluation-methods discussions. The literature audit of how many sessions published outdoor-navigation papers use is low-risk and reusable on its own terms. But three things cap the impact:
- The measured ICC is specific to a hoverboard + LD19 + Pi platform, two California sites and one winter.
- The site and unit components cannot be estimated.
- The most likely outcome is a small, imprecise interaction variance with a CI that crosses both "negligible" and "matters", and that is hard to sell at IROS.

Realistically this is a strong workshop paper, or a CASE/RA-L-style short methods paper with a dataset.

## Main criticism:
The design cannot deliver its headline claims as written. It powers the session main-effect ICC, which cancels in interleaved stack comparisons, while the quantity that drives rank flips (session × stack) stays poorly estimated. The site and unit "variance components" rest on two levels each. On top of that comes a field workload (32 sessions × ~3 h, both students, winter daylight) that the timeline underestimates by about 2x.

## What would raise the score:
1. Make the session × stack interaction the primary estimand. Power the study for it by simulating the full mixed model and the rank-flip bootstrap. Report the expected CI width before committing.
2. Drop the claims about the site and unit random effects. Treat site and unit as fixed. Or reallocate: one robot unit, more sessions, or more sites with fewer sessions each, since only many sites give a site variance.
3. Install permanent surveyed reference marks for the mats and measure mat placement error every session, so ground-truth error is not counted as session variance.
4. Add a calendar-trend term and a pre-registered re-mapping policy, so seasonal drift is separated from day-to-day variance.
5. Cut S3 from the main design (it is ROS 1, needs a wide-angle camera, and runs on one unit), or budget a fisheye camera plus a ROS 2 port and pilot it before Nov 29.
6. Re-time a pilot session honestly: trials, resets and setup. Size the session count from that measured time and the actual weekend and break calendar.
7. Fix the budget: Pi 5 8 GB prices, or switch to 4 GB boards or reuse the laptop.
8. Lean on the literature audit plus a re-analysis of public multi-session navigation logs, if any exist, to widen generality beyond one platform.

## Closest prior work found:

- [Snyder et al., STEP, RSS 2025 (arXiv 2503.10966)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.10966): sequential policy comparison assuming i.i.d. trials; this is the actual STEP source.

- [Guerrier et al. 2026, Can Vision Foundation Models Navigate? (arXiv 2603.25937)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25937): 5 VNMs, 2 robots, 5 environments; multi-condition but no variance components.

- Agarwal et al. 2021, "Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice" (rliable): the same few-runs and ranking-reliability critique in RL (from memory; not re-searched this session).
