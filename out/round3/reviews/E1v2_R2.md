## Novelty (6.5): 
The specific cell this targets looks empty in what I could find. No search turned up a study that varies splat capture height and measures a navigation policy's output divergence against a real robot at 0.3 m with a real-vs-real floor. My searches for capture-height or viewpoint-mismatch splat evaluation returned only view-generalization training papers (GenSplat, Mobi-pi, SplatSearch) [S]. The proposal positions itself correctly against Wanderland, Kadian SRCC, Lindström et al. and PolaRiS. I confirmed Wanderland's 1.2 m pose-generator default and the 0.9 m camera offset in the code (`wanderland/navigation/generate_cam_poses.py`, `wanderland-lab/src/wanderland_lab/envs/scene.py`). The weakness is that the one confirmatory hypothesis (H1: D_TH > D_TR) is close to derivable from what is already known about novel-view synthesis. The T_R pole walk follows the robot path at the robot's height, so T_R renders are essentially interpolation, while T_H renders require a 1.1 m downward extrapolation onto grazing-angle pavement. The only way H1 fails is encoder invariance, and LogoNav (a small CNN at 96×96) is unlikely to be fully invariant. The open questions are the magnitude of the effect and S1 (whether LPIPS or DINOv2 distance track D). Those deserve to be primary, but S1 is pre-registered only as estimation. My exact-title search for arXiv 2610.00731 did not find it either, so a close factor study may exist that neither of us has read.

## Feasibility (6): 
**Hardware and code check out.** I read the repos myself:
- LogoNav's `deployment/LogoNav_ros.py` uses `metric_waypoint_spacing = 0.25`, an EfficientNet-B0 encoder at 96×96, a 5-frame temporal context, and runs in `model.eval()` regression mode.
- CityWalker's `config/finetune.yaml` uses a frozen `dinov2_vitb14`, a deterministic `attention` decoder (the diffusion decoder is not the configured one) and `target_fps: 1`. The "deterministic policies" claim therefore holds.
- wanderland-lab does need Isaac Sim and a GPU with at least 24 GB, so not running it is correct.
- A Jetson Orin Nano Super is a sensible swap for a Pi 5 (DINOv2-B at 1 Hz with context is impractical on a Pi CPU), and $787 fits the budget.

**The power simulation.** I re-ran it and it reproduces the quoted numbers (site-permutation power 0.82 at a 1.22 ratio, 0.04 false positives under the null, naive frame-test false positives 0.37–0.39). The structure is correct: AR(1) frames nested in routes and sites, with an exact sign-flip test over 8 sites. But the result rests on the assumed heterogeneity of the height effect (SD 0.15 across routes, 0.10 across sites). In my sensitivity runs (16 routes / 8 sites, 1,000 simulations):
- With heterogeneity doubled (0.30 / 0.20), power at a 1.22 ratio falls to 0.43, and to 0.77 at 1.35.
- At 0.50 / 0.30 it is 0.22 and 0.46.
- Losing two sites (12 routes / 6 sites) drops power at 1.22 to 0.55.

Route-to-route variation in outdoor splat quality is plausibly that large. The pilot rule (add routes within the same 8 sites) does little against site-level heterogeneity, which is what the site permutation is limited by.

**Workload.** For two part-time students, the pipeline is heavy:
- 32 joint COLMAP models, each registering phone images at 1.4 m and 0.3 m together with robot frames;
- 32 splats on free Colab T4s, which are rate-limited (about 24 GPU-hours of training before COLMAP, failed runs and rendering);
- a robot built from scratch with ROS 2, slam_toolbox, calibration and a closed-loop twin-replay loop in 4 weeks (Nov 9–Dec 6);
- 8 site sessions of about 2.5 hours each;
- 11 days for analysis and writing.

The October phone-only check, the Jan 15 gate and an open-loop-only fallback are genuinely good de-risking, which is what keeps this at 6 and not lower.

## Impact (6.5): 
A clean, measured answer to "can I capture at human height and evaluate a 30 cm robot?" would be cited by the Vid2Sim / S2E / Wanderland / EmbodiedSplat line and by anyone building phone-scan twins. The paired real/twin release (synchronised logs, `episodes.json` format) is the most reusable part. The ceiling is low generality: one robot, one camera, one region, two policies. The phone-vs-robot camera domain gap (see below) may dominate D and blur the headline. If H1 comes out positive as expected, the take-home ("capture at deployment height") is useful but thin unless S1 gives a surprising answer about image metrics. Realistic outcome: a strong workshop paper or a borderline CASE/IROS main-track paper.

## Main criticism: 
The confirmatory design tests the most predictable question (H1). It also leaves uncontrolled confounds that could produce or mask the height effect:
1. **Capture order and time.** T_H is always walked first and T_R second (fixed order in the per-route session), under fast-changing early-morning light. T_R's lighting may therefore be closer to the robot drives.
2. **Camera domain gap.** Both twins are trained on phone images and compared against a different robot camera (ISP, colour, FOV, rolling shutter). This adds a shared offset to D that can swamp a 22% ratio and makes result (C), "phone twins unfit at any height", uninterpretable.
3. **Unequal captures.** Image count, path length and coverage are not equalised between T_H and T_R.
4. **Biased floor.** The real-vs-real floor allows a 10 cm / 3° pose mismatch, while twins are rendered at the exact pose. This inflates the floor and flatters both twins.
5. **CityWalker is out of distribution.** CityWalker was trained on human-height walking video, so 0.3 m real frames are OOD for it. Policy sensitivity may be erratic, which inflates both D and its route heterogeneity.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Counterbalance T_H/T_R capture order across routes (randomised, pre-registered), equalise image count and path length, and record solar time. Put capture order in the model.
2. Add a cheap camera-domain control: a third twin trained on the robot's own frames from teleop drive 1 and evaluated on drive 2's poses ("T_robot"). It costs no field time, separates the phone-vs-robot camera gap from the height effect, and gives a fair best case for interpreting outcome (C). Alternatively, use the same camera module on the pole for T_R and at 1.4 m for T_H.
3. Promote S1 (do LPIPS or DINOv2 distance track D, and does height still predict D after adjusting for LPIPS?) to a co-primary pre-registered hypothesis with a stated decision rule, because that is the result that cannot be settled on paper.
4. Re-run the power simulation over a grid of height-effect heterogeneity values (0.15–0.5), and pre-register a site-count rule (for example, 10 sites if the pilot heterogeneity is above 0.25) rather than adding routes within sites. Let T_H and T_R frame noise be correlated at the same frame; this is realistic and raises power.
5. Compute the real-vs-real floor with a tighter pose match (5 cm / 1.5°), or model floor D against pose offset and extrapolate to zero offset.
6. Cut the closed-loop twin replay (C1) from the main plan, or make it explicitly conditional on the Jan 15 gate, to free time. Budget a COLMAP/GLOMAP runtime test on Colab during October.
7. Read arXiv 2610.00731 and the Wanderland PDF before committing.

## Closest prior work found: 
- Wanderland, arXiv 2511.20620 / CVPR 2026 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20620): argues that video-3DGS twins make navigation evaluation unreliable, using multi-sensor capture with a camera about 1.1–1.2 m high. Code confirmed; no capture-height manipulation visible.
- Lindström et al., "Are NeRFs ready for autonomous driving?", CVPRW 2024 (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16092v2): real2sim gap for perception models versus image metrics; LPIPS and FID are strong indicators [S]. The same question for perception rather than navigation policies.
- PolaRiS, arXiv 2512.16881 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16881): real-to-sim policy evaluation from video scans with Pearson r and MMRV over 600 real rollouts [S]. Manipulation, no height factor.
- EmbodiedSplat, ICCV 2025 (https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/supplemental/Chhablani_EmbodiedSplat_Personalized_Real-to-Sim-to-Real_ICCV_2025_supplemental.pdf): phone-scan splats for indoor navigation with sim-real correlation [S].
- Kadian et al., Sim2Real Predictivity (arXiv 1912.06321): the SRCC statistic, adopted here.
- Mobi-pi, arXiv 2505.23692 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23692): uses splat renders from new viewpoints to place policies [S]. Only adjacent: a viewpoint change used for deployment, not for validating an evaluator.
