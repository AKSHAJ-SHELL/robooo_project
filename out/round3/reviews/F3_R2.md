# F3 — Reviewer 2 (Methodology rigor and feasibility for this team)
Scores: N 5 / F 4.5 / I 6 → overall 5.17

## Novelty (5): The core question, whether a simulator's verdicts predict real outcomes, and the proposed fix, tuning simulator components to make sim agree with fixed real outcomes, were both published by Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity" (RA-L 2020, arXiv 1912.06321). That paper defines a sim-vs-real correlation coefficient (SRCC) for navigation and tunes simulator parameters against real LoCoBot runs to raise it. The proposal does not cite it, and its "non-obvious insight" (re-score many simulator variants against the same real trials) is essentially that paper's method. The proposal also says it is "not known" whether a modular lidar-plus-planner stack transfers better than an end-to-end DNN. Lambertenghi, Flores Valdez and Stocco, "A Multi-Modality Evaluation of the Reality Gap in Autonomous Driving Systems" (ASE 2025, arXiv 2509.22379), compare modular and end-to-end ADS on a small vehicle with a real camera and LiDAR plus its digital twin, across actuation, perception and behavioral fidelity. That removes a large part of the claimed gap. Khatiri et al. (SURREAL, ICST 2023) already generate simulation tests near real PX4 flights and check them against reality. Three things are still new: falsification robustness ρ as the predictor, a Nav2 ground robot, and a scenario-level calibration curve. Together that is "known question, new tool and platform", which is incremental (5).

## Feasibility (4.5): I cloned Scenic (main branch, Sep 2026), VerifAI (3.0.0b1) and webots_ros2. Scenic's Webots interface (`src/scenic/simulators/webots/simulator.py`) runs as a supervisor controller inside Webots and contains no ROS code. Scenic's simulator list (carla, gta, isaac, lgsvl, metadrive, newtonian, webots, xplane) has no Gazebo or ROS interface. I found no public BerkeleyLearnVerify ROS repo, although a 2024 ROSCon talk says one is in progress. VerifAI's Webots path (`client_webots.py` plus example supervisors) only teleports objects. So the team would have to build the hard part themselves: a supervisor that places objects, re-initializes AMCL, clears costmaps, sends the Nav2 goal, waits, records the trace, and then resets everything for the next episode without breaking `use_sim_time`/TF. That is weeks of ROS 2 debugging for one part-time student. The "confirm in week 1" plan understates it.

Compute is also understated. 5,000 Nav2 episodes of about 60 s at a laptop real-time factor of roughly 1–2 (my estimate, not measured) is 40–80+ hours plus resets. Free Colab cannot practically host ROS 2 + Webots + Nav2 in 12-hour, non-persistent sessions.

On the physical side, the timeline requires a hoverboard base built from scratch with ros2_control, a frozen Nav2, a bumper and a winch, all before 150 trials by Dec 20. That is not credible. "2 min per trial" ignores placing 1–3 obstacles to ±1 cm/±2°, the photo check and resetting the winch, which realistically makes 5–8 min per trial, or 25–40 h on a fenced blacktop in winter.

There is also an unaddressed technical problem: AMCL on a 15×10 m open lot with a 12 m LD19 and only movable obstacles may have too few static features to localize. Localization failure would then dominate the "real failures". Finally, the 8 cm curb-stop is probably below a typical LD19 mount height, so it is invisible in both sim and reality and would give deterministic failures, not calibration signal.

## Impact (6): If the work were done well, a measured ρ → P(real failure) curve for a Nav2 stack, released with Scenic worlds and real outcomes, would be useful to the Scenic/VerifAI and scenario-based ROS testing communities. But the result carries no measure of how much it generalizes. It covers one robot, one surface, one controller and one simulator, and the calibrated-sim arm and the ablation ranking are fitted and scored on the same 60 scenarios. Several statistical claims are weaker than stated:
1. **AUC SE.** My Hanley–McNeil calculation gives an SE of about 0.071–0.074 at AUC 0.75 with 18–20 positive scenarios out of 60, not 0.065. A 95% CI of about ±0.14 does not separate 0.85 from 0.65.
2. **Spectrum bias.** Stratifying 20/20/20 by sim ρ over-samples the extremes, so the measured AUC overstates discrimination compared with the natural falsifier distribution.
3. **Yield comparison.** The random-vs-falsification arm (20 scenarios × 3 runs) is too small for any yield comparison.

(The r = 0.4, power 0.9, n ≈ 61 figure is correct.) Realistically this is a solid workshop paper or an ICST/ISSTA-style empirical paper, not likely main-track IROS.

## Lab appeal: Fremont is the right person, and a large-n real check of his own tools on a new robot class is a real gift to him. The Scenic team is reportedly building a ROS interface, so they might actively want a Nav2 user, which is the best lever in this proposal. Elkaim's ASL fit (Pi + ROS 2 + Nav2) is plausible. Borrowing an ASL robot instead of building one would help feasibility more than anything else.

## Main criticism: The proposal underestimates the engineering, and it is novel only in the platform. The team must build a missing Scenic↔Webots↔Nav2 episode-reset harness and a robot from scratch, then run about 300 carefully placed outdoor trials, all by Dec 20 to Jan 14. And the study's distinctive methodological idea (tune simulator components to maximize sim–real predictivity against fixed real runs) is already Kadian et al. 2020, which the proposal does not cite.

## What would raise the score:
1. Cite Kadian et al. 2020 (SRCC) and Lambertenghi/Stocco ASE 2025, and restate the contribution relative to them. One option is "does falsification robustness, not average success, transfer, and does falsification-guided selection beat random in real yield", powered properly.
2. Drop the from-scratch base. Borrow a TurtleBot 4 or an ASL Pi/Nav2 robot, or use the bin-robot base only if it already runs Nav2 by October. Run indoors (gym or hallway) for weather and for AMCL features, or add fixed lidar landmarks to the lot.
3. In October, build and time a working "one Scenic sample → one Nav2 episode → reset" loop with webots_ros2's TurtleBot3/Nav2 demo before ordering anything. Report the measured real-time factor and plan the sim budget from that number (likely about 1,000 runs, not 5,000). Ask the Scenic team whether their ROS/Gazebo interface is available.
4. Fix the stats. Pre-register the AUC target with a correct SE (or use a likelihood-ratio test on the ρ slope in the GLMM). Sample physical scenarios in proportion to the falsifier's ρ distribution, or report spectrum-corrected AUC. Hold out about 20 scenarios for the calibrated-sim arm and the ablation ranking. Either power the random-vs-falsification yield arm (an equal number of trials) or drop it.
5. Budget about 6 min per trial and cut to about 40 scenarios × 4 repeats if needed. Remove obstacles invisible to the 2D lidar, or analyze them separately as a known blind-spot class.

## Closest prior work found:
- [Kadian et al., Sim2Real Predictivity, RA-L 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321): measures sim-vs-real correlation for navigation and tunes simulator parameters to maximize it. Same core method; not cited.
- [Lambertenghi, Flores Valdez & Stocco, Multi-Modality Evaluation of the Reality Gap, ASE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22379): small vehicle with LiDAR and camera, modular vs end-to-end, SiL/ViL/MR/real comparison. Undercuts the "modular stack not known" claim.
- [Stocco et al., Two is Better Than One: Digital Siblings, 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08060): multi-simulator prediction of real Donkey Car failures.
- [Khatiri et al., SURREAL, ICST 2023](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icst-2023/icst-2023-papers/17/Simulation-based-Test-Case-Generation-for-Unmanned-Aerial-Vehicles-in-the-Neighborhoo): simulation test generation anchored to real UAV flights.
- [Fremont et al., ITSC 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07739) and [Stocco et al., Mind the Gap, TSE 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.11255): cited correctly in the proposal.
- [Scenic for ROS, ROSCon 2024 talk](https://roscon.ros.org/2024/talks/Scenic_for_ROS_-_A_Probabilistic_Programming_for_World_Modeling_and_Data_Generation_for_Robotics.pdf): the Scenic team's own ROS/Gazebo integration effort. Relevant both as tooling and as the lab hook.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- "Whether a modular lidar-plus-planner stack ... behaves better or worse is not known": addressed by Lambertenghi et al., ASE 2025.
- Hanley–McNeil SE of about 0.065 "separates ≥0.85 from ≤0.65": my calculation gives about 0.071–0.074, and the resulting CI does not separate those values.
- "Scenic has a Webots interface ... avoids writing a new interface": partly true. The interface exists (supervisor-only, no ROS), but the Nav2 episode and reset harness still has to be written.
- "Free Colab" for headless Webots + ROS 2 Nav2 falsification: not realistic.
- "300 trials ≈ 10 h of robot time": ignores placement and reset time.
