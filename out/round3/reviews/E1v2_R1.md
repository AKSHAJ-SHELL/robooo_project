# E1.v2 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 6.5 / F 6.5 / I 6.5 → overall 6.50

## Novelty (6.5):
The v2 positioning is far more honest than v1: it now cites Kadian SRCC, Wanderland, Cam2Sim, Lindström and PolaRiS, and claims only (i) a capture-height manipulation, (ii) an image-metric-vs-policy-divergence test against a real-vs-real floor, and (iii) sidewalk SRCC with a test-retest ceiling. But the method it calls the "non-obvious insight" is already standard in driving. Feeding the same frozen planner real frames and splat renders at the same poses and measuring trajectory displacement is exactly DecoupleGS's "open-loop sim-to-real consistency test" (arXiv 2608.01761 [search snippet]). It runs UniAD on real images as the reference against renders from DecoupleGS, HUGSIM, RealEngine and OASim, and reports mADE of 0.82 m against 0.76 m for real input. The proposal does not cite it. That leaves capture height as the one truly new factor. Height is a real gap: Wanderland renders at about 1.1–1.2 m, Vid2Sim uses hand-held video, and GVNav (arXiv 2502.19024 [snippet]) shows a viewpoint-height generalisation gap for navigation policies, though it does not look at twins. But it is a single-factor extension of a known protocol, and it partly reduces to the known novel-view-synthesis result that extrapolating away from training views degrades renders (Mode-GS documents splat drift on ground-robot trajectories [snippet]). The real-vs-real floor is a good methodological addition, but it is not new in principle: it is test-retest reliability, which Kadian-style SRCC work already reasons about. Net: a new result in an active area, with the closest work only partly distinguished. That places it at the top of 6, not 7.

## Feasibility (6.5):
The primary analysis (open-loop D on teleop drives, plus image metrics) is well de-risked. The October phone-only floor check, the parity checks against Wanderland's fixed CityWalker port, the pre-registered site-level permutation, and a power simulation that I confirmed exists and matches the reported numbers are all strong for a high-school team. Remaining risks are substantial, though:
- **Robot build.** A new Jetson, camera, lidar and ROS 2 robot has to be built in 4 weeks.
- **Cross-height registration.** A 0.3 m pole capture has to register jointly with a 1.4 m capture in COLMAP outdoors. Grazing-angle pavement is a known failure case.
- **Splat training.** 32 splats are to be trained on free T4s.
- **Alignment accuracy.** A 5 cm RMSE Sim(3) has to be reached through slam_toolbox from a $99 2D lidar.
- **Field time.** 8 sites need 75-minute dry sessions in winter.

Closed-loop replay with logged latency is a second full pipeline. Its fallback to open-loop is sensible, but it means the main-track version depends on everything working. The real-vs-real floor also has a subtle problem. Two teleop passes matched within 10 cm and 3° include pose error, while renders sit at exact poses. The floor is therefore inflated, and twins will look better than they are.

## Impact (6.5):
A measured capture rule ("scan at deployment height, or not") plus evidence on whether LPIPS or DINO distance is enough would be cited by the Vid2Sim/S2E, Wanderland and EmbodiedSplat lines, and the paired real/twin sidewalk data at 30 cm is genuinely scarce. But the result covers one robot, one region, two policies and 16 short static routes. The most likely outcome (T_R renders closer to the evaluation poses and therefore better, with policies only modestly sensitive) is partly predictable from novel-view-synthesis theory. That caps it at a solid workshop or CASE paper rather than an IROS paper others build on. If the image-metric analysis (S1) shows LPIPS failing to track policy divergence, that is the more citable finding. It deserves equal billing with H1.

## Main criticism:
The core method (open-loop real-vs-render policy-output divergence) already exists in driving simulation (DecoupleGS's UniAD consistency test; NAVSIM-v2-style rendered-observation re-evaluation), and the proposal misses it. This leaves capture height as the only new factor. Height is also confounded with view coverage: the T_R pole walk "follows the robot path", so its renders are near-interpolations of the evaluation poses, while T_H renders are extrapolations. H1 therefore largely tests interpolation vs extrapolation, which theory already predicts for image quality. The open question is only whether the policies care.

## What would raise the score:
1. Cite DecoupleGS (2608.01761), HUGSIM and NAVSIM v2 pseudo-simulation (2506.04218) as prior art for open-loop real-vs-render planner consistency. Reframe the contribution as the height factor plus metric-validity at low viewpoints, not as the divergence method.
2. Break the height–coverage confound. Equalise frame count, path length and baseline between T_H and T_R. Report D as a function of the distance and angle from each evaluation pose to its nearest training view, so height can be separated from extrapolation distance. A 0.3 m capture on a path offset from the robot path (for example ±0.75 m) would make T_R a genuine novel-view condition.
3. Fix the floor asymmetry. Also compute a "render at the teleop-B pose" baseline, so twin D and real-vs-real D carry the same pose-matching error. Alternatively, report the floor as a function of the pose-match residual.
4. Give S1 (do LPIPS or DINO distance predict D?) co-primary status with a pre-registered threshold. It is the result most likely to be reused, and it can come out either way.
5. Read and confirm the Wanderland PDF (any real-robot runs?) and the unlocated 2610.00731 before claiming the low-viewpoint real-robot gap.
6. Add GVNav (2502.19024) as evidence that height itself shifts navigation-policy behaviour, and separate the policy's own height sensitivity (real 0.3 m vs real 1.4 m) from twin infidelity.

## Closest prior work found:
- DecoupleGS, arXiv 2608.01761 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01761): runs an open-loop sim-to-real consistency test with UniAD on real vs rendered observations, measuring mADE across GS simulators. It is the same "policy-output divergence" idea at car height. Not cited. [snippet only]
- Wanderland, arXiv 2511.20620 / CVPR 2026 (https://ai4ce.github.io/wanderland/): argues video-3DGS twins are unreliable for navigation evaluation and uses a multi-sensor handheld rig. Its abstract claims to show how image-only pipelines "adversely affect navigation policy learning and evaluation reliability". I could not confirm whether it has real-robot runs (arXiv blocked).
- NAVSIM v2 pseudo-simulation, arXiv 2506.04218 (https://research.nvidia.com/labs/avg/publication/cao.hallgarten.etal.corl2025): 3DGS-rendered observations used to evaluate planners. Correlation with closed loop is R² of about 0.8. It shows the "do renders preserve planner behaviour?" line in driving. [snippet]
- HUGSIM, arXiv 2412.01718 (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01718v1): a closed-loop 3DGS driving simulator, used as a baseline in DecoupleGS's consistency test.
- Kadian et al., Sim2Real Predictivity, arXiv 1912.06321: SRCC. Already adopted by the proposal.
- EmbodiedSplat, arXiv 2509.17430 (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17430v2): phone-captured splats, with sim-vs-real correlation of 0.87–0.97 for reconstructed meshes indoors. [snippet]
- PolaRiS, arXiv 2512.16881, and Real-to-Sim GS soft-body evaluation, arXiv 2511.04665: real-to-sim evaluation correlation for manipulation (MMRV, Pearson).
- GVNav, arXiv 2502.19024 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19024): the first VLN work to highlight a generalisation gap across observation heights (quadruped vs human). It covers height sensitivity of policies, not twins. Not cited.
- Mode-GS, arXiv 2410.04646 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04646): documents splat drift and insufficient multi-view coverage in ground-robot trajectories, which supports the predictability concern for H1.
- VR-Robo, arXiv 2502.01536, and NavGSim, arXiv 2603.15186: GS real-to-sim-to-real navigation with real deployment. They measure training transfer, not evaluation predictivity.
- Sudhakar et al., "Exploring the Sim2Real Gap using Digital Twins", ICCV 2023: a factor study of twin artefacts vs downstream detection accuracy. It is an analogous design, for perception.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- "Counterfactual open-loop divergence" is presented as a non-obvious insight. It is an established protocol (DecoupleGS's UniAD open-loop sim-to-real consistency test, with real images as reference) and is uncited. This is an omission and overclaim, not a fabricated link.
- arXiv 2610.00731 ("Measuring Asset and Scene Reconstruction Effects in Real-to-Sim Robot Evaluation"): my exact-title and ID searches also failed to locate it. It should not be cited until verified. The proposal itself already flags it as unread.
- "Only deterministic policies are used, so twin reliability is 1": this holds for the render-and-policy step only. It ignores the fact that the real-vs-real floor includes pose-matching error that the twin comparison lacks, which biases the floor upward.
- Otherwise none found. The Cam2Sim description (GS plus CARLA, Nerfstudio splatfacto) and the Mode-GS ground-robot splat drift claim are consistent with my search snippets. I did not verify the Kadian SRCC values of 0.18 and 0.844, or the EmbodiedSplat 1.31 m height.
