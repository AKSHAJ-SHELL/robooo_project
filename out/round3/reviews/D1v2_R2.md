# D1.v2 — Reviewer 2 (Methodology rigor and feasibility for this team)
Scores: N 6 / F 5.5 / I 6 → overall 5.83

## Novelty (6): 
The UTK prior work is real and now correctly placed. A search confirms trace.tennessee.edu/utk_gradthes/7107 is Sarten (1990), "Tractive performance of lawn and garden tractor tires during vehicle operation on turf", with reverse up-slope spinout tests. utk_gradthes/6831 is Arthur (1996, advisor Wilkerson): a Ford L&G tractor on tall fescue, towing a constant drawbar load, with "free-surface moisture" true/false as a factor (search snippets only). Given that, the new parts are (a) wheel loads about 20x lower, (b) leaf water and soil water as separate factors, and (c) a natural-state panel. Each is a real but incremental step, a known method in a new regime, as the authors admit. My searches found no small-wheel or robot-mower study of wet turf. Maker and patent material talks about "wet grass" only qualitatively, so the gap is genuine. The leaf/soil split is the most interesting idea. But the design's power for the soil factor is weak (see Feasibility), so in practice the paper is likely to show only "mist vs dry at 60 N on 3 lawns". That is a narrower novelty than the title promises.

## Feasibility (5.5): 
**Instrument validity.** The carriage idea is sound: speed-controlled tow, a torque-ramped test wheel, a fifth wheel and a hitch load cell form a standard single-wheel-tester layout. The EFeru FOC firmware does support GD32F103 boards, but whether the specific Gotrax board is compatible has to be checked by opening it before the parts order. There are three validity gaps.
- **Slip zero is not specified, and the headline is a difference in slip.** Leaf water changes canopy compression, so it changes both the test wheel's effective rolling radius and the fifth wheel's radius on turf. A 1% radius shift moves s by 0.01, which is the same order as plausible Δslip@0.3 values. The fifth-wheel calibration on a taped line (surface unspecified) does not fix this. Both radii must be re-zeroed per strip and per wetness state, using a zero-net-traction pass under the ASABE-style zero condition.
- **Hall resolution is coarse at low slip.** Typical hoverboard hubs give about 90 Hall edges per revolution (unverified for Gotrax). At 0.3 m/s that is only about 50 edges/s. The low-slip region, which sets dμ/ds and slip@0.3, therefore gets few counts in a 4 s ramp. A higher-resolution encoder on the test wheel, or a slower ramp, is needed.
- **The slope tow-stall check is physically marginal.** The braked 60 N test wheel can resist at most about μ·60 ≈ 36–48 N, plus the carriage's grade load. A ~12 kg fully driven robot has F_tow,max ≈ 65 N on flat ground (by the proposal's own formula with μ = 0.6). So the robot will not stall on the flat or 10% strips unless the carriage is re-ballasted, which changes the wheel load.

**Confounds.** These hit both headline quantities.
- Part A always runs dry before mist. Leaf state is therefore confounded with time-in-session and with track position. A split-strip design (mist alternate tracks, interleave ramps) would remove this.
- In Part B, σ²_occasion absorbs day-to-day instrument drift (load-cell zero, tyre temperature, ballast placement, tow-speed error) and mowing between visits. All of that would be reported as "time-varying wetness". A daily reference-surface run is needed to subtract it.
- The "natural share" depends on the team's own choice of mix of dawn, late-morning and afternoon slots. Fix #3 therefore only moves the design-dependence from manipulation levels to the sampling schedule.
- In winter, the warm-season lawns chosen for species contrast (kikuyu, Bermuda) are dormant. "Species" will really be "growing vs dormant".

**Statistics.** I re-ran the Part B simulation myself: 8 × 10 × 6 ramps, ANOVA components, percentile lawn-cluster bootstrap. It reproduces the proposal's numbers: power 0.93 at share 0.5, median CI width 0.50.
- It also shows a 20% false-positive rate at the true share of 0.2. A pre-registered test with a nominal 2.5% one-sided error that rejects 20% of the time is miscalibrated. The cause is the bootstrap with 8 clusters, which is known to under-cover. Use a parametric bootstrap or profile-likelihood interval instead.
- The low end of the stated occasion SD (0.03, with between-lawn SD 0.06) is exactly share = 0.2. A null result is therefore quite plausible.
- The Part A power (SE ≈ 0.010, power ≈ 0.85 for 0.03) checks out arithmetically. But it treats 24 contrasts as independent when they sit in 12 sessions on 3 lawns. It is also computed for Δμ_peak, while the pre-registered sign test is on Δslip@0.3, for which no SD is assumed.
- The soil contrast is between-session: 6 sessions vs 6 sessions, in the rainy season, when soil is often already at field capacity. No power is given for it, and it will probably be underpowered.
- LOLO over lawns does not hold out time. All lawns share one CIMIS station, and 3–4 lawns are visited per session. M2 therefore learns each day's weather effect from the other lawns on the same day, which leaks information and inflates the M2 gain. CV should be blocked by date or session.

**Schedule.** The plan needs about 25 panel sessions, 12 factorial sessions, 3 dew mornings, about 72 slope stalls and a pilot. The build, calibration, pilot, power re-run and pre-registration all fall in 3 weeks (Nov 16–Dec 6). Most of the field work lands in winter break and rainy weeks. A real occasion (set up the tow robot, 6 ramps, blot, 3 VWC readings, photo) is closer to 25–30 min than 15 min, so "3–4 lawns in a 1 h session" is optimistic. The plan can be done with heavy cuts, and the Jan 15 go/no-go is sensible, but this is significant risk for two part-time students. The budget (about $620–760) is fine.

## Impact (6): 
A clean, well-supported sign result would be a real design rule that robot-mower makers and test labs could use. One example is "dew derate yes or no", together with tread dependence. The Consumer NZ "heavy dew or light rain" conflation is a good, specific hook. However:
- The sign is established on 3 lawns in winter at one wheel load and one tyre size.
- The variance share will come with a CI about 0.5 wide.
- The weather-model result is confirmatory only in the favourable case.

So the most likely paper is a solid CASE submission or a strong workshop paper with a small dataset (about 600 ramps across 8 lawns, all in one region and one season). The dataset is too small for the traversability community to evaluate image models seriously. Its reuse value rests mainly on the protocol and the carriage design.

## Lab appeal: 
Kitts (SCU RSL) is a plausible fit. A cheap single-wheel tester that undergraduates can build for agricultural or vegetated-ground rovers costs the lab little and fits its education focus. Elkaim (UCSC ASL) is more of a bridge than a current research input, as the authors honestly say, and Carpin is asked only for evaluation-design feedback. Moderate appeal, and no lab has an active traction or terramechanics line that this directly feeds.

## Main criticism: 
The headline quantities are differences in slip and in variance components. Uncontrolled nuisance terms can shift both by the same amount as the expected effect:
- per-state rolling-radius changes (slip zero);
- the fixed dry-then-mist order;
- day-to-day instrument drift counted as "natural wetness variance".

On top of that, the pre-registered share test uses a cluster bootstrap that, in the authors' own simulation and mine, has about 20% type I error at the boundary, and the weather model is cross-validated with temporal leakage.

## What would raise the score:
1. Define slip against a measured zero-condition per strip and per wetness state, re-deriving both test-wheel and fifth-wheel effective radii. Add a ≥1000 CPR encoder to the test wheel and report the slip-measurement SD from the pilot.
2. Use a split-strip, interleaved design in Part A: mist alternate tracks and randomize the ramp order so leaf state is not confounded with time.
3. Run a daily reference-surface check (e.g. a fixed rubber mat or plywood strip ramp) at every panel session. Subtract instrument day-variance from σ²_occasion, and log mowing dates as a covariate.
4. Replace the percentile cluster bootstrap with a parametric bootstrap or profile-likelihood CI for the share, and show calibrated type I error (≤5%) at share = 0.2 before pre-registering.
5. Cross-validate M2 blocked by date or session (leave-one-day-out, or lawn × day blocks), not just LOLO.
6. Fix the sampling frame for the "natural share": weight occasions to an explicit operating window, such as typical robot-mower run hours, and state that the share is conditional on it.
7. Re-ballast or re-gear the slope check so the carriage braking capacity exceeds the robot's flat-ground F_tow,max, or drop the flat and 10% grades.
8. Cut scope. Drop either the slope check or the commercial-mower check, budget 30 min per occasion, and pre-commit to a 6-lawn panel.
9. Give a power calculation for the soil contrast and for Δslip@0.3 specifically. Otherwise, demote the soil factor to exploratory.

## Closest prior work found:
- [Sarten 1990, UTK MS thesis 7107](https://trace.tennessee.edu/utk_gradthes/7107): L&G tractor tyres on turf with slope spinout tests. This is the direct origin of the "sign" being tested (snippet).
- [Arthur 1996, UTK MS thesis 6831](https://trace.tennessee.edu/utk_gradthes/6831): tall fescue, constant drawbar load, free-surface moisture as a true/false factor, axle load, sipe density. This is the closest design, at tractor loads (snippet).
- [SAE 911240](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/911240/): the published version of the UTK work.
- [Hohenheim single wheel tester](https://reifenmodell.uni-hohenheim.de/en/single-wheel-tester) and field single-wheel testers generally ([ARREO example](https://fooder.areeo.ac.ir/article_100514.html)): the established instrument class. The carriage is a scaled version.
- [Comparison of tractor slip at three different driving wheels on grass (EMU)](https://dspace.emu.ee/items/f5f78856-22a1-45cd-8adf-8a5674103f4d/full): wheel slip on grass at tractor scale (title only).
- Robot-mower patents and maker blogs (e.g. [USPTO 9043952](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9043952), [Sunseeker](https://sunseekerelite.com/us/blog/fwd-vs-rwd-vs-awd-robot-mowers)): describe wet-grass slip only qualitatively, so the measurement gap is real.

## Wrong links/claims in the proposal:
- The Part B power claim is reproduced, but the stated "about 0.15–0.2 false-positive rate at the boundary" is described as the test being "correctly not significant". At a nominal 2.5% one-sided level, that rate is an inflated type I error, not correct behaviour.
- "Slip at F/N = 0.3 is comparable to UTK's constant-drawbar-load slip metric" is overstated. UTK's metric is whole-vehicle slip at a fixed drawbar pull (0–375 lb), not single-wheel slip at a fixed net traction ratio.
- The slope check's implicit claim that the braked carriage can stall the robot on all three grades fails on flat and ~10% grades with a 60 N test wheel, by the proposal's own formula.
- Thesis 6831's advisor is listed in the snippet as John Wilkerson. The proposal attributes Tompkins only to the 1990 thesis, which I could not confirm or refute.
- Otherwise none found. Links 7107, 6831 and SAE 911240 resolve to the described works (snippets).
