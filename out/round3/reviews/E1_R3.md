# E1 — Reviewer 3 (Impact, venue fit and lab appeal)
Scores: N 7 / F 5.5 / I 7 → overall 6.50

## Novelty (7): 
The question is timely and real. Since mid-2026 several splat-based closed-loop evaluators have appeared: NavArena (arXiv 2609.04602, >2,000 frozen 3DGS scenes, Gaussian-density costmap), Neverwhere (arXiv 2609.16443, >60 urban splat scenes for visual locomotion) and SidewalkBench (arXiv 2606.16953, 9 models in Isaac Sim). I confirmed all three exist from search snippets; arXiv itself was blocked, so I could not read them in full. I found no outdoor, low-viewpoint, episode-level sim-vs-real validation, and no work that manipulates capture height. The parts that are new are that height manipulation and the "fidelity in policy-output space" metric. But the framing "does sim evaluation predict real performance" is not new. Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity" (RA-L 2020, arXiv 1912.06321), defined the Sim-vs-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) for PointNav and showed Habitat's SRCC was 0.18, rising to 0.844 after tuning. That is the canonical ancestor of this design, and the proposal never cites it. NavDP (arXiv 2505.08712) also claims, per the snippet, that its splat real-to-sim evaluation is "consistent with real-world evaluation" for wheeled and legged robots. That weakens the claim to be "first for navigation" and leaves "first outdoor, low-height, with foundation models" as the defensible version. This is a new result in an active area, with clear prior-work boundaries once Kadian is added.

## Feasibility (5.5): 
The project has to build four things in about 12 part-time weeks: a robot from scratch, upstream inference for 4 policies on a Jetson with parity checks, a 24-splat outdoor capture pipeline on free Colab (whether it fits is admitted to be unconfirmed), and a closed-loop twin simulator (gsplat render → policy → unicycle model with logged latency → density-occupancy collision, checked against lidar). Each piece is reasonable, but together they are a lot for two students. There is also a large floor-effect risk. CityWalker learned from human-height walking video, and LogoNav/MBRA and S2E from other embodiments. Running zero-shot from a 30 cm camera on a hoverboard base may give near-zero route completion for most policies. That would make the 96-episode correlations meaningless, and the proposal names this as its main risk. The fallback, counterfactual open-loop divergence, is cheap and well chosen and still publishable as a workshop paper. That is why feasibility is not lower. The power analysis is sensible, but it assumes r ≈ 0.7 vs 0.5 effect sizes with no pilot evidence behind them.

## Impact (7): 
If the full study lands, IROS reviewers would care: splat twins are quickly becoming the default way to rank navigation and locomotion policies, and a paired real/twin episode set plus a validated "check your twin in output space" procedure is the kind of thing benchmark authors cite and reuse. Result B (capture height matters) would give a concrete design rule for NavBench-GS/Vid2Sim/NavArena-style pipelines. Several things cap the impact below 8. There is one robot, one region, 8 routes, 4 policies, and static obstacles only. The policy-level comparison against NavBench-GS can only be descriptive, because τ over 4 policies means little, and NavBench-GS's own benchmark settings are unreleased: I verified that the repository's `envs/metrics/scenarios` folders hold only empty `__init__.py` files and the README marks "Benchmark detail settings" as not done. So "validating NavBench-GS" is really "validating Vid2Sim-style capture." A sceptical reviewer will also ask whether results with these 4 policies generalise to the next ones. The fallback-only outcome is workshop-level (about 5.5). Expected impact is about a solid CASE/IROS submission, and the upside is real.

## Lab appeal: 
Fremont is a defensible but loose fit. His ITSC 2020 sim-to-track paper and Scenic work make the failure-transfer stretch study his methodology, but the stretch experiment is exactly the part most likely to be cut. His current SIP project is RL robustness, and he does no splat or visual-navigation work, so his gain is a dataset he would have to adopt. Elkaim's ASL runs a classical Pi + Nav2 stack with no learned visual policies. "A phone-scan twin pipeline validated for its own robots" is an overstatement, since the twin would be validated for these 4 foundation models, not for Nav2 with lidar. The labs that would truly want this, UCLA VAIL / Bolei Zhou's group (Vid2Sim, UrbanVerse, S2E), are not on the reachable list. So the realistic outcome is modest advice from Fremont, not co-authorship.

## Main criticism: 
The headline result depends on 96 closed-loop episodes of zero-shot foundation models on a home-built 30 cm robot. A floor effect, which the proposal itself names as its main risk, is likely enough that the expected paper is the open-loop divergence fallback (workshop level). And the novelty framing misses the canonical SRCC paper it directly extends.

## What would raise the score: 
1. Cite and position against Kadian et al. 2020 (SRCC) and NavDP's real-to-sim evaluation claim. Report SRCC in their exact form so the result plugs into an established metric.
2. Before Oct 25, check the floor-effect risk at zero cost. Run each policy open-loop on a 30 cm-height phone video of a sidewalk and check that the waypoints are sane. Drop or replace policies that are clearly broken at that height, before committing to the robot build.
3. Make the counterfactual output-space divergence vs PSNR/LPIPS result the primary, guaranteed contribution, and make the closed-loop correlation the confirmatory layer. Pre-register this order.
4. Cut scope to 3 policies, and make the T_H vs T_R contrast the single main hypothesis (drop T_HR or make it secondary).
5. Contact VAIL-UCLA (the S2E/Vid2Sim authors) with the pilot data. Their endorsement or co-use would add more impact than any reachable lab. For Fremont, pitch the falsification angle concretely, e.g. a Scenic-generated obstacle placement in the twin.
6. Add one dynamic but controllable element (e.g. a rolled cart or moved planter between capture and runs) so the dataset tests what benchmarks actually assume about scene staleness.

## Closest prior work found: 
- Kadian et al., Sim2Real Predictivity, RA-L 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06321 — defines SRCC for sim-vs-real navigation evaluation (indoor, Habitat mesh, LoCoBot); same core question, not splats, not outdoors, no height gap. Not cited in the proposal.
- EmbodiedSplat, ICCV 2025 — https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17430v2 — splat sim-vs-real correlation of 0.87–0.97, indoors, capture height matched to the robot.
- NavDP — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08712 — splat real-to-sim fine-tuning plus an evaluation platform claimed consistent with real-world evaluation (snippet only).
- Real-to-Sim Robot Policy Evaluation with GS soft-body simulation — https://real2sim-eval.github.io/ — r > 0.9 sim-real, manipulation.
- PolaRiS — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16881 — real-to-sim evaluation for generalist manipulation policies.
- NavArena — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04602 — 2,000+ splat benchmarks, the costmap the proposal reuses; the snippet showed no real-world validation.
- Neverwhere — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16443 — 60+ urban splat scenes for visual locomotion evaluation.
- SidewalkBench — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16953 — Isaac Sim sidewalk benchmark, 9 models.
- Vid2Sim (CVPR 2025) — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06693 — monocular-video urban twins; the source of the human-height capture practice.
- R2S-EGO — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06827 — sparse human capture under-supports robot views; a view-synthesis fix, not an evaluation-validity test.
- "Measuring Asset and Scene Reconstruction Effects in Real-to-Sim Robot Evaluation" — https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00731 — surfaced in search but its content could not be read (arXiv blocked). Its title suggests a factor study of reconstruction effects on real-to-sim evaluation, so the authors should check it before claiming that "reconstruction choices → evaluation validity" is untested.
