# F1 — Reviewer 1 (Academic depth and novelty)
Scores: N 6 / F 5.5 / I 6 → overall 5.83

## Novelty (6):
I found no paper that reports session-level (day/time) variance components or an ICC for outdoor ground-robot navigation metrics, so the central number does look unmeasured. But the area around it is crowded and moving fast. Policy-evaluation statistics is now a main-track topic: STEP ([Snyder et al. 2025, arXiv 2503.10966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10966)), N-SCORE, which does sequential anytime-valid comparison on *continuous* metrics ([RSS 2026, arXiv 2603.13616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13616)), RoboArena's distributed multi-site pairwise ranking ([CoRL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18123)), PhAIL and Kress-Gazit et al. The statistics here are textbook: mixed models, design effect 1+(m−1)ρ, pseudoreplication. Once the ICC is known, the result that i.i.d. sequential tests lose their nominal error rate under clustering follows on paper, so that sub-contribution is predictable (brief penalty #1). The "non-obvious insight" that nobody reports build-to-build robot variance is false: Duckietown's Autolab paper ([Tani et al., IROS 2020, arXiv 2009.04362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04362)) ran the same experiments on several nominally identical robots and reported inter-robot spread against single-robot repeatability. That work is indoor, but the claim as written does not hold. The new part is a calibration number for one class of robot and site. That counts as "known method, new setting, with a useful dataset", plus a literature audit.

## Feasibility (5.5):
The plan is about 32 outdoor sessions of roughly 75 minutes each, plus transport, setup and map checks. It needs two hand-built hoverboard robots, three navigation stacks (one is NoMaD/ViNT on a laptop carried on board), and a new AprilTag-mat ground-truth reader validated to 5 mm. All of this falls in a California winter, run by two part-time students who must have both units driving by Nov 15. That is a lot of hardware and logistics before any variance can be estimated. The design also has a structural problem. Site and robot unit each have only **2 levels**, so their "variance components" cannot be identified in any useful sense. With two levels you get a fixed contrast, not a variance with a meaningful CI. Only session (k≈28) is powered. The ±0.14 CI on the ICC also assumes the Fisher large-sample formula holds at k=28 and that S3 does not degenerate. The fallbacks help: drop S3, 20 sessions is still workable, continuous metrics. So the core ICC estimate is reachable even though the full factorial is not.

## Impact (6):
If the result is crisp, it would be cited in the evaluation-methods debate, and a released multi-session field dataset plus protocol has reuse value. One framing error weakens the headline, though. The proposal interleaves stacks in random order within each session. A session *main* effect then shifts all stacks equally and cancels out of a paired comparison. The "20 trials are worth 4" design-effect argument applies to estimating absolute performance, not to the stack comparison. Rank flips are driven by the **session × stack interaction**, which is smaller and harder to estimate. Result B as stated overstates the threat to current comparative practice. External validity is narrow: one hobby platform, Nav2 tuned once, Bay Area parks in winter. A Husky-in-a-vineyard or forest lab can reasonably say the ICC does not transfer. This is a solid CASE paper or strong workshop paper. IROS main track is possible only if the interaction result is striking.

## Lab appeal:
Carpin's lab runs Nav2 outdoors and could plausibly offer cheap advice on mixed models, but the connection is "we also evaluate outdoors", not a shared research line. Elkaim's SIP Pi/Nav2 project (verified in out/labs.md) would get a reusable protocol and a mat tool, which is low cost and a modest gain. Labs that would actually care, such as Pomerleau/Norlab for field experimental quality or the TRI/Snyder evaluation-statistics group, are not reachable.

## Main criticism:
The headline confuses the session main effect with the session × stack interaction. Under the proposal's own interleaved design, only the interaction threatens comparisons. That makes the "pseudo-replication" story partly derivable on paper and partly overstated. Meanwhile the "unit" and "site" components cannot be estimated from 2 levels each, so the claimed first decomposition by session, site and unit reduces to one session ICC on one platform.

## What would raise the score:
1. Reframe the primary estimand as the session × stack interaction variance, plus the rank-flip probability for close pairs. Keep the session main-effect ICC secondary, for absolute-performance claims only.
2. Drop the claim of a unit or site variance. Report the two units and two sites as fixed contrasts, or make one of them a real random factor: for example, 6+ cheap sites with fewer sessions each, which also supports the held-out-site prediction.
3. Cite and position against N-SCORE (continuous-metric sequential tests, RSS 2026), STEP (correct authors), RoboArena and Duckietown Autolab. Then test N-SCORE, not only STEP and PhAIL, under clustering.
4. Cut to two Nav2 stacks plus one deliberately lighting-sensitive variant on a single robot design to make the timeline realistic. Spend the saved effort on more sessions.
5. Pre-register the analysis. Get one outside lab, such as Merced's Husky, to run a few sessions of the protocol as a transfer check on the ICC.

## Closest prior work found:
- [Kress-Gazit et al. 2024, Robot Learning as an Empirical Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09491): best-practice evaluation (conditions, metrics, statistics) for manipulation. No session variance decomposition.
- [Snyder et al. 2025, STEP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10966): sequential comparison of binary-success policies with near-optimal stopping. Assumes i.i.d. trials.
- [Snyder et al., N-SCORE, RSS 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13616): anytime-valid sequential comparison for continuous and partial-credit metrics. The closest statistical tool to what F1 tests, and missing from the proposal.
- [RoboArena, CoRL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18123): distributed pairwise evaluation across 7 institutions. Addresses location and evaluator heterogeneity in ranking.
- [PhAIL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29710): distributional time-to-success CDFs with bootstrap CIs; notes typical ~20-trial evaluations without CIs. Single fixed cell.
- [Tani et al. IROS 2020, Duckietown Autolab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04362): measures inter-robot and inter-lab reproducibility on nominally identical low-cost robots. Directly overlaps the "robot unit" claim.
- [MIRRER 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04736): conceptual framework for iterated, reproduced and replicated robot experiments.
- [Weng et al. 2025, Repeatable measures with statistical query](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08216): algorithm-level repeatability of standardized tests. No field sessions.
- [Baril et al. 2022, Kilometer-scale navigation in subarctic forests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13981) and [Boxan et al. 2026, One year in a forest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27628): long-term field campaigns that report condition effects descriptively, not as variance components.
- [Stochasticity in Agentic Evaluations: ICC (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06710): the same ICC-for-evaluation idea applied to LLM agents. Shows the framing is spreading but has not yet reached field robotics.
- NIST/ASTM E54 response-robot test methods ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/standard-test-methods-response-robots/ground-robot-tests)): 10/20/30-repetition reliability testing on standardized apparatus. Relevant, established practice on trial counts.
