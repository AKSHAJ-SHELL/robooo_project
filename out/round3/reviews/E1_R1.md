## Novelty (6.5)
The question is a good one: does a phone-scan splat predict how a navigation foundation model behaves at 30 cm, and does capture height matter? But the proposal misses the closest prior work. **Wanderland** (Liu, ..., Chen Feng, NYU AI4CE / Cornell, CVPR 2026 Highlight, arXiv 2511.20620) [S][G] makes almost this exact case for urban navigation. Its abstract says "video-3DGS methods ... are still unsuitable for benchmarking due to large visual and geometric sim-to-real gaps". It also says the paper shows "how image-only pipelines scale poorly, how geometry quality impacts novel view synthesis, and how all of these adversely affect navigation policy learning and evaluation reliability." It comes from the CityWalker group, and its released `wanderland-lab` [G] runs closed-loop CityWalker benchmarks in Isaac Sim. Its pose generator defaults to `cam_height` 1.2 m (`navigation/generate_cam_poses.py`) [G]. The proposal's "Result C" warning is therefore already partly made, by a top group, at CVPR. The method is also old. Kadian et al., "Sim2Real Predictivity" (RA-L 2020, arXiv 1912.06321) [S], defined the Sim-vs-Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) for PointGoal navigation. It found SRCC 0.18 for the Habitat challenge setup and 0.844 after tuning. The proposal never cites it, even though its main statistic is essentially SRCC. Three things still look new: (a) a real robot-vs-twin comparison outdoors, episode by episode, with a low camera; (b) a controlled change of capture height; (c) testing whether output-space divergence predicts closed-loop disagreement better than PSNR/LPIPS. I could not confirm whether Wanderland ran real robots, so (a) needs checking. Point (c) is a sensible idea but not a new principle. Judging a neural simulator by how a downstream model reacts, rather than by pixels, is common practice in driving sensor simulation; my search budget ran out before I could pin a citation, so I list none. Overall this is "a new result in an active area" only if the paper sets itself clearly against Wanderland and SRCC. Otherwise reviewers will read it as Wanderland/EmbodiedSplat with a robot.

## Feasibility (5.5)
The scope is heavy for two part-time high-school students:
- four upstream policies, with parity checks against the known-broken zoo wrappers (I confirmed the VisNavKit note that the CityWalker wrapper "ignores `goal_xy`" [G]);
- a Jetson closed-loop rig;
- 24 outdoor splats on free Colab;
- a custom twin-rollout simulator with Gaussian-density collision;
- 96 real episodes, plus a stretch study.

Outdoor splats of 25–40 m sidewalks from a phone are fragile: sun and shadows change within the hour, cars and leaves move, and pavement has little texture. A pole capture at 0.3 m will make COLMAP even harder. The bigger hidden risk is method. The proposal never measures real-vs-real repeatability, meaning the same policy, route and start run twice. Stochastic policies (NoMaD is a diffusion model) and noisy real starts cap the twin-vs-real correlation, so a gap between r = 0.5 and r = 0.7 can't be interpreted without that ceiling. Correlation pooled over episodes can also be driven by differences between policies and routes, which makes it look good even when the twin is wrong within a policy. These two fixes cost robot time the plan doesn't have. The counterfactual-divergence fallback is a real safety net and keeps this above 5.

## Impact (6.5)
If it works, splat-benchmark authors (NavBench-GS, Wanderland, Neverwhere, Vid2Sim) would cite it, and the paired real/twin episode set is reusable. But Wanderland has already moved the community toward lidar-grounded, multi-sensor capture. So "phone video twins are unreliable" is less surprising news, and "phone twins are fine at 30 cm" runs against a CVPR highlight unless the evidence is strong. With 4 policies, 8 routes and 3–4 sites, a main-track reviewer will see a small study. The most citable piece would be the capture-height rule plus the output-space fidelity check, if both come out clearly.

## Lab appeal
Fremont's ITSC 2020 sim-to-track scenario testing is a real methodological match, and the falsification stretch study is his kind of work. It is still a sideways fit to formal methods, so expect light advising rather than co-authorship. Elkaim's ASL gets a twin pipeline for its own robots, which is modest. The group that would care most is NYU AI4CE (Wanderland/CityWalker), not UCLA VAIL, and it isn't reachable.

## Main criticism
The proposal misses Wanderland (CVPR 2026), which already argues, for urban navigation with CityWalker, that video-3DGS twins give unreliable evaluation. It also misses Kadian et al.'s SRCC, the standard way to test whether simulator results predict real ones in navigation. Both of the paper's headline claims ("first outdoor test of twin validity" and the fidelity-metric framing) need to be rebuilt around these papers. The design also lacks a real-vs-real repeatability ceiling, without which the correlations can't be interpreted.

## What would raise the score
1. Read Wanderland in full (paper and `ai4ce/wanderland-lab`). State exactly what it did and did not measure: real robots? which camera heights? correlation with real outcomes? Then pitch E1 as the low-viewpoint, real-robot validation that Wanderland's lidar rig does not cover. Better, add a Wanderland-style geometry-grounded twin (lidar point cloud + splat) as a fourth capture condition. That turns "phone vs human height" into "phone vs lidar-grounded vs height".
2. Use SRCC (Kadian 2020) as the named policy-level metric and report it next to the episode-level r.
3. Add real-vs-real test-retest runs: for example, repeat every route × policy pair once (or a 24-episode subset). That sets the reliability ceiling and lets you correct correlations for attenuation.
4. Report within-policy and within-route correlations (or the mixed-model gap) as the primary result, so differences between policies can't drive a good-looking pooled r.
5. Cut scope: 3 policies (drop NoMaD collision-only) and 2 capture conditions (T_H vs T_R). Make the counterfactual divergence analysis the primary contribution and closed-loop correlation the validation.
6. Before claiming novelty for policy-output fidelity, search driving neural-simulator papers that judge realism by downstream perception agreement.
